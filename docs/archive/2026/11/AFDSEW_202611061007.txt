National Weather Service Seattle WA

.SYNOPSIS...A strong frontal system will bring gusty winds and 
additional rainfall to western Washington today. A warm front 
will boost temperatures as it moves northwards into the area 
Saturday. A follow-up cold front Saturday night into Sunday will
bring another round of rain and gusty winds. The weather 
pattern remains active next week as additional storms impact the
area. 

&&

.SHORT TERM /TODAY THROUGH SUNDAY/...The first in a series of
strong Pacific frontal systems will impact the region today 
with strong winds along the coast and North Interior. Expect 
south winds with gusts to 50 MPH - Wind Advisories are in 
effect. Winds will ease late this afternoon. Rain-wise, most of 
the precipitation will be along the coast, Olympic peninsula, 
and north part this morning, with rain filling in across the 
Seattle metro this afternoon and evening. Showers will taper off
after midnight as the cold front exits. 

A warm front will move into B.C. on Saturday and we will start
the day dry and mild. Highs will track warmer than average with
temperatures in the 60s. A deep low will head toward Haida
Gwaii Saturday night while sending a cold front into western WA.
This will bring another round of windy and rainy weather with
the potential for strong winds along the coast. It'll be windy
in the interior too with gusts to 30-40 MPH. 

There's a brief break between systems on Sunday but the focus 
will turn toward the coast again as a large swell train reaches 
Washington. Seas of 20-25 ft will reach the coastal waters with 
potential for high surf condition along the beaches. 33

&&

.LONG TERM /SUNDAY NIGHT THROUGH THURSDAY/...The weather pattern
remains active next week as additional Pacific storms track NE 
and impact the Pac NW. Each storm brings the potential for windy
conditions (especially the coast), with more lowland rain and 
high elevation snow. There's little break in the action through
the period. 33

&&

.AVIATION...West to southwest flow aloft prevails into tonight 
as a warm front stalled offshore shifts into Western Washington 
later today and weakens. As precipitation associated with this 
frontal system spreads onshore, a deterioration to MVFR ceilings
is expected along the coast and across the North Interior 
toward mid/late morning with said conditions spreading to the 
remainder of the area this afternoon. Gusty south to 
southeasterly surface winds of 20 to 30 knots with locally 
higher gusts can be expected for coastal areas and the interior 
from KPAE northward into this afternoon before easing. 

KSEA...Ceilings are expected to remain at or above 4000 feet 
through 18Z with periods of light rain developing this morning. 
Ceilings are then expected to fall to MVFR near or after 21Z as the 
bulk of the rain with the front shifts into the area. These 
conditions are expected to persist well into the evening before 
precipitation decreases overnight. Surface winds S/SE 7 to 10 knots 
will rise to 10 to 15 knots at times this afternoon with occasional 
higher gusts before easing tonight.

.MARINE...An active weather patter across area waters will 
continue into next week as a series of frontal systems cross the
region. Gales remain in place today for the coastal waters, 
strait entrances, and northern inland waters as a stalled warm 
front eventually shifts onshore and weakens this evening. All 
attention then turns toward a surface low that will deepen to 
near 970 millibars as it lifts northeastward offshore on 
Saturday night pushing a vigorous frontal system into the area. 
This system looks sufficiently strong to produce gales across 
all waters between midday Saturday and midday Sunday. And there 
is enough potential for some storm force gusts to hoist a storm 
watch for the coastal waters during said period. In addition to 
the wind threat, coastal seas will build substantially and could
exceed 25 feet across portions of the coastal waters by early 
Sunday morning. Brief high pressure will build over the waters 
Sunday night into early Monday before additional systems arrive 
with the potential for more headlines. 

Rough to severe bar conditions will develop at Grays Harbor today 
and continue until further notice.

.HYDROLOGY...River flooding is not expected through the next 
seven days. However, a series of increasingly wet weather 
systems will bring rain (and high mountain snow) to the region, 
especially from Friday onward. While current forecasts are not 
expected to cause flooding, this will begin to saturate soils 
and raise base flows that could bring a concern with subsequent 
storms systems into next week.
