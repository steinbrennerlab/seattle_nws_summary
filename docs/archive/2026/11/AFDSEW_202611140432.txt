.SYNOPSIS...
A series of frontal systems will bring a return of widespread 
rain and occasionally windy conditions through the weekend. A 
trend toward drier and cooler conditions are expected toward the
middle portion of next week as the storm track shifts south of 
Western Washington. 

&&

.SHORT TERM /TONIGHT THROUGH SUNDAY NIGHT/...
The latest forecast remains on track this evening with no
updates. A trough will push inland tonight and Saturday, ahead 
of the next strong Pacific frontal system. Pressure is a little 
higher Saturday morning and coastal flooding is confined to the 
San Juan Islands and western Whatcom County during the morning 
high tide. The main impacts with this second system will be 
strong winds (coast and San Juans), periods of heavy rain (coast
and Olympics), and large waves (coast). Winds will peak 
Saturday night into early Sunday morning. For precipitation 
totals through the weekend, we are looking at another 1-3" along
the coast with 0.50-1.00" in the interior. Most of the rain 
will fall Saturday night. Due to additional heavy rain in the 
Olympics, the Skokomish River is at risk of flooding and a Flood
Watch remains in effect. In addition, this system is generating
large waves offshore that will reach the WA coast this weekend 
leading to High Surf conditions. 33

&&

.LONG TERM /MONDAY THROUGH FRIDAY/...
We're under broad, longwave troughing Sunday and Monday for 
isolated to scattered showers. Temperatures will track closer to
average with lows in the 40s and highs in the lower 50s. An 
upper low will shift E/SE and into Oregon midweek, with some 
light moisture clipping the WA/OR border. A Rex Block pattern 
forms toward the end of week over the West. This blocking 
pattern keeps western WA dry through the end of the week. 33

&&

.AVIATION...
South flow aloft continues into tonight before transitioning 
more southwest on Saturday as the next frontal system approaches
the Pacific Northwest. Showers continuing along the coast this 
evening. Predominantly VFR conditions continue into Saturday 
with periodic MVFR cigs mainly along the Olympic Peninsula and 
OLM westwards. Post-frontal isolated showers expected tonight 
into Saturday. The next front arrives Saturday evening along the
coast and moves inland Saturday night with increasing rain and 
winds. Surface winds into Saturday remain southerly with 
localized gusts to 25 kts along the coast and KBLI. 

KSEA...VFR conditions with light rain into this evening. VFR cigs 
with continued mid level clouds through Saturday. The next front 
arrives Saturday evening into Saturday night for increasing rain. 
Surface winds remain southerly, fluctuating between SE/SW at times 
into Saturday. 

JD/Mazurkiewicz

.MARINE...
A Gale Warning remains in place through this evening across the
Coastal Waters with SCA for portions of the interior waters. A 
brief break early Saturday before southerly winds increase again
Saturday ahead of a stronger weather system offshore. A Storm 
Force Warning has been issued for the outer Coastal Waters 
Saturday afternoon into Saturday night with gusts of 45 to 55 
kts during this period. A Storm Force Watch remains in affect 
for the inner Coastal Waters. Gale Warnings and Watches are also
in affect for the western Strait of Juan de Fuca, Admiralty 
Inlet, eastern Strait of Juan de Fuca, and northern inland 
waters Saturday night. Additional headlines may be necessary for
Puget Sound and the central Strait of Juan de Fuca with the 
next system. South winds will ease a bit on Sunday, but remain 
elevated, especially for the Coastal Waters. Winds subside 
further Monday into early Tuesday before increasing again going 
into midweek with a stronger weather system moving southwards 
well offshore. 

Seas of 20 to 25 feet are expected into Saturday morning. Seas then 
range 15 to 20 feet Saturday into Saturday night, building again 
towards 22 feet for the outer Coastal Waters again Sunday. These 
large waves will also be energetic with a dominant period of 17 to 
20 seconds into Saturday. Seas remain elevated before subsiding to 
10 to 15 feet on Tuesday. JD

&&

.HYDROLOGY...
The potential for flooding continues on the Skokomish River 
with periods of heavy rainfall through the weekend. A Flood 
Watch remains in effect. 

River flooding is not expected on the other rivers in the area 
in the next 7 days at this time.
