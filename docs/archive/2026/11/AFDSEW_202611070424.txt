.UPDATE...
We're post-frontal across western WA this evening as the
front that brought widespread rainfall and gusty winds has
since crossed the Cascades. In its wake, showers are lingering 
given recent KATX/KLGX scans but latest hi-res modeled
reflectivity has conditions drying out as the night progresses -
indicative of building transient 500 mb ridging overhead.
Overnight lows are to bottom into the 40s. A High Wind Watch
remains in place along the coast from Saturday afternoon through
Saturday night as the next disturbance looms well offshore. The
previous discussion remains below.

.SYNOPSIS...
A frontal system continues to move east into the 
Cascades into tonight. A warm front will boost temperatures as 
it moves northwards Saturday for brief dry conditions. A 
potent cold front brings rain and gusty winds Saturday night 
into Sunday. The active pattern continues at times into next 
week.

&&

.SHORT TERM /TONIGHT THROUGH SUNDAY NIGHT/...
A front continues
to slowly slide southeast this afternoon, leading to lingering
steadier rain across the southern tier of western Washington.
South winds are also slowly tapering off. Peak wind gusts over
the last 12 hours generally reached 40 to 60 MPH along the 
coast, and from Admiralty Inlet northwards. The front will
progress east of the Cascades into tonight, resulting in
generally drier conditions and lighter winds. A warm front then
lifts northwards into Saturday. Although the majority of western
Washington will be dry into the afternoon, rain may continue at
times along the western Olympic Peninsula. Temperatures will
peak in the 60s on Saturday as well in the warm sector. 

A potent cold front progresses eastwards Saturday evening into
Saturday night across W WA. This will bring a period of steadier
rainfall, with rain rates briefly reaching 0.15-0.25" per hour
along the front. The more impactful aspect to this system will be
the stronger southerly winds associated with the front. Latest 
REFS guidance suggests a 50-75% chance of gusts exceeding 58 MPH
later Saturday (High Wind Warning threshold), with a High Wind 
Watch remaining in effect. Additional wind headlines may be 
needed elsewhere with gusts ranging 30 to 45 MPH for most areas 
Saturday PM into early Sunday. Due to continued foliage on the 
trees for many locations, localized power outages will be 
possible. Furthermore, clogged storm drains may occur when the 
period of gusty winds overlaps with steadier rain Saturday 
evening. Give yourself extra time if traveling Saturday evening.

Steadier rain transitions to periodic showers on Sunday, with
the majority of the QPF focused on the North Cascades and
Olympics. Snow levels around 7500 feet through Saturday night
fall to near 4500 feet on Sunday for high elevation mountain
snowfall. By Sunday, the primary concern shifts to high surf
conditions along the coast. Seas build to 20 to 28 feet on
Sunday, resulting in large breaking waves along the coast. A
High Surf Advisory has been issued, and will need to monitor the
need for a High Surf Warning over the next day as periods
approach 15 to 17 seconds. Caution should be used when in or 
near the water, especially those who will be participating in 
razor clam digging. High temperatures cool into the 50s by
Sunday.

&&

.LONG TERM /MONDAY THROUGH FRIDAY/...
A brief shortwave ridge
builds over the Pacific Northwest Sunday night into Monday for
drier conditions. The next warm front quickly approaches Monday
afternoon for additional rain and locally breezy conditions 
late Monday through Monday night. Ensembles suggest a steady 
plume of moisture then becomes focused over Vancouver Island and
Western Washington on Tuesday for additional rainfall, warmer 
temperatures, and breezy conditions. At this time the heaviest 
QPF amounts are focused over the Olympics and North Cascades. 
Uncertainty increases on Wednesday as ridging builds over the 
interior Rockies with troughing offshore. This will continue to 
result in continued warmer temperatures, with the highest 
probability for rainfall over the Olympics. More widespread 
rainfall chances return Thursday into late week. JD

.AVIATION...
Mixed bag of flight conditions across western Washington this 
evening, with some inland terminals seeing ceilings lifting to low 
end VFR and other terminals in IFR low clouds as rain showers 
continue to move east. Ceilings should gradually improve later this 
evening as rain pushes into Eastern Washington to low-end VFR for 
the majority of terminals. However, terminals along the coast, 
Kitsap Peninsula, and southern interior may struggle to improve and 
may not see improvement until Saturday afternoon. Breezy southerly 
winds possible through this evening with gusts 20-25 kt possible. 
Winds will ease tonight for light to calm winds in the interior. 

A strong frontal system will impact Western Washington starting 
Saturday afternoon. While the impacts to the interior are expected 
to arrive after the 00Z TAF issuance, impacts will start to be felt 
along the coast in the afternoon as early as 21Z. Southwesterly 
winds will increase after 21Z and will approach gusts of 35-40 kt 
towards Sun 00Z, with rain reaching the coast after Sun 01Z.

.MARINE...
An active marine pattern will continue into next week as a series of 
frontal systems impact area waters. A frontal system earlier today 
has brought elevated seas and winds. Seas will remain elevated above 
10 feet and a Small Craft Advisory will follow once gale-force winds 
subside for the coastal waters and West Strait. 

A stronger frontal system is expected Saturday afternoon into Sunday 
morning. This system will bring a plethora of hazards to area 
waters, including elevated seas and winds. With high-res guidance 
continuing to indicate a 70% chance or higher of seeing storm-force 
winds over the coastal waters, the Storm Watch has been upgraded to 
a Storm Warning. Gale Watches remain in effect for the interior 
waters. Seas are expected to build over 20 feet late Saturday night 
into Sunday, with seas building towards 28 feet in the outer coastal 
waters. Seas will gradually decrease towards Sunday night into 
Monday, but will remain above 10 feet. 

Weak high pressure will briefly build over the waters on Monday. 
However, the next frontal system arrives in the evening, followed by 
multiple systems that will pose additional marine hazards to area 
waters.

.HYDROLOGY...
River flooding is not expected through the next 
seven days. However, multiple weather systems will bring
steadier rainfall at times today, late Saturday into Sunday, and
again late Monday into midweek. While current forecasts are not
expected to cause flooding, this will begin to saturate soils 
and raise base flows that could bring a concern with subsequent 
storms systems, particularly by late next week.
