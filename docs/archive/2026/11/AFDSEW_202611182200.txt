.SYNOPSIS...
An upper level low drifting inland to the south will maintain
light showers through tonight before high pressure brings drier
conditions through early Saturday. Chances for precipitation 
increase Sunday and beyond, alongside breezy winds and chances 
for coastal flooding due to higher astronomical tides.

&&

.SHORT TERM /TODAY THROUGH FRIDAY NIGHT/...
Rain showers will continue across western Washington through the
evening tonight as an upper level low to the south slowly 
shifts inland. As the low weakens, winds will calm through the
afternoon and become light overnight. Wrap around moisture from
the low pressure system to the south will likely be cut off by 
Thursday, though a few lingering showers over the southern 
Cascades cannot be ruled out. An upper level ridge will build 
into the region as the low exits, bringing in drier conditions 
and maintaining near normal temperatures. The weather will stay 
fairly benign through Friday, with periods of morning fog and
low stratus and cloudy afternoons. Highs across the lowlands 
will peak in the upper 40s to lower 50s, with low temperatures 
generally in the 30s. Near-freezing temperatures early Friday
morning across the southwest interior may also allow for pockets
freezing fog to develop early Friday.

&&

.LONG TERM /SATURDAY THROUGH TUESDAY/...
The upper level ridge will likely keep conditions dry through at
least Saturday morning before shifting eastward as the next
weather system moves into the region. A cutoff low will develop
offshore by late Saturday, advecting bands of moisture into
western Washington into Sunday while driving breezy easterly
winds through the Cascade gaps. With snow levels around 
2000-2500 feet, the mountains will see a dusting of snow with 
light rain showers over the lowlands.

Conditions may briefly calm in between storm systems on Monday
before a negatively tilted frontal system moves into the region
on Tuesday. Forecast models continue to show that this system
will be stronger and more organized, potentially bringing in 
widespread breezy winds and snow through the Cascade passes. 
Astronomical tides will also peak next week, bringing increased 
potential for coastal flooding, which may be enhanced by this 
incoming low pressure system.

.AVIATION...
A low pressure system with an associated occluded front will 
continue to drive showers across most southern terminals through the 
afternoon. Most terminals will hold on to VFR conditions through the 
afternoon/evening as the rain goes through. The south interior still 
has the best chance for brief MVFR conditions going through the 
evening. Visibility reductions down to 4 miles will be possible with 
the rain moving through. East winds will decrease down to 4-8 kt 
through the afternoon and evening, becoming more northeast this 
overnight/Thursday. VFR ceilings will likely keep fog and mist 
concerns to isolated valleys/low lying areas, with skies becoming 
mostly clear Thursday afternoon.

KSEA...VFR generally anticipated through the TAF period, with 
ceilings clearing late Thursday morning. East/northeast winds 5-10 
kt with gusts to 20 kt will reduce to around 5 kt tonight into 
Thursday, increasing back to 5-10 kt from the northeast.

HPR

.MARINE...
A low pressure system spinning offshore continues to bring elevated 
seas and winds to the coastal waters. Small craft advisories will 
continue for the coastal waters as the winds decrease this 
afternoon, with 10-14 ft seas lingering through Thursday. The small 
craft advisory also includes Grays Harbor Bar through Thursday. The 
segment for the western two thirds of the Strait of Juan de Fuca 
will be allowed to expire this evening. High pressure will briefly 
rebuild over the waters Thursday and Friday for more benign marine 
conditions, and seas falling to 5-8 ft. Next weekend into early next 
week become active again with multiple low pressure systems moving 
through. The windiest period will be Monday into Tuesday with 
widespread gale gusts likely in the coastal waters and portions of 
the strait. There is still a 40-50% chance of storm force gusts in 
the outer coastal waters late Monday. Seas will also rise back over 
10 ft over the weekend, with seas approaching 12 to 17 ft by midweek.
