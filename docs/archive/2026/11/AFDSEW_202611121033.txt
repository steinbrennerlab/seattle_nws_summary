.SYNOPSIS...
A cold front remains stalled offshore today with mainly dry 
conditions for interior areas and light rain at times near the 
coast. A series of frontal systems will bring a return of 
widespread rain and occasionally windy conditions Friday into 
early next week.

&&

.SHORT TERM /TODAY THROUGH SATURDAY/...
Much like yesterday, a front remains stalled offshore with a
very mild air mass in place across the region. Clouds will 
stream across the area throughout the day ahead with periods of 
light rainfall skirting the coast and Olympic Peninsula as the 
interior remains mostly dry. Models remain largely unchanged
with the overall pattern heading into the conclusion of the
week. A vertically stacked system moving into the offshore
waters tonight and early Friday will push an occluded front
onshore during the day on Friday with another shot of rain and
breezy to windy conditions...especially coast and near east/west 
gaps in terrain. High tides and lower pressure may lead to minor
coastal flooding Friday morning. The main area is the shoreline
and beaches around the Salish Sea during the time of high tide.
While precip with this system doesn't look particularly heavy, 
it should be sufficient to push the Skokomish River back above 
flood stage. 

&&

.LONG TERM /SATURDAY NIGHT THROUGH WEDNESDAY/...
A stronger system remains on track to arrive later Saturday 
into Saturday night. More widespread windy conditions are
expected with this system...strongest along the coast where NBM
probabilities of 50 mph gusts are 50-70 percent. QPF is also 
heavier with storm total rainfall estimates between between 
Saturday midday and Sunday afternoon of 1.5 to 2 inches along 
the coast and upwards of 3 to 4 inches in the Olympics. 

Showery conditions continue Sunday night into Monday before
tapering off. Forecast confidence begins to decrease Tuesday and
beyond as both deterministic and ensembles start to diverge 
with some solutions now suggesting that a system around the 
middle of next week will cut off and head south of the area. 
One portion of the forecast that retains at least some 
confidence is a downward trend in temperatures to nearer 
seasonal norms next week.

.AVIATION...
A stalled frontal boundary will remain offshore today as showers 
continue west of the Olympics for terminals such as KHQM and KUIL. 
As a result, IFR conditions will be met throughout the remainder of 
the morning with MVFR cigs possible for KCLM as well. Mid-high level 
cloud coverage will keep mostly VFR in place for inland terminals 
but KOLM and KPWT may have to contend with fog and low clouds 
throughout the remainder of the morning until conditions improve 
around 18-20z. VFR area wide including the coastal areas towards the 
afternoon but steadier widespread rain will return late overnight 
into Friday morning with a return of low ceilings.


KSEA...VFR likely to persist through the upcoming TAF period as high 
clouds stream overhead. Low chance (5%) of vicinity fog in the 
morning near the Duwamish River/east of the terminal this morning. 
Winds mainly out of the SE 5 kt or less.

.MARINE...
A frontal boundary remains stalled offshore today as winds begin to 
pick up this afternoon across marine waters. A pair of strong low 
pressure systems will later move across the coastal waters later 
tonight into Friday, with the second moving through Saturday night 
into Sunday. As a result, the inherited Gale Watch across the 
coastal waters have been upgraded to a warning and a new SCA has 
been issued for the East Strait, starting later this morning and 
lasting into early Friday morning. Additional SCAs may be needed 
elsewhere. Seas will remain around 12 to 15 ft today before 
increasing above 20 ft Friday night with a 25-35% chance of seas 
above 25 ft through Saturday morning. An active pattern may remain 
into early next week as the parade of storms continue.

.HYDROLOGY...
The Skokomish River fell below flood stage overnight. The river
will remain elevated into Friday then rise back above flood 
stage later Friday into the weekend with another round of heavy 
rain over the Olympics.

River flooding is not expected on the other rivers in the area 
in the next 7 days at this time. Felton/27
