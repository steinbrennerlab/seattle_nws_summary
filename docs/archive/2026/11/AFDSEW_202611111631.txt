.SYNOPSIS...A warm front has introduced a very mild air mass to
Western Washington with near record high temperatures expected 
today. A cold front will stall offshore into Thursday with 
mainly dry conditions for interior areas and light rain at times
near the coast. A series of frontal systems will bring a return
of widespread rain and occasionally windy conditions Friday 
into early next week.

&&

.SHORT TERM /TODAY THROUGH FRIDAY/...Winds remain gusty along
the coast and North Interior this morning. Lastest guidance
shows winds easing this afternoon. Minor coastal flooding is
expected in the San Juans and western Wahtcom county this
morning with high tide - a Coastal Flood Advisory remains in
effect. 33

Previous discussion...Rain has ended for most areas except for
a few showers near the coast early this morning. Temperatures 
are extraordinarily mild across the region in the wake of a warm
front that lifted north across Western Washington late 
yesterday. Windy conditions will continue along the coast and 
across the north interior, but are expected to ease late today. 
A cold front will stall offshore today into Thursday. Areas of 
light rain will occasionally skirt the coast and Olympic 
Peninsula ahead of this boundary, but the remainder of the area 
is expected to be largely dry with high temperatures, especially
today, threatening some daily records. 

A fairly deep upper trough moving across the Gulf of Alaska will
push an occluded front onshore into the region during the day 
on Friday. Fortunately, this system looks like it will be 
vertically stacked upon arrival...thereby losing a lot of its 
punch before coming ashore. Nonetheless, it will bring another 
round of rain along with breezy to locally windy conditions. It 
will also churn up some pretty large surf during its offshore 
journey with seas of 20+ feet reaching coastal areas again by 
Friday night.

&&

.LONG TERM /FRIDAY NIGHT THROUGH TUESDAY/...A trailing upper 
trough is expected to quickly swing onshore Friday night. 
Another potent front approaches the region on Saturday night. 
Though this system is also vertically stacked, the path of the 
surface low gives it better wind potential, especially for the 
coast, along with a fairly deep moisture feed. And, as such, WPC
has placed a large swath of the Olympic Peninsula under 
marginal risk in their Excessive Rainfall Outlook for 12Z 
Saturday to 12Z Sunday. Plenty of post-frontal showers are 
expected to spread onshore into Sunday keeping things fairly 
soggy. There's still decent model agreement with a brief break 
for Monday, but the key word is brief. Another wet and 
potentially windy front arrives in the Tuesday time frame. It 
might have started slow, but November as we know it is well and 
truly here now.

.AVIATION...Southwest flow aloft continues into tonight with a 
stalled frontal boundary over the offshore waters. Areas of MVFR
in light rain will continue through the morning for coastal 
areas while VFR is expected to continue throughout the day for 
the interior under varying amounts of mid and high level cloud 
cover. Gusty southerly surface winds along the coast and across 
the north interior are expected to ease later this afternoon. 
With lighter surface winds expected tonight, there is an 
increased risk of areas of IFR low clouds forming west of Puget 
Sound, across the Southwest Interior, and far south Puget Sound 
on Thursday morning.

KSEA...VFR with varying amounts of high and mid level clouds today. 
Surface winds south to southeasterly 7 to 10 knots with gusts 
to 20 kt in the early afternoon easing to 6 knots or less tonight.

.MARINE...Strong winds will continue to persist across the 
coastal waters through much of the day today as a cold front 
remains stalled offshore. This front will weaken as it slowly 
drifts onshore this evening, allowing winds to slowly ease this 
evening into early Thursday morning. Gale Warnings for the 
coastal waters and East Strait of Juan de Fuca, and Northern 
Inland Waters, and Small Craft Advisories for Admiralty Inlet 
will continue through evening as well. Seas 13 to 15 ft this 
morning may creep slightly upward towards 16 to 18 ft, but 
generally remaining in the 14 to 16 ft range through tonight. 
Seas will ease to around 11 to 13 ft on Thursday.

A pair of strong low pressure systems will move across the coastal 
waters late Thursday night into Friday, with the second moving 
through Saturday night into Sunday. Winds will begin to increase 
across the area waters on Thursday. Gales will begin as early as 
Thursday afternoon, through winds look to peak overnight into early 
Friday morning. Guidance continues to indicate 50-70% chances for 
storm force gusts over the outer coastal waters, but confidence has 
decreased for the inner coastal waters with high-end gales the more 
likely scenario there. Winds will ease Friday night before 
increasing again Saturday with a secondary peak Saturday evening. 
Gale force winds again are very likely across the coastal waters, 
with 40-50% chances for storm force gusts over the outer coastal 
waters.

Seas once again will build over the coastal waters on Friday.
With the low center tracking closer towards our area, that will send 
the largest waves farther south towards Oregon. That being said, 
very large seas are expected, with ensemble guidance continuing to 
show very high confidence (>90%) in seas above 20 ft by Friday 
evening, with a 25-35% chance of seas above 25 ft through Saturday 
morning. Dominant periods will be very long, in the 16-18 s range. 
Seas look to briefly ease Saturday morning below 20 ft before 
another system will send another round of large, long period waves 
on Sunday with high (70-80%) confidence in seas above 20 ft with a 
15-30% chance of seas exceeding 25 ft through early Monday.

With these systems, slight shifts in the low tracks will determine 
where the strongest wave generation/dynamic fetch regions will be. 
While it is looking more likely that the largest waves will be aimed 
towards Oregon and California from the Friday/Saturday system, the 
Sunday/Monday system favors a more northward track with the largest 
waves aimed towards the Washington coast. There is a 5-10% chance of 
waves exceeding 30 ft at the peak wave height Sunday night.

.HYDROLOGY...Steady rain throughout Tuesday across the Olympic 
basin contributed to a rapid rise on the Skokomish River near 
Potlatch with the river reaching moderate flood stage late 
yesterday evening. The river is expected to slowly recede later 
this morning, but additional rainfall over the upcoming weekend 
will bring renewed potential for flooding. 

River flooding is not expected on the other rivers in the area 
in the next 7 days at this time.
