.SYNOPSIS...
Precipitation chances will linger today as a weak system brings
light rain to the lowlands and light snow to the Cascades. A 
weak ridge building over the Pacific Northwest will bring a 
brief break in precipitation this weekend. An active weather 
pattern resumes early next week with a series of systems moving
across the region.

&&

.SHORT TERM /TODAY THROUGH SUNDAY/...
Precipitation associated with a stalled frontal boundary over 
Oregon will spread northward into the area later today, but it 
will be considerably lighter than what we saw on Thanksgiving 
morning. With a cooler air mass in place, we'll see some light 
snowfall in the passes. But it is expected to fall short of any 
headlines. Shortwave ridging will slide across the region late 
tonight into Saturday for a short reprieve from the recent 
active weather. 

Another developing system moves into the offshore waters on
Sunday with some rain spreading inland across Western Washington
through the course of the day. Models do not have a good handle
on the track and strength of the surface low. Parts of Western 
Washington will likely see windy conditions developing by Sunday
night, but the path of the low will define a lot of the 
details. And we'll discuss that in the long term discussion.

Coastal flooding remains a threat for many areas heading into 
the weekend. Areas along the north interior coastline are 
favored to see the highest degree of coastal flooding over the 
weekend, with lesser impacts expected elsewhere.

&&

.LONG TERM /SUNDAY NIGHT THROUGH THURSDAY/...
As alluded to in the short term discussion, the path of a surface
low Sunday night into Monday could have significant implications
in terms of the wind forecast. Models solutions range from a
weakening surface low tracking into northern Vancouver Island to
a concerning 00Z operational ECMWF showing a sub 985 millibar
low making landfall over Grays Harbor county early Monday AM 
and tracking northeastward across the CWA. Stay tuned to the 
forecast as models get a better handle as to how this is 
expected to play out. 

There is a trend toward a somewhat quieter period of weather as
we move toward the middle of the coming week with the storm 
track diving southward into California. This pattern favors 
anomalous weak ridging just offshore. While this doesn't imply
an entirely dry forecast, it does favor somewhat more benign 
conditions as systems moving into British Columbia clip the 
area.

Additional coastal flooding may occur into next week due to 
high astronomical tides.

.AVIATION...
Westerly flow aloft this morning as a weak system will move through 
western Washington late this morning and afternoon. Showers 
near OLM this morning, with rain lifting north and becoming 
widespread after 19Z. Rain activity will subside this evening. 
Majority of terminals remain VFR this morning. However, a few 
terminals are reporting MVFR to IFR. Ceilings will continue to 
lower with widespread MVFR by this afternoon, continuing into 
Saturday. S/SW winds 5-7 kt today, will become light to calm 
tonight into Saturday morning. 

With light/calm winds tonight and residual moisture, there is a 60-
70% chance for visibility less than 1SM over the southern interior. 
However, there is uncertainty with Puget Sound terminals as fog 
development would be dependent on the amount of clearing behind 
today's system. Latest guidance suggests there is a 35-45% chance 
for visibility less than 1SM, especially in the valleys. 

KSEA...MVFR this morning and will likely prevail into Saturday. Rain 
will start impacting the terminal around 19Z, which could reduce 
visibility to 4SM at times in heavier rain. Rain will subside 
this evening. S/SW winds 5-7 kt will become light this 
evening/tonight, introducing the potential for fog at terminal 
Saturday morning. While there is uncertainty on fog development 
due to sky cover, high-res guidance suggests there is a 35% 
chance for visibility below 1SM around 16Z. Winds will shift NE 
after 12Z Saturday at 3-5 kt.

.MARINE...
Winds will continue throughout the day with Small 
Craft Advisories continuing for the coastal waters, mainly due 
to elevated seas. A weak system will approach the area waters
today, with minimal impacts to wind and seas. High pressure will
then develop over the coastal waters through the weekend, with
wind and seas further easing. The exception is the Strait of
Juan De Fuca, where a westerly push of winds is likely.

A low pressure looks to move into the area Monday into Tuesday,
for increasing winds and seas. Latest guidance is a bit shaky on
the exact tracking of the low pressure system, which could
impact the strength of the winds. Will continue to monitor as
more guidance becomes available.

Coastal seas generally 10 to 15 this morning decreasing to below
10 early this evening. Seas will decrease even further to 4 to 6
over the weekend, before the aforementioned system moves in on
Monday, increasing seas upwards to 15 to 18 feet.

.HYDROLOGY...
River levels should continue to gradually fall with the 
Skokomish expected to recede below flood stage tonight. 
Additional rises to the Skokomish are possible next week with 
additional incoming precipitation, but a large degree of 
uncertainty remains over the extended forecast.
