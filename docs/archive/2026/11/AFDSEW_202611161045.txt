.SYNOPSIS...
Cool upper level trough offshore will move inland tonight. 
Split flow aloft Tuesday ahead of the next system moving inland 
south of the area Wednesday into Thursday. Upper level ridge 
over Western Washington Friday. Next system splitting as it 
moves into the Pacific Northwest over the weekend with most of 
the energy going into Northern California. 

&&

.SHORT TERM /TODAY THROUGH WEDNESDAY/...
Satellite imagery shows upper level trough offshore with a band
of showers from the Western Strait of Juan de Fuca southward 
along the coast. Doppler radar indicating a few light showers 
in the Cascade Foothills. Decreasing clouds the last few hours 
have allowed temperatures to drop into the mid 30s to lower 40s.

Upper level trough remaining offshore today keeping showers in
the forecast. Air mass slightly unstable. Shower activity
increasing this afternoon as the trough axis approaches the
coast and a little daytime heating increases the instablilty. 
Today will be the coolest day in two weeks with highs only in 
the lower 50s. 

Seas in the upper teens to near 20 feet this morning subsiding 
to 10 to 14 feet by tonight. High surf advisory will remain in
effect until 10 AM this morning. 

Upper level trough axis moving through Western Washington
tonight. Shower activity becoming confined to the coast and
mountains after midnight. Lows in the mid 30s to lower 40s. 

Dry day Tuesday with split flow aloft caused by the deep cool 
upper level trough and associated front digging south offshore 
between 130W and 140W. Light surface gradients in the morning 
combining with plenty of low level moisture to produce areas of 
fog. Increasing offshore flow in the afternoon as the system 
offshore gets closer to the coast will help dry out the lower 
layers of the air mass enough to dissipate the fog. Highs 
remaining in the lower 50s.

Vertically stacked low offshore moving southeast Tuesday night.
Low level offshore flow continuing to increase with the cross 
cascade gradients getting down to around -6 mb. Low enough to 
create some breezy easterly winds in the Cascade Foothills. 
Middle and high level clouds out ahead of the frontal system 
spreading over the area overnight but not before temperatures 
have dropped into the mid 30s to lower 40s. 

Weather system moving inland south of the area Wednesday. 
Showers rotating around the low could get as far north as 
Everett in the afternoon. Best chances for showers will be over 
the Southwest Interior and Central Coast in the morning. 
Increasing cloud cover will knock a couple of degrees off the 
high temperatures with some locations, especially over the 
southern portion of the area, remaining in the 40s. Highs in the
lower 50s elsewhere. 

&&

.LONG TERM /WEDNESDAY NIGHT THROUGH SUNDAY/...
Upper level low a little further north in the solutions this 
morning keeping a chance of showers over the Central and 
Southern Cascades Wednesday evening. 

Dry for the most part Thursday but what is left of the upper 
level low hanging around in the vicinity. Will not be surprised 
if future runs reintroduce a chance of showers to the southern 
half Thursday. Upper low kicks off to the east Thursday night 
with a high amplitude, but narrow, upper level ridge over the 
area Friday making for a dry day with areas of morning fog. 
Upper level ridge gets pinched off Friday night as another upper
level low drops down from the Gulf of Alaska and takes a 
position off the coast for the weekend. This feature will bring 
showers back to the forecast Saturday and Sunday. Snow levels
lowering to below pass level over the weekend but at this point
not much moisture making it into the area making for light new
snow accumulations at best.

Lows in the 30s with the cooler locations getting into the upper
20s Friday and Saturday morning. Highs in the mid 40s to lower
50s. Felton

.MARINE...
More much benign conditions across are waters today (minus seas over 
the coastal waters). Winds are lighter across all area waters on 
Monday as high pressure briefly builds over the area. Seas will be 
around 15-20 ft this morning before gradually decreasing reaching 9-
12 ft by early Tuesday morning. Winds will increase on Tuesday and 
Tuesday night as a low pressure system passes by off to the west and 
south. Winds will turn offshore, which may be locally enhanced near 
the Strait of Juan de Fuca and near Grays Harbor when the wind 
direction is aligned with the terrain gaps, mainly Tuesday night. 
This will also push seas back up to 12-15 ft Tuesday night into 
early Wednesday. SCAs and potential gales may be issued for the 
coastal waters and Strait of Juan de Fuca. 

Calmer conditions will then follow as winds and seas ease during the 
day Wednesday, with another brief round of high pressure over the 
area Thursday into early Friday. Another series of systems look to 
move through the area Friday night into the weekend.
