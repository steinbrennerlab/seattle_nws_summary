.SYNOPSIS...
A strong frontal system will impact Western Washington today
with widespread rain, gusty winds, and coastal flooding. 
Precipitation chances will linger into Friday with a weaker 
system. A brief break in precipitation is expected this weekend 
as a weak ridge builds over the Pacific Northwest. An active 
weather pattern resumes early next week. 

&&

.SHORT TERM /TODAY THROUGH SATURDAY/...
A strong warm front reached the central Washington coast around
midnight as evidenced by a quick veer to strong southerly wind
and a spike in temperature at Hoquiam. Windy conditions will
continue through the morning and spread to Puget Sound as the
warm front lifts northward across the interior. A trailing cold
front will push inland this afternoon for a transition to a 
post-frontal unstable air mass with showers and isolated 
thunderstorms. Winds will ease behind the front. 

A much weaker system riding over the northern periphery of a
ridge to our south will spread some light rain back across the 
area by Friday afternoon, but QPF and wind will be much less of
a factor with this system. A low amplitude upper ridge will
shift onshore across the Pacific Northwest on Saturday for a
brief drying trend and a return to seasonal temperatures.

Coastal flooding will continue to be a concern today as
astronomical high tides combine with storm surge and high surf 
to produce some significant tidal anomalies and impacts to 
coastal communities. Coastal flooding impacts are expected to 
peak for the inland waters today before tides gradually weaken.

&&

.LONG TERM /SATURDAY NIGHT THROUGH WEDNESDAY/...
A weak upper ridge axis is expected to shift east of the area
Saturday night. Another upper trough is expected to move into 
the offshore waters on Sunday, but ensembles lack any 
significant cohesion with its evolution. It's worth noting that 
the 00Z operational run of the GFS spins up another sub 980 
millibar surface low offshore by Sunday night. Given the 
potential impacts to holiday travel, we'll have to keep an eye 
on this. 

Moving forward into the first half of next week, model
disagreements persist. There is some hint of another short-lived
dry break in the Wednesday timeframe. Current forecasts for the
period have near normal temps and a generic chance of rain. In 
other words, a near climo forecast for early December.

.AVIATION...
Breezy and wet conditions early this morning with a mixed bag of
conditions across western Washington. As a front moves through
the area this morning, heavier batches of rain have put most
terminals in MVFR/IFR conditions, with some interior terminals
seeing VFR as well. Additionally, heavier rain has brought 
reductions in visibilities, generally around 2-5 SM. Expect this
trend throughout most of the morning. Precipitation will become
more showery in nature later this afternoon, which will allow
ceilings to improve to MVFR/VFR across the area. There is a
15-25% chance of thunderstorms across most of the area this
morning into the afternoon hours, with confidence being low in
occurrence to put in the TAF at this time. 

S/SE gusty winds this morning, with gusts up to 25 to 35 kts 
for interior locations. Strongest winds continue to be along the
coast for HQM and near BLI, where gusts up to 40 to 45 kts 
continue to be possible this morning. Winds will remain elevated
throughout the afternoon, with a notable decrease this evening,
with southwesterly winds gusting up briefly up to 15 kts by 
00z.

KSEA...VFR conditions this morning in rain and breezy winds.
Heavier rain moving through the terminal this morning could drop
conditions down to MVFR at times, along with reductions in
visibilities 3 to 5 SM. Rain will transition into more showery
in nature, allowing for more broken ceilings and a return to
more VFR conditions over the terminal. Additionally, there
remains a 15% chance of thunderstorms this afternoon, however
confidence continues to remain low. 

Breezy S/SE this morning with sustained winds 15 to 20 kts,
with potential gusts up to 30 kts. Winds will remain breezy
through the afternoon with a notable decrease by 00z, turning
southwesterly and sustained 10 to 15 kts, with gusts up to 15 to

.MARINE...A strong frontal system will continue to cross over
the waters this morning, bringing strong winds and elevated 
seas to area waters. A Gale Warning remains in effect for all
coastal water zones, and all interior zones; the exception being
the East Entrance of the Strait of Juan De Fuca, where a Storm 
Warning continues. A Small Craft remains in the Central Strait 
of Juan De Fuca. Thunderstorms are also favored to develop over
the coastal waters early this morning and move inland towards 
the inner waters throughout the day, producing localized 
lightning, gusty winds, and small hail.

Winds will start to decrease this evening over the waters, with
a Small Craft Advisory out for the coastal waters, mainly for
continued elevated wind and seas. Conditions will start to calm
heading into Friday, with a weak system approaching the waters,
however impacts should remain minimal. High pressure is favored
to develop over the waters through the weekend, with seas and
winds to ease, the exception is the Strait of Juan De Fuca,
where a westerly push of winds is likely. 

Seas around 15 to 20 feet this morning will hover in this range
throughout the majority of the day, slowly decreasing this
evening into Friday morning around 10 to 15 feet. Seas will
lower even further by late Friday into Saturday, where we could
see seas get below 10 feet through the weekend. Seas are 
favored to rise above 10 feet into next week.

.HYDROLOGY...
A frontal system will bring an additional 1 to 3 inches of 
rainfall to the region today with snow levels as high as 
6000-7000 feet. The bulk of the moisture is expected over the 
Olympic Peninsula and along the coast. While area rivers will 
likely experience rises, no widespread river flooding is 
expected.

The exception is the Skokomish River, where a River Flood Warning 
remains in effect through Friday. The Skokomish will remain in 
moderate flood stage into this afternoon. The Skokomish is 
expected to fall below flood stage on Friday, with additional 
rises possible into next week.

The incoming heavy rainfall through Thursday may also pose a hazard 
to the Bear Gulch burn scar, which has been sensitive to 
precipitation so far this winter season. A handful of small 
landslides have already occurred on the burn scar, and additional 
landslides in this area cannot be ruled out this week as heavy rain 
continues over the Olympic Peninsula.
