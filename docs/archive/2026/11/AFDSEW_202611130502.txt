.SYNOPSIS...
A cold front remains stalled offshore today with 
mainly dry conditions for interior areas and light rain at times
near the coast. A series of frontal systems will bring a return
of widespread rain and occasionally windy conditions Friday 
into early next week.

.SHORT TERM /TONIGHT THROUGH SATURDAY NIGHT/...
A frontal system
remains stalled offshore today for another dry and mild day
across western WA. Temperatures continue to track warmer than
average with highs around 60. East winds will increase across 
the Cascade tonight with gusts to 20-30 MPH in the gaps and 
foothills. 

The stalled frontal system will slowly track east and inland on
Friday with the bulk of the rain along the coast. Rain will 
spread inland mid to late morning and afternoon with around a 
tenth to a quarter inch for most areas. High astronomical tide 
and lower pressure may lead to minor to moderate coastal 
flooding. The timing would be around the time of high tide
Friday morning. In addition, large waves will reach the coast 
with seas of 20 ft and a High Surf Advisory is in effect.

Another strong Pacific frontal system is poised to impact the
Pac NW Saturday-Sunday for more rain, gusty winds and high
elevation snow. Rain-wise, we're looking at another 1-3 inches 
of rain along the coast this weekend with 0.50-1.00 inches in 
the interior. Additional rainfall in the Olympics will keep the 
Skokomish River running high with the potential for flooding. 
It'll be windy too with the potential for strong wind gusts 
along the coast of 40-50 MPH. Interior areas will see gusts of 
30-40 MPH (winds peaking Saturday night). 33

.LONG TERM /SUNDAY THROUGH THURSDAY/...

Sunday and Monday with a trough over the region. Snow levels
will lower to around 4000 ft on Monday with just light snow

.AVIATION...
A stalled front remains offshore this evening. Majority of terminals 
are VFR this evening with some mist being reported around KOLM down 
to MVFR/IFR. The front will gain momentum inland tonight into Friday 
with showers expected to spread inland. Low level wind shear may 
impact terminals as the frontal system moves through. Ceilings will 
likely briefly dip to MVFR as the rain pushes through Friday 
morning, though conditions are favored to remain predominantly VFR 
for much of the day. The exception will be along the coast, where 
IFR conditions are expected to develop by Friday morning and 
continue throughout the day in rain, low ceilings, and mist. Post 
frontal showers will develop Friday afternoon, with slight chances 
for a few lightning strikes along the coast (confidence too low to 
include in TAFs at this time). Surface winds will generally remain 
south/southeast through the period, with winds generally 5-10 kt 
with gusts to 20 kt for the Puget Sound terminals and higher gusts 
up to 30 to 40 kt along the coast and over the mountains.

KSEA...VFR conditions with high clouds will continue into the mid 
morning. A frontal system will push inland between 12z-18z, bringing 
rain and potential for low level wind shear at the terminal. While 
conditions are expected to remain predominantly VFR, MVFR may 
briefly develop in heavier rain showers. Winds will generally remain 
out of the south or southeast around 10 kt or less, with gusts to 20 
kt developing at the terminal Friday afternoon.

.MARINE...
Small Craft Advisories for the East Strait of Juan de Fuca and Grays 
Harbor Bar will continue through the evening as SCAs transition to a 
Gale Warning over the coastal waters this evening as a stalled 
frontal system begins to push inland. The strongest winds will occur 
over the coastal waters later this evening into early Friday 
morning, reaching high end gale force. An occasional storm force 
gust cannot be ruled out, particularly over the outer coastal 
waters. Winds will remain elevated over the coastal waters and over 
the East Entrance of the Strait of Juan de Fuca throughout the day 
Friday, with Small Craft winds picking up over the northern inland 
waters late Friday as the front shifts eastward.

While winds over the inner waters remain elevated into Saturday 
morning, winds over the coastal waters will briefly decrease. A 
Small Craft Advisory has been issued for seas during this lull in 
wind, with an even stronger frontal system moving in close behind. 
Ensembles continue to show strong potential for Storm Force winds 
over the outer waters on Saturday, with an 60% to 70% chance of 
maximum wind gusts exceeding 50 knots over the outer coastal waters.

Small Craft winds are favored to continue on Sunday, at least over 
the coastal waters and potentially over some of the inner waters as 
well. Conditions are favored to calm by Monday, with potential for 
continued windy conditions over the waters returning by Tuesday.

Seas will remain elevated through the weekend as a series of dynamic 
systems cross area waters. Friday's system will bring very large 
seas to the Washington coast, with high confidence in waves building 
to 20 feet or above by Friday afternoon, with waves across the 
coastal waters as high as 26-27 feet. These large waves will also be 
very energetic, with a dominant period of 18 to 22 seconds. Seas 
will remain elevated through Saturday morning and will briefly ease 
a bit into early Sunday, remaining between 14 to 18 feet. Another 
system will send another round of large, long period waves on Sunday 
with another round of waves likely 20 feet or higher through early 
Monday. Seas look to ease a bit after Monday, but will likely stay 
in the 12-15 foot range heading into the middle of next week.
