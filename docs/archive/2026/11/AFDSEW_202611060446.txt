.SYNOPSIS...
A strong frontal system will bring gusty winds and 
additional rainfall to western Washington tonight and Friday. A warm 
front will boost temperatures as it moves northwards into the area 
Saturday. A follow-up cold front Saturday night into Sunday will 
bring another round of rain and gusty winds. The weather pattern 
remains active into at least the first half of next week.

&&

.SHORT TERM /TONIGHT THROUGH SATURDAY NIGHT/...
Current satellite imagery shows typical autumn cloudy skies over W 
WA in advance of the incoming frontal system. Radar is starting to 
clearly show the leading edge of this system over the outer coastal 
waters.

A weak shortwave ridge will hold that system at bay briefly, keeping 
conditions dry for much of W WA this afternoon and into the early 
evening before rain moves in tonight. Gusty winds will accompany the 
incoming front with particular concern for higher wind speeds along 
the coast and the NW Interior /including the islands as well as 
portions of western Whatcom and Skagit counties/...where speeds 
could get as high as 20-30 mph with gusts to 50 mph. Models have 
remained consistent with these speeds and as such, inherited Wind 
Advisory for the aforementioned areas remains on track. As this 
system mainly impacts the area through the day Friday, this is when 
the bulk of the rainfall is expected with amounts ranging one-half 
to three-quarters of an inch on the high end...mainly expected over 
the higher terrain and along the coast...to one tenth to one quarter 
of an inch most remaining lowland locations. Snow levels will rise 
throughout the event, starting the day off at around 5000-6000 ft 
and lifting to around 9000 in the afternoon. As such, not much of a 
concern for high elevation snowfall at this time.

The front pushes out of the area very late Friday night, allowing 
for another weak, short-lived shortwave ridge to enter the area. 
This feature will not be terribly effective at dispelling clouds nor 
lingering post-frontal showers, but does provide a relative lull in 
activity for much of Saturday before the next frontal system comes 
knocking on the coastline come Saturday night. Another round of 
strong winds will accompany this front as it moves through during 
the overnight hours, with wind impacts being felt over much of the 
CWA. While the more wind prone locations along the coast, the 
islands and the NW interior will likely see headline conditions, it 
is entirely possible that other locations along the Sound may see 
speeds high enough to qualify. It is a little too far out to make 
decisions on headlines just yet, but situation is being monitored.

Even though the short term will be active, temperatures are expected 
to remain mild with daytime highs still a few degrees above seasonal 
normals. Saturday will actually see a boost in temperatures with 
daytime highs that day getting into the 60s for most lowland 
locations. Overnight clouds will keep lows from sinking too much, 
mostly ranging from the mid 40s to around 50 for the entire short 
term period.

.LONG TERM /SUNDAY THROUGH THURSDAY/...
Winds ease quickly Sunday with most breezy conditions wrapping up by 
mid-morning. Frontal passage throughout the day will drag its feet a 
bit, but a follow-up shortwave ridge should begin to impact the area 
Sunday night into Monday. This break in the action continues until 
Monday afternoon, when the next frontal system will begin to enter 
the area. By this point, snow levels will have fallen to around 4000-
5000 ft, but are quick to rebound late Monday to above 6000 ft. The 
frontal passage late Monday will extend into Tuesday with another 
potential break possible Wednesday as upper level ridging builds 
over the Rockies. Models fall into disagreement here as the westward 
extent of the ridge is a point of contention. The operational 
deterministic models show the split well, with the ECMWF keeping the 
ridge east enough where rain could get at least as far east as the 
Olympic Peninsula while the GFS shows the ridge west enough to keep 
any systems off the coast. Ensembles show a wide array of solutions, 
however the mean does trend slightly drier...leaning more toward the 
ECMWF and perhaps even dragging precip slightly further east. The 
NBM falls in line with this latter thinking and is a good starting 
point for the forecast this far out. Neither daytime highs nor 
overnight lows stray too much from the temperature pattern 
established during the short term.

.AVIATION...
Generally VFR across western Washington terminals this evening, with 
areas of MVFR ceilings along the Pacific Coast as a frontal system 
with rain approaches the coast. An OVC100 ceiling across the region 
will gradually lower tonight as a frontal system pushes through the 
area, trending towards low end VFR to MVFR, with potential for lower 
IFR conditions in heavier rain. Rain will start moving in along the 
coast around 06Z and fill into the interior terminals after 10Z 
tonight. Additionally, gusty winds will develop tonight into Friday, 
mainly along the coast and north of PAE, where sustained wind speeds 
of 15-25 kt with gusts toward 30-40 kt are possible after 09z Fri. 
Southeasterly winds will also become breezy elsewhere, with gusts up 
to 20-30 kt through Friday afternoon.

KSEA...VFR this evening with mid to high clouds gradually lowering 
early Friday morning closer to low end VFR/MVFR as a frontal system 
moves inland. Rain will spread over the terminal in the early 
morning Friday, likely bringing MVFR conditions after 09z Fri. SE 
winds around 5-10 kt, with gusts up to 20 kt possible during the day 
Friday.

.MARINE...
An active weather patter across area waters will continue into next 
week as a series of frontal systems cross the region. These frontal 
systems will bring both the combination of seas and winds. The next 
frontal system will impact the waters tonight into Friday, bringing 
elevated seas around 14-20 ft and gale force winds across the 
coastal waters and portions of the interior waters. Additionally, 
there is a slight chance (15-20%) for storm force winds at times for 
the coastal waters, East Strait, and Northern Inland Waters. A 
stronger system will arrive this weekend for elevated seas and 
possible widespread gale force winds. Models are hinting at a 30-40% 
chance for storm force gusts over the coastal waters and Strait on 
Saturday afternoon and a 20% chance for for Admiralty Inlet, 
Northern Inland Waters, and Puget Sound. Additionally, models 
continue to be consistent with the chances for seas above 20 feet by 
early Sunday morning. Brief high pressure will build over the waters 
Sunday night into early Monday. Multiple systems will move over the 
waters next week, with the continued potential for additional 
headlines. 

A period of strong afternoon ebbs are expected for Grays Harbor Bar 
starting Friday, which could coincide with with a rough to severe 
bar at times.

.HYDROLOGY...
River flooding is not expected through the next 
seven days. However, a series of increasingly wet weather 
systems will bring rain (and high mountain snow) to the region,
especially from Friday onward. While current forecasts are not 
expected to cause flooding, this will begin to saturate soils 
and raise base flows that could bring a concern with subsequent 
storms systems into next week.
