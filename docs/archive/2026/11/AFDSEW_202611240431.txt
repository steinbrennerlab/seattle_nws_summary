Area Forecast Discussion
National Weather Service Seattle WA
831 PM PST Mon Nov 23 2026

.SYNOPSIS...
A series of systems will bring widespread precipitation, gusty 
winds, and increase the potential for coastal flooding along with 
high surf throughout much of the upcoming week. An upper level ridge 
is possible for the end of the week, however there is uncertainty 
regarding its placement and amplitude. These factors would determine 
the impacts of this ridge for western Washington.

&&

.SHORT TERM /TONIGHT THROUGH WEDNESDAY NIGHT/...
Main band of precip has lingered over the coastline and adjacent 
interior lowlands for much of the evening...moving eastward at a 
very slow rate. This continues to vibe well with current model 
guidance which really does not have precip making its way east of 
the Sound until the overnight hours tonight. Increasing winds line 
up with precip band...mainly present in gust speeds however 
sustained are starting to increase as well. That said, inherited 
forecast and headlines remain in good shape and no evening update 
appears to be needed at this time. For additional forecast details, 
please refer to the Previous Discussion section below.  18

From Previous Discussion...Shower coverage is becoming sparse across 
the region this afternoon. However, a 965 mb surface low and its 
associated frontal boundary will lift widespread rainfall across the 
region, starting at the coast by the evening before spreading inland 
as the night progress. Snow levels will rise, ending the winter 
weather advisory for the Olympics but they are to stay low enough 
over higher Cascade passes. As a result, a winter weather advisory 
remains in effect for the North Cascades through Tuesday night. 
Surface gradients tighten as gusty SE winds is forecast for portions 
of the area. The strongest winds will be over the San Juan and 
Island counties and along the coast where the probability for gusts 
over 40 MPH exceed 80%. Wind advisories are in effect along the 
coast. Overnight lows range between the upper 30s to mid 40s.

The frontal system will round out of the region Tuesday evening
but lingering showers and breezy winds are forecast to remain.
It'll only clear the runway for yet another disturbance to track
into the region on Wednesday night into early Thursday with yet
another round of widespread rainfall and breezy winds. Snow 
levels look to be well above pass level by Wednesday evening as 
any precipitation that's slated to fall will be liquid. In 
addition to periods of widespread precipitation and gusty winds,
coastal flooding and high surf will also be of concern. High 
surf along the pacific in concert with high tides will combine 
to lead to hazardous conditions along the coastline this eek 
including both major coastal flooding and high surf. Conditions 
will likely peak on Tuesday and last into the latter half of the
week. Tides will also cause concern for inland coastlines 
starting Wednesday. A flurry of coastal flood advisories/warning
and high surf advisories are in effect. Temperatures throughout
the short-term will rise above average with highs in the lower 
to mid 50s and lows well into the 40s. 


&&

.LONG TERM /THURSDAY THROUGH MONDAY/...
Thursday's frontal system will push off and away by the evening
but it won't go away without the concern for additional impacts.
Models are signaling at yet another round of gusty winds and
periods of widespread rainfall possibly moderate to heavy at
times. High surf and coastal flood impacts will also peak on
Thursday. Active conditions will linger into Friday but guidance
is beginning to signal at a building upper-level ridge over the
weekend. Confidence lacks in its intensity and exact placement
but brief pattern change may be on the horizon. Temperatures
will return closer to average Friday onwards.

.AVIATION...
VFR all areas currently. However, the next system will bring more 
widespread rain this evening into early Tuesday. Visibility 
reductions down to MVFR are possible with the heaviest bands of 
rain. Mountains likely see lower ceiling obstructions. Southeast 
winds will continue, with most seeing 5-10 kt but isolated gusts up 
to 20 kt have been observed. Gusts may top out near 30 kt at KHQM 
late tonight into early Tuesday. Winds aloft will be stronger and 
out of the southwest, so there will be some potential for wind shear 
(especially Tuesday morning). Periods of rainfall likely into 
Tuesday with continued breezy winds. 

KSEA...Confidence is leaning towards mostly VFR ceilings throughout 
much of the upcoming period. However, the NBM is hinting at 30% 
chance of MVFR conditions are possible overnight into early Tuesday 
morning under heavier rainfall. Winds out of the south-southeast 8-
15 kt with gusts near 20 kt at times. Rainfall will begin at the 
terminal between 00z-06z Tuesday and last before breaking into 
showers later on Tuesday. 

&&

.MARINE...
A low pressure system will remain offshore for much of the week, 
with several fronts moving over area waters. The first frontal 
passage associated with this system is crossing the waters today, 
with a slew of headlines across the waters. A Storm Warning remains 
in effect over the outer coastal waters, with Gale Warnings in place 
for both the west and east entrances of the Strait of Juan de Fuca. 
Small Craft Advisories also remain in effect for the central Strait 
of Juan de Fuca, the northern inland waters, and for Grays Harbor 
Bar. Winds will remain elevated throughout the evening and overnight 
areas for most locations, with a brief lull over the central Strait 
before picking back up again Tuesday.

The system offshore will continue to bring elevated winds across 
area waters, with headlines likely to continue well into the week. 
Gusty winds will continue on Tuesday, and another frontal passage 
Wednesday into Thursday will bring in a round of potentially even 
stronger winds over area waters. This will likely bring additional 
Storm Warnings over the outer coastal waters, with potential for 
small crafts to gales for most other areas. As this system continues 
to influence the area, isolated thunderstorms remain possible each 
afternoon, mainly over the coastal waters.

High pressure is favored to build over the region by Friday, 
bringing a brief break from winds before additional systems bring 
widespread elevated winds by next week.

Seas around 10 to 15 feet this afternoon will continue to build to 
18 to 25 feet by Tuesday afternoon. Seas will ease slightly over the 
outer waters Wednesday and Thursday, though wave period will 
increase by a few seconds, bringing long period swells. Seas will 
gradually ease throughout the day Friday, falling below 10 feet 
overnight into Saturday. However, that is expected to be short-
lived, with seas building to 8 to 12 feet by the end of the weekend.



&&

.HYDROLOGY...
Wetter systems moving into the region through this 
week will start to bring some rises to area rivers. At this 
time, no widespread river flooding is expected, however the 
Skokomish River will likely reach flood stage around midweek and
topping into moderate, possibly near major given latest guidance.
A river flood watch has been issued for Mason County from
Wednesday morning through Sunday afternoon.
