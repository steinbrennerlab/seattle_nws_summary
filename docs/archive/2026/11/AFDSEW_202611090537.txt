.SYNOPSIS...
A brief break in the weather this afternoon with some showers
around. Multiple systems look to cross over the area through
Tuesday, bringing rounds of widespread lowland rain, gusty 
winds, and high elevation mountain snow. Active weather 
continues through the end of the week with additional systems 
passing into the area.

&&

.UPDATE...
We're in between disturbances here across western Washington as 
transient ridging builds overhead. Mostly dry conditions are favored 
tonight as mid-high level cloud coverage streams in ahead of the 
next frontal system. With abundant surface moisture from recent 
rainfall, areas of fog are set to develop with aid of light surface 
winds due to weak gradients. Overnight lows will range between the 
upper 30s to mid 40s.

&&

.SHORT TERM /TONIGHT THROUGH TUESDAY NIGHT/...
Recent radar imagery shows some scattered showers across the
Northern Interior and Cascades. Expect this trend to continue
throughout the evening hours, with a slight chance (10-20%) of 
thunderstorms north of Everett. An upper level ridge will move
through the area tonight, with drying overnight. Another frontal
system will start to move over the area by Monday morning, with
rain reaching the coast by the afternoon. Rain will slowly move
through the interior by the late afternoon and early evening
hours. Additionally, southerly winds will increase once again,
with widespread gusts of 20 to 30 mph possible at times
throughout much of the area (less strong than the previous days
wind). Areas along the coast and Whidbey Island could see gusts
up to 35 mph. High temperatures in the mid to upper 50s. 

Heading into Tuesday, a long fetch of moisture being aimed at
Vancouver Island slightly moves down through western Washington.
We'll see another round of widespread rain, with a bulk of the
QPF being aimed right at the coast and the Olympic Peninsula.
Southerly winds will increase in the afternoon and be slightly
stronger than Mondays, with widespread gusts of 35 to 45 mph
along the coast, and areas Whidbey Island northward. Rain and
wind will continue into Wednesday morning.

&&

.LONG TERM /WEDNESDAY THROUGH SUNDAY/...
Upper level troughing continues Wednesday into Thursday for some
scattered shower activity over the region, keeping conditions 
unsettled. Additionally, minor coastal flooding may be possible
on Wednesday morning due to high astronomical tides, primarily
along the shorelines of San Juans and Whatcom county. High 
temps warm up slightly on Wednesday, in the low 60s. 

Latest ensemble guidance shows an upper level low around BC that
is funneling a plume of moisture towards BC and western 
Washington Friday into Saturday. At this time, looks like active
weather will continue, with widespread rain and breezy winds 
into the weekend. We'll continue to monitor this system as it 
gets closer.

.AVIATION...
Generally VFR conditions with variable mid and high clouds over
the region tonight, though a few patches of fog have developed
right after sunset around KOLM and KCLM. Expect increasing
mid clouds to inhibit cooling a bit, but given recent rains this
may not be enough to preclude fog development or persistence in
areas where winds remain light. Any fog that develops likely
brings IFR conditions through early Monday morning. The next
front then approaches later Monday with heavier rain lowering
ceilings and visibility and gusty winds. Expect the line to
reach the coast around 20z and into the interior toward 22z.
Gusts to 25 kt can be expected as the system moves through.

KSEA...VFR conditions will persist through Monday morning at the
terminal. Light winds overnight, becoming easterly. Expect
stronger southeast winds to increase around 22z with gusts to 
20 kt and the return of rain brining likely MVFR conditions as 
the front approaches by 00z.

&&

.MARINE...
High pressure building back over the area this evening will 
bring a brief period of calmer winds to the waters through late 
tonight. The next frontal system will move across the coastal 
waters on Monday, with winds starting to increase across the
coastal waters again by early Monday morning and across the
interior waters by late morning. Winds will peak in the 
afternoon, before easing Monday night. Gale warnings are in 
effect for the Coastal Waters and the Eastern Strait, where 
probabilities continue to highlight roughly a 60-80% chance of 
gusts reaching 34 kt. Elsewhere, southerly winds will be breezy,
with winds gusting to small craft strength through the afternoon
and early evening. There will be a brief lull in winds on 
Monday night, before another system moves into the region on 
Tuesday and brings a stronger round of southerlies to the area 
waters and likely another round of widespread headlines. The 
weather will remain active through the end of the week as 
additional frontal systems move through the waters.

Seas between 19-24 ft this afternoon will gradually subside
towards 10-12 ft on Monday before the next system brings seas 
back to the 16-18 ft range on Tuesday. Seas will largely remain
above 13 ft through much of the week. The next best chance for 
seas to build to 20 ft will be by next weekend, with the GEFS 
highlighting a 30-40% chance of seas exceeding 20 ft off the
Washington coast.

.HYDROLOGY...
River flooding is not expected the next seven days. Multiple 
weather systems moving through the area in the next week. While 
current precipitation forecasts are not expected to cause 
flooding, this will saturate soils and raise base flows. If the 
atmospheric river stalls over the area late next week into next
weekend we might need to keep an eye on our old friend the 
Skokomish River. Felton
