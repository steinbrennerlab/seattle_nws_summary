.SYNOPSIS...
Dry conditions will continue across western Washington through
Monday as an upper level ridge moves over the Pacific Northwest.
The first half of the week will largely remain dry across the
area, with chances of precipitation increasing the second half
of the week as a series of systems approaches the Pacific 
Northwest. A brief break in the unsettled pattern is possible 
Friday night into Saturday as another ridge moves over the 
region.

&&

.SHORT TERM /TODAY THROUGH TUESDAY/...
An upper level ridge will move into the region today and push 
inland on Monday, maintaining dry conditions across western 
Washington for the start of the week. Overnight lows will remain
cool, mainly dipping down into the 30s, and will bring areas of
additional frost development during the early morning hours. Ample
moisture in the low levels will also promote another round of 
fog development across the western Washington on Monday morning.
Afternoon highs will be seasonable, mainly in the low to mid 
50s both days. 

A splitting system approaching the region Monday night into 
Tuesday may bring some light precipitation to portions of the 
coast and the Olympic Peninsula. The majority of western Washington
will remain dry, however, with temperatures warming a few degrees.
Morning lows will be in the mid 30s to mid 40s across the area,
with afternoon highs expected to be in the mid 50s to low 60s.

&&

.LONG TERM /TUESDAY NIGHT THROUGH SATURDAY/...
Chances for precipitation will increase the second half of the
week as a series of systems looks to approach the Pacific Northwest.
At this time, a weak system looks to move across the area on 
Wednesday, followed by a stronger system on Friday. The stronger
system on Friday will likely bring gusty winds and more widespread
lowland rain and mountain snow to the area. Discrepancy still 
exists with the pattern heading into next weekend, but ensemble
clusters hint at a brief break in the parade of systems Friday
night into early Saturday as a ridge of high pressure temporarily
moves back over the region. The ridge looks to push inland 
rather quickly however, which would open the door for additional
systems to move into the area by Saturday evening.

Temperatures will be near to just slightly above normal through
the period, with morning lows expected to be in the upper 30s 
to upper 40s and afternoon highs expected to be in the mid to 
upper 50s.

.MARINE...
High pressure rebuilds over the coastal waters today with dry 
conditions finishing the weekend over the waters. Couple patches of 
fog may affect portions of the Puget Sound waters this morning. A 
weak system will brush the coastal waters Monday into Tuesday with 
the chance of showers. While winds will pick up in the outer coastal 
waters (with winds gusting up to 20 kt), the main concern for the 
first part of the week will be seas increasing to 8-11 ft going into 
tonight/Monday. A small craft advisory was issued to cover the 
threat, which will likely continue into Tuesday with seas returning 
to 6-8 ft Wednesday.

The second half of the week is more active with trough dropping out 
of Alaska, and a couple of systems likely to affect the waters 
finishing the week into next weekend. Best chance of winds greater 
than 20 kt is with a system Wednesday/Thursday, Friday, and this 
weekend, affecting the coastal and interior waters. Seas will 
increase going towards the end of the week, peaking Friday with 
heights up to 15 to 17 ft.
