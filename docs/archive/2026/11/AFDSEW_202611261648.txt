.SYNOPSIS...
Post-frontal airmass today with rain showers, isolated 
thunderstorms, breezy winds, and coastal flooding. Precipitation 
chances will linger into Friday with a weaker system. A brief break 
in precipitation is expected this weekend as a weak ridge builds 
over the Pacific Northwest. An active weather pattern resumes early 
next week. 

&&

.SHORT TERM /TODAY THROUGH SATURDAY/...
Post-frontal showers will continue through the afternoon, with 
periods of dry weather amongst the showers. There is also a 
slight chance for thunderstorms today, especially along the 
Olympic Peninsula. Breezy southwest winds slowly ease this 
evening and tonight.

A much weaker system riding over the northern periphery of a
ridge to our south will spread some light rain back across the 
area by Friday afternoon, but QPF and wind will be much less of
a factor with this system. A low amplitude upper ridge will
shift onshore across the Pacific Northwest on Saturday for a
brief drying trend and a return to seasonal temperatures.

Coastal flooding will continue to be a concern today as
astronomical high tides combine with storm surge and high surf 
to produce some significant tidal anomalies and impacts to 
coastal communities. Coastal flooding impacts are expected to 
peak for the inland waters today before tides gradually weaken.

&&

.LONG TERM /SATURDAY NIGHT THROUGH WEDNESDAY/...
A weak upper ridge axis is expected to shift east of the area
Saturday night. Another upper trough is expected to move into 
the offshore waters on Sunday, but ensembles lack any 
significant cohesion with its evolution. It's worth noting that 
the 00Z operational run of the GFS spins up another sub 980 
millibar surface low offshore by Sunday night. Given the 
potential impacts to holiday travel, we'll have to keep an eye 
on this. 

Moving forward into the first half of next week, model
disagreements persist. There is some hint of another short-lived
dry break in the Wednesday timeframe. Current forecasts for the
period have near normal temps and a generic chance of rain. In 
other words, a near climo forecast for early December.

Chances for thunderstorms decrease into the evening hours. 

S/SE winds will remain elevated through the afternoon with gusts 20-

interior, and will gradually decrease into the evening hours to

.MARINE...
A strong frontal system will continue to cross over
the waters this morning, bringing strong winds and elevated 
seas to area waters. A Gale Warning remains in effect for all
coastal water zones, and all interior zones; the exception being
the East Entrance of the Strait of Juan De Fuca, where a Storm 
Warning continues. A Small Craft remains in the Central Strait 
of Juan De Fuca. Thunderstorms are also favored to develop over
the coastal waters early this morning and move inland towards 
the inner waters throughout the day, producing localized 
lightning, gusty winds, and small hail.

Winds will start to decrease this evening over the waters, with
a Small Craft Advisory out for the coastal waters, mainly for
continued elevated wind and seas. Conditions will start to calm
heading into Friday, with a weak system approaching the waters,
however impacts should remain minimal. High pressure is favored
to develop over the waters through the weekend, with seas and
winds to ease, the exception is the Strait of Juan De Fuca,
where a westerly push of winds is likely. 

Seas around 15 to 20 feet this morning will hover in this range
throughout the majority of the day, slowly decreasing this
evening into Friday morning around 10 to 15 feet. Seas will
lower even further by late Friday into Saturday, where we could
see seas get below 10 feet through the weekend. Seas are 
favored to rise above 10 feet into next week.

Mazurkiewicz

.HYDROLOGY...
A frontal system brought heavy rain to portions of the 
area over the last 24 hours, with rainfall amounts peaking across 
the Olympic Peninsula, ranging 3 to 6 inches. This led to rapid 
rivers on rivers off the Olympics. In particular, the Skokomish 
River rose into Moderate flood stage overnight, reaching near Major 
flood stage early this morning. River flooding is expected to 
continue on the Skokomish through tonight, before falling below 
flood stage on Friday. Additional rises are possible on the 
Skokomish River next week. Elsewhere, rivers will continue to rise 
today, but no additional river flooding is expected. JD
