.SYNOPSIS...
A front will move over Western Washington today. The front will
lift north tonight ending up in British Columbia Wednesday. A
cold front will stall offshore Wednesday into Thursday. What is
left of the front moving through Thursday night. Additional 
weather systems will move through Friday through Sunday. An 
upper level trough will move over the area Sunday night and Monday.

.SHORT TERM /TODAY THROUGH THURSDAY/...
Satellite imagery shows cloudy skies over Western Washington
early this morning. Doppler radar has rain out ahead of the next
system reached the coast around 3 AM/11Z. Temperatures were in 
the mid 40s to lower 50s. 

Juicy system moving into Western Washington today. IVT values in
the 800-1000 kg/m/s range making for a rainy day across the
area. Southwesterly winds aloft will create the usual rain
shadow northeast of the Olympics but with the air mass so moist
even that area will fill in with rain later today. In addition 
to the rain, winds will be increasing later this morning into 
the afternoon hours. Model 925 mb winds 40-50 knots along the 
coast and just slightly weaker over the Northwest Interior 
beginning around 21Z. These winds will mix down towards the 
surface at times. The wind advisory for the coastal beaches and 
Northwest Interior beginning at 1 PM will remain in effect for
gusts as high as 50 mph. The combination of winds, waves and 
lower surface pressure around the time of high tides will 
produce some minor coastal flooding along the ocean beaches. 
Inundation of 1.5 to 2 feet above ground levels is possible 
between 10 AM and 3 PM. Highs today in the mid 50s to lower 60s.

Front hanging over Western Washington this evening before 
lifting north overnight with rain chances becoming confined to 
west of Puget Sound and the Northwest Interior by early 
Wednesday morning. Front still close enough to keep windy 
conditions going along the coast and in the Northwest Interior. 
With the cloud cover and southerly winds it is going to be a 
mild night with lows in the lower to mid 50s. Record high 
minimums are possible. 

Little change in the pattern Wednesday with the front to the
north and a cold front stalled offshore. Rain along the coast
and over the Olympics continuing with cloudy skies over most of
the remainder of the area. Breezy southerly winds continuing
throughout the day warming daytime temperatures into the 60s.
Record highs are also possible. 

Cold front remaining stalled offshore Wednesday night but not
far enough off the coast to keep rain away for the coastline.
Winds easing Wednesday evening with surface gradients relaxing 
and winds aloft decreasing. Plenty of cloud cover over the area
making for another mild night with lows near 50. 

Stalled cold front becoming more north south oriented Thursday
as an upper level trough behind the front digs south offshore.
Front will still be close enough to give locations west of Puget
Sound more rain. Mild temperatures continuing with highs in the 
upper 50s to mid 60s. Felton

.LONG TERM /THURSDAY NIGHT THROUGH MONDAY/...
Models in good agreement with the stalled front getting kicked
inland Thursday night as the flow aloft consolidates over the 
Eastern Pacific. Earlier model solutions indicated more of an 
atmospheric river pattern with the river getting stuck over the 
area Friday and Saturday. Model solutions are still wet for the
Friday through the weekend but instead of an atmospheric river 
the solutions now favor a pair of wet and windy frontal 
systems Friday and again Saturday night and Sunday. Upper level
trough trailing the front for Sunday night and Monday. Snow 
levels remaining above pass levels until the upper level trough 
arrives Sunday night lowering the snow level down to the 3500 to
4000 foot range. Felton

.MARINE...
Winds will continue to increase over the area waters through this 
morning and afternoon, peaking this evening and persisting through 
the overnight hours tonight into early Wednesday. Winds will reach 
up to gale force across the coastal waters and the East Strait of 
Juan de Fuca, with Small Craft Advisories from Admiralty Inlet 
through the Northern Inland Waters as well.  Winds will start to 
very slowly ease throughout the day Wednesday, with winds finally 
easing below advisory thresholds area-wide by Wednesday evening as 
the stalled front offshore causing these winds falls apart.

Seas around 9 to 10 ft will rise this morning to 16 to 19 ft this 
afternoon and evening both due to the stronger winds and another 
westerly swell group arriving. Seas will remain high through 
Wednesday morning before slowly easing throughout the day Thursday, 
through remaining above 11-13 ft.

The next stronger low pressure system begins to move into the 
coastal waters and increase the area winds late Thursday night into 
Friday. Winds look to peak Friday morning, with guidance still 
showing 60-75% chances for storm force wind gusts over the coastal 
waters. Winds look to ease slightly Friday night before another wave 
brings another round of strong winds on Saturday (40-55% chc of 
storm force gusts) and into Saturday night. Winds look to finally 
ease below thresholds late Sunday as the low broadens and weakens as 
it moves onshore.

Guidance has shifted the largest waves slightly farther south 
towards southern Washington and Oregon, but remains very confident 
(60-80%) in seas building above 20 ft on Friday, with GEFS and ECMWF 
probabilities showing 25-35% chances of seas above 25 ft. Slight 
shifts in the low tracks will determine where the dynamic fetch 
effects will be maximized from these systems, with seas nearing or 
exceeding 30 ft still not out of the equation, especially towards 
the southern portions of the waters.

.HYDROLOGY...
A flood watch has been issued for Mason county for the
possibility of the Skokomish River flooding with this next round
of rain. The buffer from the dry October is gone with the river
only dropping to around a foot and a half below flood stage
after this recent rain event. As mentioned earlier this is a
juicy system moving into the area today. Model 850 mb winds 40 
to 50 knots out of the southwest creating good orographics over
the south slopes of the Olympics. Snow levels will also be 
high, around 9000 feet. Forecasted rain amounts for the 24 
hours between 4 AM today and 4 AM Wednesday along the south 
slopes 2.5 to 4 inches. With the elevated snow levels and the 
river running high already there is a good chance the Skokomish 
river will reach flood stage this evening or in the early 
morning hours Wednesday. Another round of rainfall with the 
system Friday could also push the Skokomish back above flood 
stage. 

River flooding not expected on the other rivers in the area in
the next 7 days. Felton
