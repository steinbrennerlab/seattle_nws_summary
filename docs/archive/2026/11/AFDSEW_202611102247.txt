.SYNOPSIS...A warm front will lift north into British Columbia
tonight, while a cold front stalls offshore Wednesday and into
Thursday. Additional weather systems will move through Friday 
through Sunday for more lowland rain and gusty winds. After a
brief break Monday, another front will arrive on Tuesday. 

&&

.SHORT TERM /TONIGHT THROUGH THURSDAY NIGHT/...Western WA
remains under strong and moist SW flow with rain and high
elevation snow across the region. The warm front will lift north
into B.C tonight and the interior will see a break in the
precipitation. Wetter conditions will remain along the coast and
Olympics through Wednesday as the cold front stalls offshore.
Wednesday will be mild in the warm sector with temperatures in
the 60s. 

Several weather headlines are in place with the main impact 
being strong winds along the coast and North Interior. Expect 
south winds of 20 to 30 MPH with gusts to 50 MPH. Winds will 
ease Wednesday afternoon. In addition, there's potential for 
large waves crashing along the coast leading to a High Surf 
Advisory. Minor coastal flooding is also possible near Wednesday
morning's high tide in the San Juans and western Whatcom County.
Lastly, due to heavy precipitation, the Skokomish River in Mason
County is seeing sharp rises with Minor flooding possible. 

The cold front will continue to stall and weaken offshore moving
into Thursday. The coast and Olympics may see periods of light
rain but the interior and Cascades will see a brief break.
There's more active weather starting Thursday night. 33

&&

.LONG TERM /FRIDAY THROUGH TUESDAY/...From Thursday night into
Friday, there's another strong Pacific storm system moving in
for more lowland rain, high elevation mountain snow, and gusty
winds. Winds will be the strongest along the coast where the 
probability of seeing gusts of 50 MPH is 70-80 percent. It will
be windy in the interior as well with gusts to 30-40 MPH. This
system will weaken as it moves inland on Saturday. We will then
see a second system track through on Sunday for more wind and
rain. Additional rainfall will keep the Skokomish River running
high with the possibility of river flooding. These systems will
generate large waves with the potential for High Surf and beach
erosion along the Pacific coast. Minor coastal flooding will 
also need to monitored around the time of high tides across the
Salish Sea.

Moving forward, there appears to be a brief break in the 
pattern on Monday. By Tuesday we see more breezy winds and rain 
as the next front moves in. 33

.AVIATION...A mixed bag of ceilings this afternoon as ongoing 
showers are keeping conditions messy through the interior. Most 
areas are seeing MVFR/IFR in showers along with low ceilings and
low visibilities (3-6SM). Ceilings should slowly, and gradually
improve towards low-end VFR late this evening as rain begins to
dissipate. S/SE winds will remain breezy this afternoon, with 
gusts up to 20 to 25 kts possible. Areas along the coast and 
especially terminals in the north interior (KBLI) could see 
gusts up to 30 kts tonight. Winds will remain elevated Wednesday
morning, easing by the afternoon. Ceilings should remain VFR on
Wednesday morning. 

KSEA...MVFR/IFR conditions this afternoon in showers. Low

the early evening hours, before conditions start to ease up into

.MARINE...Winds will continue to increase over the area waters 
this afternoon, peaking this evening and remaining elevated 
throughout the overnight hours. A Gale Warning remains in effect
for the coastal waters until Wednesday evening as these wind 
speeds persist. A Gale Warning is also in effect for the East 
Strait of Juan De Fuca, along with the Northern Inland Waters. 
Elsewhere, A Small Craft Advisory will continue for Admiralty 
Inlet and the West Entrance of the Strait until Wednesday 
evening when winds will start to ease across the whole region. 

Seas this afternoon generally around 16 to 18 feet this
afternoon, remaining high through Wednesday morning. Seas will
ease towards more of a 12 to 14 feet on Thursday.

Another strong low pressure system will start to move towards
the coastal waters late Thursday night into Friday, bringing
stronger winds and higher waves. Latest guidance still suggests
a 60-70% chance of storm force wind gusts over the coastal 
waters. Winds may ease slightly Friday night before another
round of strong winds arrives on the coastal waters, with a
30-40% chance of storm force gusts over the waters. Winds will
slowly ease late night Sunday as these systems move away from 
the area.

Additionally, guidance suggests building large waves over the 
coast, generally above 20 feet starting on Friday afternoon.
GEFS and ECMWF probabilities continuously has shown a 20-30%
chance of seas above 25 feet heading into late Friday into early
Saturday morning. Subtle shifts in the low pressure may change
waves and where the largest waves generate, with seas nearing 30
ft not out of the equation, especially near the southern 
portions of the waters. 

Mazurkiewicz
