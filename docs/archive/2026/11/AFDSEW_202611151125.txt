.SYNOPSIS...
Cold front has moved east of the Cascades early this morning. 
Cool upper level trough remaining offshore through Monday before
moving inland Monday night. Next system moving southeast 
Tuesday will move inland Wednesday south of the area. Short 
blocked upper level pattern Wednesday night through Friday 
giving Western Washington a dry spell. Next system weakening as 
it approaches Saturday.

&&

.SHORT TERM /TODAY THROUGH TUESDAY/...
Satellite imagery and surface observations show cold front has
moved east of the Cascades early this morning. Doppler radar has
a line of post frontal showers between the coast and Puget Sound
at 3 AM/11Z. Temperatures even with the frontal passage were 
still in the upper 40s to mid 50s. 

Band moving through early this morning will be the most
organized shower activity of the day. Satellite imagery shows
plenty of cold cu off the coast but the parent low associated
with the trough is moving north northwest towards the Gulf of
Alaska. This will pull the coldest post frontal air northwest of
the area through Monday. Air mass somewhat unstable so showers 
remaining in the forecast with the highest pops along the coast.
Snow levels near 5000 feet only lowering to 3500 to 4000 feet by
Monday. A few inches of new snow in the higher elevations today
but by the time the snow levels drop down to the pass levels
new snow accumulations will be light. High temperatures today, 
in the lower to mid 50s, could occur this morning with little 
change in the temperatures throughout the day. A little cooler 
Monday with highs a couple of degrees either side of 50. Lows 
tonight in the mid 30s to lower 40s. 

A coastal flood advisory remains in effect for the Northern
Inland Waters this morning between 8 am and noon for possible
minor coastal flooding with the combination of high tides, 
lower surface pressure and winds. 

Current wave forecasts are a little lower than previous
forecasts for later today into Monday morning. Have cancelled 
the high surf warning for the beaches beginning at 10 am this 
morning and extended the high surf advisory through Monday 
morning. The beaches will still be a dangerous place to be even 
with an advisory. Still could see waves in the 20 foot range 
into Monday morning. 

Upper level trough moving through the area Monday night keeping
a chance of showers in the forecast. Snow levels 3500 to 4000
feet but not much in the way of moisture with the trough
passage. Lows in the lower 30s to lower 40s. 

Split flow aloft Tuesday with the next system digging south well
off the coast giving Western Washington a dry day. Light surface
gradients in the morning could produce areas of low clouds or
fog. Even with some afternoon sunshine highs remaining near 50. 

&&

.LONG TERM /TUESDAY NIGHT THROUGH SATURDAY/...
Models in good agreement with the mid week system moving inland
south of the area. The Southwest Interior could get brushed by 
the northern portion of the system Wednesday. Short lived upper 
level blocking pattern setting up Wednesday night through Friday
with an upper level ridge to the north and an upper level low 
to the south keeping Western Washington dry. If the models light
surface gradient scenario works out there could be areas of fog
Thursday and Friday morning.

Model solutions showing more variability Saturday as the next
system develops off the coast. No real clear winner in the
ensembles so for now will go with a broadbrush chance of rain. 

Highs staying around 50 through the period. Lows in the 30s and
lower 40s cooling to the upper 20s and 30s Thursday through
Saturday.

The first two weeks of November have been quite mild. For
Seattle the average temperature of 51.6 is the 5th warmest in 
82 years of records at Seattle-Tacoma airport. Saturday was the 
6th day of the month with a high in the 60s. Only three 
Novembers have had more 60 degree plus days, 2016 with 9 days, 
1976 with 8 days and 1949 with 7 days. With the 3 am temperature
of 55 degrees this makes the 13th day in a row with a high of 
at least 55 degrees in Seattle. This is the longest streak of 
its kind. The previous longest, two 12 days streaks November 
1-12, 2016 and November 3-14, 1949. Felton

.MARINE...
A vigorous front has pushed east of the waters but elevated winds 
and seas will remain today. The previous Storm Force warning for the 
coastal waters has expired. Any lingering Gale Warnings will be 
allowed to expire later this morning but SCAs will remain throughout 
the day for both inner and outer waters. Winds will gradually ease 
over the waters and later tonight. Waves 15-18 ft this morning will 
rise to 20-25 ft this afternoon with the highest waves over the 
offshore waters. High pressure is still on track to build across the 
area on Monday allowing for a brief reprieve. Winds are to be 
generally benign as seas lower to 10-12 ft, still SCA for the 
coastal waters but much lower than we've seen recently. 

A low pressure system will form over the open northeast Pacific and 
move off south and away from WA's coastal waters Tuesday into 
Wednesday before tracking into CA later on Wednesday. While somewhat 
distant, this system will allow winds to turn offshore and increase, 
as well as increasing waves for the next round of headlines mid-
week. Calmer conditions under high pressure are then favored 
Thursday into the end of the week.

.HYDROLOGY...
The post frontal band of showers is still producing significant
rain amounts over the south slopes of the Olympics. In the 3 
hour period between midnight and 3 AM rainfall totals in this 
area have been in the 0.30 to 0.60 inch range. Heavier showers 
will be coming to an end this morning. The Skokomish River near 
Potlatch is three tenths of a foot below flood stage right now. 
This last shot of rain will push the river just over flood stage
later this morning. A flood warning is already out and will 
continue for the Skokomish. The river will recede back below 
flood stage late today or early Monday. The river will remain 
below flood stage for the remainder of the week. 

River flooding not expected on the remainder of the rivers in
the area for the next 7 days. Felton
