.SYNOPSIS...A series of frontal systems will bring a return of 
widespread rain and occasionally windy conditions today through 
the weekend. A trend toward drier and cooler conditions are 
expected toward the middle portion of next week as the storm 
track shifts south of Western Washington. 

&&

.SHORT TERM /TODAY THROUGH SUNDAY/...Based on area tide gauges,
we're seeing minor to moderate coastal flooding with high tide 
this morning. Pressure is a little higher on Saturday but will 
evaluate if more headlines are needed. 33

Previous discussion...Rain is spreading onto the coast this 
morning ahead of the first of a couple frontal systems that will
impact the region in the days ahead. Along with the rain, 
breezy to locally windy conditions will develop along the coast,
the North Interior, and near the Cascade gaps. A combination of
high tide and lower atmospheric pressure will contribute to 
minor to moderate coastal flooding today as well. Larger seas 
building to around 20 feet will reach the coast where a high 
surf advisory remains in effect. Rain will turn to showers by 
tonight as an upper trough axis shifts onshore. 

A second and stronger frontal system will move into the area
later Saturday into Saturday night. QPF remains in the same
ballpark as previous estimates with around 1.5 to 2 inches for
much of the coast, 3+ inches on the south facing slopes of the
Olympics, and somewhere between 0.50 and 1 inch for much of the
interior lowlands. NBM probabilities of wind gusts exceeding 50
mph along portions of the coast are around 60 to 80 percent for
Saturday afternoon/evening with another bullseye of 50 to 60
percent probabilities near the east entrance to the strait later
Saturday night. Elsewhere, peak wind gusts Saturday night will
be roughly 30 to 40 mph. Post-frontal showers continue into
Sunday with winds easing. 

&&

.LONG TERM /SUNDAY NIGHT THROUGH THURSDAY/...An upper trough 
will move into the area Sunday night and Monday and introduce a 
notably cooler air mass from what we've seen recently. QPF will 
be light by this time, but snow levels will dip to around 4000 
feet. Models are continuing the trend of the next system toward 
the middle of next week taking a more southern track and just 
clipping the far southern portion of the CWA. Beyond that, upper
ridging is on track to bring a period of drier weather to round
out the remainder of the week. And this time of year, that 
probably means some fog and cool temperatures.

.MARINE...SCAs will remain for the East Strait, Admiralty Inlet
and Grays Harbor Bar well into tonight. A SCA has been issued 
for the West Strait as well for the remainder of the morning 
before dropping off this afternoon due to ESE winds reaching 
criteria. Gales gusts are firmly in place across the coastal 
waters as an occasional storm force gust cannot be ruled out, 
particularly over the outer coastal waters. SCA winds look to 
potentially pick up later tonight into Saturday morning for the 
Northern Inland waters 

Winds over the inner waters will remain elevated into Saturday 
morning, winds over the coastal waters will briefly decrease as 
gales transition to more Small Craft strength. However, the next 
frontal system is set to arrive late Saturday afternoon as a sub 970 
mb low generates very strong southerly winds. REFS probabilities are 
now showing greater than 90% chance of Storm Force gusts being met 
over the outer and inner coastal waters while the NBM is showing 50-
60% into Saturday night. For this reason a Storm Force Watch has 
been issued for the Coastal Waters, with additional Gale Watches 
issued for portions of the interior waters. Small Craft winds are 
favored to continue on Sunday, at least over the coastal waters and 
portions of the inland waters Conditions are favored to calm by 
Monday, with potential for continued windy conditions over the 
waters returning by Tuesday.

Today's system will bring very large seas to the Washington coast, 
with high confidence in waves building to 20-26 ft over the coastal 
waters by this afternoon. These large waves will also be very 
energetic, with a dominant period of 18 to 20 seconds. Seas will 
remain elevated through Saturday morning and will briefly ease a bit 
into early Sunday, remaining between 14 to 18 feet. Another system 
will send another round of large, long period waves on Sunday with 
another round of waves likely 20-22 ft or higher through Monday 
morning. Seas look to ease a bit after Monday, but will likely stay 
in the 12-15 foot range heading into the middle of next week.
