.SYNOPSIS...Dry conditions continue into Wednesday. A frontal
system will bring a brief period of lowland rain late Wednesday
into Thursday. A stronger front on Friday brings gusty winds and
additional precipitation. A cold front moves through over the 
weekend, with additional systems next week for a continued 
active pattern.

&&

.SHORT TERM /TODAY THROUGH THURSDAY NIGHT/...Dry conditions for
most areas this afternoon with a mix of clouds and sunshine. A
few showers are moving northwards towards Mount Rainier this
afternoon with an upper low well south of the area in
California. These showers will dissipate into this evening, with
dry conditions into Wednesday AM. A weak frontal system moves
across the region Wednesday evening into Thursday bringing
lowland rain, and locally breezy winds around Whidbey Island
northwards. Mainly dry conditions returns for lowland areas for
Thursday afternoon with lingering rain or drizzle in the
mountains. Highs in the mid to upper 50s through Thursday.

&&

.LONG TERM /FRIDAY THROUGH MONDAY/...A more active weather
pattern begins Friday and continues into next week as multiple
frontal systems move across Western Washington. The first front
arrives Friday bringing lowland rain and gusty winds. South
winds will be strongest along the immediate coast and from
Admiralty Inlet northwards. NBM probabilities of gusts 
exceeding 40 MPH on Friday range 40 to 80% in these areas, and 
will need to monitor the potential for any wind headlines on 
Friday. A warm front then lifts northwards on Saturday, 
resulting in continued rain at times for the Olympic Peninsula 
and near BC, with a brief period of drier weather for some of 
Puget Sound. The associated cold front then slides across the 
region Saturday night into Sunday for the return of additional 
rain and breezy winds. Due to continued foliage on the trees, 
localized power outages may be possible during this period. Snow
levels remain higher Friday into Sunday, mainly 5000 to 8000 
feet, for high elevation mountain snowfall. Furthermore, seas 
rise towards 15 to 20 feet Friday, and will need to monitor high
surf concerns along the coast.

Ensembles suggest there may be a brief break in the heavier
precipitation Sunday night into Monday, before another front
moves into the Pacific Northwest late Monday. The active pattern
then continues next week resulting in further lowland rain,
mountain snow, gusty winds, and increased waves. Continue to
monitor the forecast for additional updates. JD

.AVIATION...VFR conditions persist across western Washington 
this evening as broken mid to high level clouds continue to move
across the area. Cloud cover should continue to decrease in 
coverage this evening and overnight, which will open up the door
for areas of low stratus and fog to develop overnight as winds 
remain light. Confidence is highest in southwest Washington 
(OLM, PWT) but other sheltered terminals (PAE, BLI) could also 
see some periods of fog. The biggest uncertainty will be SEA and
the metro area terminals, with a 25-30% chc of seeing LIFR 
conditions. Fog should clear out succinctly around 18Z as 
southerly winds begin to increase due to an approaching frontal 
system. MVFR to IFR cigs as well as rain begin to move onshore 
along the coast by 00Z Thur.

KSEA...VFR conditions through most of the TAF period. High cloud 
coverage will decrease this evening, opening the door up to a brief 
period of low stratus/fog development. Chances are highest (25-30% 
chc) between 13-18Z Thur for a brief period of IFR to LIFR 
conditions. N/NW winds 5-7 kt decrease to 5 kt or less overnight. 
Winds switch to S overnight, between 06-12Z Thur, increasing back to

.MARINE...Conditions remain relatively benign today as the area
remains under relatively higher pressure with a front 
approaching from well offshore. Seas in the outermost waters may
still be hovering near 10 ft but are slowly easing and should 
remain around 6-8 ft through tonight. A Small Craft Advisory 
remains in effect through 4 PM. 

South/southwesterly winds will start to increase over the coastal 
waters early Wednesday morning as the front approaches the area. 
Small Craft Advisories begin across outer coastal waters first, then 
including the inner coastal waters by Wednesday afternoon as winds 
increase. Southerly winds will also increase Wednesday afternoon 
through the interior Salish Sea from the central Puget Sound up 
through the southern Strait of Georgia, but confidence is high in 
winds remaining below advisory thresholds. The front moves through 
Wednesday evening and winds slowly ease into early Thursday. In 
addition to winds, seas build back to 10-12 ft late Wednesday and 
continue into Thursday.

Conditions become more active Thursday night into Friday as another 
stronger frontal system traverses the area waters. Winds increase 
area-wide overnight, with 60-80% chances for gale force wind gusts 
across portions of the coastal waters and the East Strait of Juan de 
Fuca through Friday morning. Winds look to slowly weaken during the 
day Friday. Seas will also begin to increase Thursday night, in 
conjunction with the winds but also as a large, long-period swell 
group arrives to the coastal waters. Seas will most likely rise to 
around 15-18 ft, through ensemble guidance shows a 25-40% chance of 
seas exceeding 20 ft. Seas look to maximize by mid-day Friday before 
easing Friday night.

The next frontal system, however, will be on its heels with winds 
increasing once again on Saturday, with higher (50-70%) chances for 
gale force winds across the coastal waters through early Sunday 
morning. Winds remain elevated overnight before easing on Sunday. 
Seas rise once again late Saturday up to 16-20 ft during the day 
Sunday. Seas ease slightly Sunday night.

Conditions look to remain hazardous going into next week.
Additional frontal systems move near or through the area waters 
almost every day, bringing additional periods of strong to gale-
force winds. Deep low centers over the Gulf of Alaska will continue 
to generate and send several series of large, longer- period waves 
to the coast, keeping seas at least above 12 ft.

.HYDROLOGY...River flooding is not expected through the next 
seven days. However, a series of increasingly wet weather 
systems will bring rain (and high mountain snow) to the region,
especially from Friday onward. While current forecasts are not 
expected to cause flooding, this will begin to saturate soils 
and raise base flows that could bring a concern with subsequent 
storms systems.
