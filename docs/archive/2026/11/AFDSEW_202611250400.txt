.SYNOPSIS...
An active weather pattern will persist into the end of week as a 
series of disturbances moves through the area. This, coupled with 
high astronomical tides, will continue the threat for coastal 
flooding through the end of the week. Rain, strong winds and high 
elevation snow will remain present in the forecast. 

&&

.SHORT TERM /TONIGHT THROUGH THURSDAY NIGHT/...
Latest radar imagery shows rainfall mainly focused over the Olympic 
Peninsula and along the east side of the Sound and moving north to 
northeastward. This is allowing some portions of Thurston, Lewis and 
Pierce county to see generally dry conditions with only a scattered 
cell here or there. Radar estimated rainfall rates are mostly around 
0.10 in/hr...although isolated spots east of the Sound could see 
rates increase slightly to 0.25 in/hr while SW aspects of the 
Olympics could see rates getting up to 0.50 in/hr. With Bellingham 
and Sea-Tac seeing record highs today /only breaking previous 
records by a degree though/, it does lend support to warmer air in 
place, keeping the main concern from this system being rainfall as 
snow levels remain elevated.

Inherited forecast and headlines look good and remain in line with 
most recent model output. That said, no evening update is necessary. 
For additional forecast details, please refer to the Previous 
Discussion Section below and its highlighted breakdown of each 
hazard.   18

From Previous Discussion...This section will be organized by hazard 
through the short term period ending Thursday night. 

Coastal Flooding: 
Another round of high astronomical tides will be arriving on
Thursday. Another frontal system will be moving inland
concurrently, for widespread coastal flooding impacts in
waterfront locations. There is potential for major flooding at
Friday Harbor, Westport, and Cherry Point, with moderate
flooding possible for the rest of the tide gauge locations.
Significant impacts have been reported along the Pacific coast
as a result of coastal flooding today so be sure to avoid
walking on jetties or the immediate coast. The waves are capable
of carrying large rocks and logs onshore. The coastal flood
threat will ease slightly on Friday. 

Wind: 
Winds will remain elevated through this evening as the front
finally makes its way onshore. While winds will ease in Puget
Sound overnight, they will remain gusty along the coast and in
through the Northern Inland waters of the Salish Sea. The next
round of wind, prompting another round of widespread wind
advisories, will arrive ahead of the front late Wednesday night
into Thursday. The wind direction will be more southerly as
opposed to the east to southeasterly winds experienced today.
The winds will remain elevated throughout much of the day on
Thursday which could make holiday travel more difficult,
particularly in along the I-5 corridor. Allow extra time to
arrive at your destination. 

Snow: A Winter Storm Watch has been issued for the North
Cascades including Washington Pass late Wednesday night into
Thursday. As much as 15 inches of snow is possible at the pass
level and areas above 5,000 feet. 

Rain will continue to move onshore tonight and into the
overnight hours as the front moves inland. Some of the bands of
rain may be heavy at times. Additional rainfall is expected with
the next system arriving Wednesday night into Thursday. There is
also a slight chance of thunderstorms in the area on Thursday in
the post-frontal airmass.

.LONG TERM /FRIDAY THROUGH TUESDAY/...
Coastal flooding begins to wane a bit on Friday, but the
indication is that low pressure over the weekend could bring the
threat back per the extended model guidance. There is a brief
precipitation break on Saturday for most of the area but
additional systems are forecast Sunday and beyond as low
pressure returns offshore. This will keep conditions active into
early December.

.AVIATION...
Showers continue across western WA with troughing over the region. 
The flow aloft is S to SW. Cigs are VFR and will remain as such 
through the bulk of the TAF period. Usually lower terminals may drop 
to MVFR overnight, but the general consensus is on VFR conditions 
continuing. Surface winds will ease slightly overnight, becoming 
more southerly into the rest of the day Wednesday. The next front 
arrives Wednesday night into Thursday. Gusts up to 40-45 kts are 
expected when that next system arrives through Puget Sound. Slightly 
higher gusts up to 45-50 kts is likely on the coast Wednesday night 
into Thursday. Expect periods of rain showers throughout the TAF. 

KSEA...VFR cigs continue throughout the TAF period with on and off 
rain showers expected throughout the evening and overnight period. 
The next period to watch will be late Wednesday night into Thursday 
as the next front arrives. The winds in that system will be more 
southerly but have potential to gust up to 35-40 kts.

.MARINE...
A low pressure system will continue to bring strong winds and 
elevated seas to area waters, with a Storm Warning remaining in 
effect over the coastal waters, Gale Warnings over both the West and 
East Strait of Juan de Fuca and Northern Inland waters, and Small 
Craft Advisories elsewhere. Wind gusts have been somewhat 
intermittent, but are expected to continue overnight and into 
Wednesday morning or most areas at Small Craft to Gale strength. The 
exception will be the Central Strait, where south to southeast winds 
will experience a lull overnight.

Another frontal passage Wednesday into Thursday will bring another 
round of strong winds across the region, with Storm Force winds 
slated to return across the coastal waters by Wednesday afternoon 
and a handful of headlines elsewhere across the inland waters 
through Thursday. This frontal system is expected to bring slightly 
stronger winds than today. In addition, as this system continues to 
influence the area, isolated thunderstorms remain possible each 
afternoon and evening through Thursday, mainly over the coastal 
waters along the coast.

High pressure is favored to build over the region by Friday, 
bringing a brief break from winds before additional systems bring 
widespread elevated winds by next week.

Seas around 15 to 25 feet will remain elevated through midday 
Wednesday before gradually easing to 10 to 15 feet by Thursday 
afternoon. Seas will continue to lower, reaching 6 to 9 feet on 
Saturday and Sunday. Seas are expected to build above 10 feet going 
into Monday.

.HYDROLOGY...
Ongoing rain will bring an additional 2 to 5 inches of rainfall to 
the region, with the highest rainfall totals over the Olympic 
Mountains. While no widespread river flooding is expected, the 
Skokomish River is still on track to reach flood stage mid week, 
topping out into moderate. A river flood watch remains in effect for 
Mason County from Wednesday morning through Sunday afternoon.
