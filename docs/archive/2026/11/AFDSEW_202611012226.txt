.SYNOPSIS...
High pressure overhead will maintain dry conditions, cloudy 
skies, and areas of patchy fog through Monday morning. A series
of splitting systems will bring slight chances of rain to 
western Washington Monday and Tuesday ahead of a series of 
systems that will bring increasingly more lowland rain, mountain
snow, and breezy winds through the end of next week.

&&

.SHORT TERM /TONIGHT THROUGH TUESDAY NIGHT/...
Low stratus continues to remain firmly entrenched over the
interior lowlands this afternoon, with some patches of sun
breaking through the cloud deck. However, any sun breaks will be
short lived with clouds filling back in this evening aided by
high pressure aloft and light winds. Residual moisture from 
recent rains will also result in patchy fog early Monday 
morning, which may pose localized visibility limitations during 
the morning commute for fog-prone areas. However, light 
easterly winds in the early morning may help to reduce fog 
formation under the stratus. As the upper level ridge axis
nudges eastward on Monday, light rain showers offshore will
slide inland Monday evening, though rainfall amounts will be
very light. As a cutoff low develops to the south Monday night
into Tuesday and weak ridging develops offshore, moisture will 
be nudged out of the area, allowing warmer and drier conditions
to briefly settle in for most areas by Tuesday afternoon.
However, sunny skies may be few and far between through Tuesday,
with temperatures near or a few degrees above normal.

&&

.LONG TERM /WEDNESDAY THROUGH SUNDAY/...
Ensembles continue to show good agreement over a series of
increasingly stronger systems passing over the region as the
rest of the week goes on. A frontal system on Wednesday is
expected to bring a round of widespread rain and high elevation
snow to western Washington, with the bulk of the moisture
focused over the Olympic Peninsula and North Cascades. Areas
east of the Olympic Mountains will likely see a high degree of
rain shadowing with this system, resulting in only a few
hundredths of rain accumulation. 

Another weather system will follow closely behind, and will be 
stronger than Wednesday's system. While shadowing will occur
once again east of the Olympics, higher elevations will see
roughly half of an inch to an inch of liquid while the lowlands
see a tenth or two of rain accumulation into Friday morning.
Snow levels will hover around 5000 feet as this system passes
through, resulting in a few inches of additional snow
accumulation over higher terrain.

Forecast confidence decreases into next weekend, depending on
how far northward an upper level ridge will build over the
western US. The ridge could either keep conditions warm or dry,
or remain too weak to ward off an incoming atmospheric river
system. While the bulk of the moisture of this system looks
like it might make landfall over British Columbia, western
Washington may see some of the heaviest rain so far this fall
with up to 2 to 3 inches of liquid over the mountains and
generally a half inch to an inch over the lowlands. The vigorous
system could also bring widespread breezy winds alongside the 
precipitation if the storm takes aim at the Pacific Northwest,
and waves could inch towards High Surf criteria along the
immediate coastline.

Beyond next weekend, longer range models continue to show
potential for high winds, heavy rain, and heavy snow across the
Pacific Northwest during the second week of November. While 
conditions remain highly uncertain at this time, potential 
impacts will continue to be monitored as we settle into the 
month of November.

.MARINE...
High pressure will continue building over the waters through Monday. 
A weather system will brush the coastal waters Monday morning, 
leading to an increase in southerly surface winds. Winds gusts may 
briefly approach 21 kt at times over zones PZZ170 and PZZ173 through 
the afternoon hours. High pressure will move inland on Tuesday ahead 
of the next system that arrives on Wednesday. Southerly winds will 
increase over the coastal waters on Wednesday with a 55-80% chance 
of small craft winds. A stronger system will arrive on Friday, 
bringing stronger winds across the coastal waters and the interior 
waters. Latest guidance suggests a 70% chance or higher for small 
craft winds and a 25-40% chance of gales. Another system expected on 
Saturday with the potential (50-75% chance) for small craft winds 
over the coastal waters and interior waters.  

Seas around 3-5 feet today will continue building to 8-11 feet by 
Monday morning, for which a Small Craft Advisory has been issued 
through early Tuesday morning. It is likely that Small Craft 
Advisory may have to be extended for the outer coastal waters, as 
seas around 10 feet look to linger through late Monday morning. Seas 
will decrease below 10 ft late Tuesday, before increasing above 10 
feet on Thursday. Seas continue to trend higher with the system 
Friday, with latest guidance now suggesting seas around 15-20 feet.
