.SYNOPSIS...After today, a pattern change will bring more fall-
like weather to the area with periods of rain, high elevation 
mountain snow and gusty winds. Active weather will continue
as we move into next week. 

&&

.SHORT TERM /TODAY THROUGH FRIDAY/...Western WA will see one
last dry day today before the overall weather pattern turns more
fall-like and active. Patchy fog this morning with clouds
increasing throughout the day. Expect highs in the 50s. A cold 
front and trough will move inland this evening and tonight for 
wetting rains along the coast and in the mountains. The interior
will see light measurable rain with overall amounts generally 
less than a tenth of an inch. 

The next weather system moves in late Thursday and Friday as a
deep low shifts east into B.C. This system will bring windy
conditions to the coast and north interior (Everett northward)
Thursday night through Friday morning. The probability of strong
winds remains low, however, minor wind impacts are still
possible. This system will be wetter with 1-2" of rain over the
coast and Olympics, up to an inch in the Cascades, and 
0.50-1.00" in the interior through Friday night. In addition, 
this system will bring building seas with high surf conditions 
possible along the coast. See marine discussion below. 33

&&

.LONG TERM /FRIDAY NIGHT THROUGH TUESDAY/...A parade of systems
will barrel through the Pac NW over the long term period 
keeping the weather active. There's little break in the pattern
and expect more fall-like weather through next week. These 
systems will bring more rain, high elevation snow, and windy
conditions. JD/33

.AVIATION...Southwest flow aloft will develop today and 
increase as a frontal system approaches the region. The front will 
shift onshore across Western Washington late this evening. Patchy 
fog  and low clouds still remains in some areas this morning, with 
improvement likely by 20Z. High and mid level clouds will increase 
through the day with ceilings lowering to MVFR in rain along the 
coast near or after 00Z. These conditions will spread to interior 
terminals by 03Z-06Z tonight. Rain will transition to showers by 
Thursday morning, with conditions likely drying out by the 
afternoon.

KSEA...VFR will prevail through this afternoon before ceilings 
deteriorate to MVFR in rain toward 06Z. Light southwesterly winds 
around 5 kt this morning then southerly and increasing to 8 to 11 
knots this afternoon/evening.

.MARINE...South/southwesterly winds will start to increase over
the coastal waters this morning as the front approaches the 
area. Small Craft Advisories begin across outer coastal waters 
first, then including the inner coastal waters by afternoon and 
in/near strait entrances tonight as winds increase. Southerly 
winds will also increase this afternoon through the interior 
Salish Sea from the central Puget Sound up through the southern 
Strait of Georgia, but winds are expected to remain below 
advisory thresholds. The front moves through late this evening 
and winds slowly ease into early Thursday. Seas build back to 
10-12 ft tonight and continue into Thursday.

Conditions become more active Thursday night into Friday as another 
stronger frontal system traverses the area waters. Winds increase 
area-wide overnight, with 60-80% chances for gale force wind gusts 
across portions of the coastal waters and the East Strait of Juan de 
Fuca through Friday morning. Winds look to slowly weaken during the 
day Friday. Seas will also begin to increase Thursday night, in 
conjunction with the winds but also as a large, long-period swell 
group arrives to the coastal waters. Seas will most likely rise to 
around 15-18 ft, through ensemble guidance shows a 25-40% chance of 
seas exceeding 20 ft. Seas look to maximize by mid-day Friday before 
easing Friday night.

The next frontal system, however, will be on its heels with winds 
increasing once again on Saturday, with higher (50-70%) chances for 
gale force winds across the coastal waters through early Sunday 
morning. Winds remain elevated overnight before easing on Sunday. 
Seas rise once again late Saturday up to 16-20 ft during the day 
Sunday. Seas ease slightly Sunday night.

Conditions look to remain hazardous going into next week.
Additional frontal systems move near or through the area waters 
almost every day, bringing additional periods of strong to gale-
force winds. Deep low centers over the Gulf of Alaska will continue 
to generate and send several series of large, longer- period waves 
to the coast, keeping seas at least above 12 ft.

.HYDROLOGY...River flooding is not expected through the next 
seven days. However, a series of increasingly wet weather 
systems will bring rain (and high mountain snow) to the region,
especially from Friday onward. While current forecasts are not 
expected to cause flooding, this will begin to saturate soils 
and raise base flows that could bring a concern with subsequent 
storms systems.
