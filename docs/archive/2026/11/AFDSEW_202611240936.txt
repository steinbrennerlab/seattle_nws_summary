.SYNOPSIS...
A series of systems will bring widespread precipitation, gusty 
winds, and increase the potential for coastal flooding along with 
high surf throughout much of the upcoming week. A weak upper
ridge may produce somewhat quieter weather over the upcoming
weekend, but overall unsettled conditions are expected to
persist.

&&

.SHORT TERM /TODAY THROUGH THURSDAY/...
The first of a series of frontal systems is spreading
precipitation inland across Western Washington early this
morning. A series of frontal waves will slow its eastward
progression. As a result, we'll likely see a lull in 
precipitation, especially across the interior, later this 
morning into the afternoon. What is left of the front will 
eventually drag onshore this evening for another uptick in 
precipitation. Winds will remain gusty through the period, 
particularly along the coast, where a wind advisory is in 
effect. 

Any relative lull in precipitation (and wind) on Wednesday will
be brief at best as a 160 knot jet streak west of 140W drives a
more vigorous frontal system toward the region. Impacts 
associated with this system will likely be more widely felt.
Though the parent low with this system stays well to our 
northwest, it will produce a strong southeasterly gradient 
capable of producing high wind Wednesday night into early 
Thursday along the coast as well as across the North Interior. 
Precipitation with this system will also be more significant 
with totals between 00Z Thursday and 00Z Friday of 2+ inches 
along the coast, 3 to 5 inches in the Olympics, 1.5 to 3 inches 
in the Cascades, and around an inch for the interior lowlands 
outside of the Olympic rain shadow. Snow levels will be well 
above the passes through the period. 

In addition to periods of widespread precipitation and gusty 
winds, coastal flooding and high surf will also be of concern. 
High surf and low atmospheric pressure coinciding with some of 
the highest tides of the year will create the potential of 
major coastal flooding. Conditions will likely peak on Wednesday
along the Pacific coast and Thanksgiving Day for inland 
coastlines.

&&

.LONG TERM /THURSDAY NIGHT THROUGH MONDAY/...
A fair degree of uncertainty hangs over the early portion of the 
extended forecast. A number of ensemble members build an upper 
ridge into California toward the weekend, but Western Washington
is simply too far north to really benefit from that as systems 
ride over the top of the ridge into the area. Once again, and
stop me if you've heard this before, any break will be brief.
Ensembles show good agreement by early next week with another
broad upper trough taking up residence offshore for a return to
more active conditions as December arrives.

.AVIATION...
Low pressure will remain offshore with a couple of fronts moving 
through or near the terminals. Steady light to moderate rain will 
continue to fall over the terminals into Wednesday. Terminals are 
VFR, and will likely remain VFR through the day (except for heavier 
rain bringing visibilities down at times). Ceilings will lower 
tonight into Wednesday morning, with the best chance of MVFR being 
along the coast and in the south interior. The coastal MVFR likely 
lingers into Wednesday. Gusty southeast winds will continue with the 
coastal areas seeing the highest winds (up to 35 to 40 kt), and the 
north interior/San Juans/Cascades (up to 35 kt). Remaining areas 
will see 5-15 kt winds with occasional gusts to 20kt. Winds just 
aloft from the surface will be stronger (especially into the 
evening) out of the southwest, which may cause instances of speed 
shear.

KSEA...VFR ceilings are favored through early Wednesday morning as 
rain continues to move in and out of the terminal. The chances for 
MVFR increase to 30-40% Wednesday morning from 06Z-15Z, otherwise 
VFR is favored afterwards. Winds will remain out of the southeast at 
8-15 kt with gusts to 20 kt at times.

HPR

.MARINE...
Low pressure will remain offshore through the majority of the week, 
with a couple of fronts remaining remaining slow or stalled 
offshore. The headlines remain in place through Tuesday evening - 
storm force wind gusts in the outer coastal waters, with gale gusts 
continuing in the inner coastal waters, and the west/east Strait of 
Juan de Fuca. Winds will slowly decrease through the day into 
tonight/first part of Wednesday, but will still remain gusty with 
gale force wind gusts in the coastal waters and east Strait of Juan 
de Fuca. Gale warning headlines were extended in these areas through 
Wednesday afternoon, except for the west strait which has the gale 
warning replaced with a small craft advisory (SCA) this afternoon 
into Wednesday. SCA's will also continue for the northern inland 
waters and Admiralty Inlet into Wednesday afternoon, and the central 
strait through early Wednesday morning as winds decrease below any 
headline thresholds.

Thursday's fronts remains on track to bring another round of 
potentially strong winds to the coastal waters (up to storm force). 
Gale force winds are also likely to continue in the east strait, and 
are also possible in the northern inland waters Thursday. As winds 
shift more to the southwest remaining waters will likely see winds 
strong enough to impact small craft. Friday will see a reprieve from 
the strong winds of this week, with winds not picking back up until 
later in the weekend (only affecting small craft in the coastal 
waters and west portions of the strait). 

Seas will build up to 18 to 22 ft with long periods today into 
Wednesday (there is also an SCA for the Grays Harbor bar where rough 
to severe bar conditions will occur with the combination of strong 
ebbs and high seas). The seas will hold at 18 to 22 ft through 
Friday where they will decrease to 10 to 13 ft, and 6 to 8 ft 
Saturday before building back to 10 to 13 ft early next week.

HPR

.HYDROLOGY...
Wetter systems moving into the region through this week will 
start to bring some rises to area rivers. At this time, no 
widespread river flooding is expected, however the Skokomish 
River will likely reach flood stage around midweek and topping 
into moderate, possibly near major given latest guidance. A 
river flood watch has been issued for Mason County from 
Wednesday morning through Sunday afternoon.
