.SYNOPSIS...A stronger frontal system will bring gusty winds
tonight and Friday. A warm front moves northwards on Saturday
for warmer weather. A cold front will bring another round of 
rain and gusty winds late Saturday into Sunday. The weather
pattern remains active moving through next week. 


&&

.SHORT TERM /TODAY THROUGH SATURDAY/...An upper level trough
will cross western WA this morning with isolated shower activity
expected (mainly in the Cascades). Otherwise, dry weather this
afternoon with temperatures in the 50s. 

Tonight, a deep low will shift east into northern B.C. with
increasing southerly winds across western WA. Highest winds 
will be along the coast and in the north interior (north of 
Everett) - Wind Advisories are in effect. Expect gusts to 40-50
MPH overnight with gusty winds easing late Friday afternoon.
Most of the rain will be over the Olympic Peninsula and near the
Canadian border tonight through Friday. The Seattle metro will
see rain late Friday afternoon or evening and continuing Friday
night as the cold front shifts SE through the area. 

Another deep low will track towards B.C. on Saturday but we will
start the day dry as we sit in the warm sector. Highs on
Saturday will be warmer and in the 60s. But as the low shifts 
closer, we will see another round of windy weather across much 
of western WA. The peak windy period will be Saturday evening 
through early Sunday morning. Per NBM guidance, there's a 50 
percent chance of seeing gusts 50 MPH or greater along the coast
and north interior during this period. 33

&&

.LONG TERM /SATURDAY NIGHT THROUGH WEDNESDAY/...Active weather
continues through next week as the parade of storms impact the
Pac NW. There's little break in the action and each storm 
brings the potential for gusty winds, additional lowland rain, 
and high elevation snow. A larger swell train may reach the 
coast Saturday night or Sunday with the potential for 20 ft seas
affecting the coast. 33 

&&

.AVIATION...West to northwest flow aloft will continue across 
Western Washington today with post-frontal shower activity 
winding down. Ceilings will be a mixed bag of VFR and MVFR 
ceilings this morning as well as a few pockets of IFR across 
parts of the Southwest Interior and Kitsap Peninsula. 
Improvement to VFR is expected for most areas toward 18Z as mid 
and high level clouds associated with a warm front offshore 
begin to thicken across the region. Ceilings will fall to MVFR 
late tonight and early Friday across portions of the Olympic 
Peninsula and North Interior as precipitation associated with 
the warm front spreads onshore. Gusty east to southeasterly 
surface winds will develop late tonight into Friday across 
portions of the coast and North Interior. 

KSEA...Periods of MVFR will continue into mid-morning with shower 
activity diminishing. VFR conditions under varying amounts of high 
and mid level clouds are expected from 18Z onward into this evening. 
Surface winds southerly 8 to 11 knots easing this afternoon.

.MARINE...An active period of weather across area waters is 
expected into early next week as a series of frontal systems 
cross the region. Winds will increase across the coastal waters,
strait entrances, and northern inland waters later tonight into
Friday as a warm front approaches the coastal waters and 
stalls. This system will shift onshore and weaken Friday night 
as an associated surface low makes landfall near the south end 
of Haida Gwaii. A complex and much deeper area of low pressure 
will lift northeastward offshore on Saturday night pushing a 
vigorous frontal system into the area. If models maintain their 
current trends, this system will likely generate gales across 
all area waters along with coastal seas easily crossing the 20 
foot threshold. 

High pressure may briefly rebuild across the waters Sunday night 
into early Monday before another front arrives with the renewed 
potential for additional headlines.

.HYDROLOGY...River flooding is not expected through the next 
seven days. However, a series of increasingly wet weather 
systems will bring rain (and high mountain snow) to the region,
especially from Friday onward. While current forecasts are not 
expected to cause flooding, this will begin to saturate soils 
and raise base flows that could bring a concern with subsequent 
storms systems into next week.
