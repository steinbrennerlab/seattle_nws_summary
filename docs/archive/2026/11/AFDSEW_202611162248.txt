.SYNOPSIS...
Mostly dry conditions this afternoon through Tuesday as brief 
ridging moves through the area. A deep upper low offshore will 
move southwards Wednesday for some spotty showers over the 
southern half of the area. Drier conditions Friday before 
troughing deepens offshore into next weekend for the return of 
more precipitation and active weather.

&&

.SHORT TERM /TONIGHT THROUGH WEDNESDAY NIGHT/...
Drier conditions this afternoon with post frontal showers mainly
offshore. A mix of sun and clouds this afternoon will make high
temperatures this afternoon range in the low to mid 50s across
much of the region, with areas closer to the water in the low
50s. With some light surface gradients tonight into Tuesday
morning, will likely see areas of fog early Tuesday AM, for
areas in the Southwest Interior and Central Puget Sound. Onshore
flow in the afternoon will help the fog dissipate with high
temperatures generally in the low 50s for most areas. 

A upper level trough offshore will start to dig southwards 
Tuesday into Wednesday. This system will primarily move south of
our area but will rotate showers over the southern half of the
area, additionally along the coast on Wednesday. Along with 
precipitation, easterly winds will kick up slightly, especially in
the Cascade gaps, with probabilities of gusts over 35 MPH 
generally around 40-60% across much of the gaps Wednesday 
morning. High temps will be slightly cooler with upper 40s and 
low 50s, areas along the Southwest Interior and coast could see 
highs in the mid 40s. Overnight lows in the low to mid 30s. 

&&

.LONG TERM /THURSDAY THROUGH MONDAY/...Drier conditions Thursday
into Friday as the aforementioned low rotates southward away
from western Washington. Likely will see areas of fog Friday
morning as residual moisture remains. Upper level troughing and
a upper level low will drop down from the Gulf of Alaska and
will situate itself over the coast into the weekend. Light
precipitation comes back into the forecast over the weekend as 
the low spins showers up in the area. Latest ensemble guidance 
suggests a more active, wetter pattern through the first half of
the week with multiple systems entering the area.

.AVIATION...Conditions are predominantly VFR this afternoon and
are expected to persist through this evening. Isolated MVFR 
cigs will remain possible for terminals along the coast (KHQM)
through this evening, however, as showers continue to move 
inland across the Olympic Peninsula. With conditions clearing 
across the interior, fog is expected to develop overnight into 
Tuesday morning, first across the South Sound and Chehalis 
River Valley (impacting KOLM), then later spreading into other 
portions of Puget Sound and resulting in the potential for
conditions to deteriorate to IFR/LIFR for a few hours early
Tuesday morning. Conditions will then rebound to VFR by mid- 
morning as fog dissipates.  

KSEA...VFR conditions will continue through this evening with
southerly winds persisting at 3-6 kt. Areas of fog look to 
develop overnight into Tuesday morning and could encroach upon 
the terminal. As a result, conditions may drop down to IFR for 
a few hours on Tuesday morning, with latest probabilistic 
guidance hinting at about a 10% chance of IFR conditions 
developing between 12-14Z.

.MARINE...Seas across the coastal waters this afternoon are 
still hovering between 16-18 ft but will continue to subside 
towards 10-12 ft by Tuesday morning. Overall, winds remain much 
lighter across the area waters today due to high pressure which
has built back into the region. Winds will increase through the
day on Tuesday and will turn offshore by Tuesday night as a low
pressure system offshore moves southward through the NE Pacific.
Winds will be locally enhanced along the gaps in terrain, most 
notably through the Strait of Juan de Fuca and near Grays 
Harbor Tuesday night. Latest REFS guidance shows a 70-90% chance
of wind gusts exceeding gale force across the outer coastal 
waters, so have have gone ahead and issued gale warnings for 
these areas. Gusts may also briefly reach gale along the inner 
coastal waters (especially the waters adjacent to the West 
Entrance of the Strait and along Grays Harbor County) but are 
not expected to be as long- lived. Nonetheless, additional small
craft advisories are likely for the central and western Strait 
as easterly winds increase to 25-30 kt Tuesday night, as well 
for the inner coastal water zones on Tuesday as both winds and 
seas increase. Seas will build back to 12-15 ft Tuesday night 
into early Wednesday.

Calmer conditions will follow on Wednesday as the low pushes 
out of the area and then persist through Friday as high pressure
builds back into the coastal waters in its wake. The pattern
will become more active again heading into the weekend as another
series of systems look to move through the area.

.HYDROLOGY...
The Skokomish River has fallen below flood stage and will 
continue to recede throughout the week. River flooding is not 
expected over the next several days, but chances increase again
early next week (mostly on the Skokomish River) as the pattern
becomes more active and additional rainfall is expected over 
the weekend and into early next week.
