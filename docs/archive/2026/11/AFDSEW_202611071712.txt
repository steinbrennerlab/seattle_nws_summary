.SYNOPSIS...
Mild day today with a warm front to the north and a cold front
still offshore. Cold front moving through tonight bringing rain
and windy conditions to most of the area along with high surf 
to the beaches. Post frontal showers ending Sunday. Another wet 
and windy system moving into the area Monday night but not
nearly as strong as the system tonight. Atmospheric river 
settling up over Western Washington Tuesday will lift north 
Tuesday night. Cold front stalling near the coast Wednesday 
and Thursday moving through Thursday night. Another system will
arrive Friday. 

&&

.SHORT TERM /TODAY THROUGH MONDAY/...Western Washington 
remaining in the warm sector today with a warm front to the 
north and a cold front still offshore late this afternoon. Rain 
developing along the coast this afternoon with the rain not 
making it as far east as the Puget Sound by late afternoon. 
Highs near 60.

Cold front reaching the coast this evening. The front will move
through the Puget Sound area around midnight and will be into
the Cascades by early Sunday morning. This front has some punch
to it. Model 925 mb winds just ahead, with and just behind the
front in the 55 to 65 knot range along the coast and in the 35
to 50 knot range over the interior from the Puget Sound 
northward. Some of these winds will mix down to the surface. 
Have upgraded the high wind watch on the beaches to a high wind 
warning beginning late this afternoon with southerly wind gusts 
has high as 60 mph. Have added wind advisories for the interior 
coast zones, along the Strait of Juan de Fuca and Admiralty 
Inlet, and over the interior near the Puget Sound from Seattle 
Northward including the San Juan Islands for southerly wind 
gusts 45 to 50 mph tonight into early Sunday morning. Strongest 
winds will be with the frontal passage this evening along the 
coast and around midnight for the interior. Our benign October 
weather left plenty of leaves on the trees increasing the 
chances for impacts with borderline advisory wind speeds. 

In addition to the winds, seas will rise rapidly into the 22 to
26 foot range along the ocean beaches tonight. A high surf 
warning has replaced the high surf advisory with the warning 
beginning at 10 pm. Seas will be well above 10 feet by low tide
late this afternoon at Mocrocks beach. This along with strong 
winds will make for dangerous conditions for the clam dig. 

Weather calming down Sunday, although breezy conditions will
still be present in the morning. Post frontal shower activity 
will be minimal with most of the activity over with by noon. 
Highs in the mid 50s under mostly cloudy skies. Seas remaining 
elevated in the 22 to 26 foot range. 

Break in the weather continuing Sunday night with the next
system near 135W by 12Z Monday. The front is showing signs of
becoming negatively tilted with the parent low moving north
along 142W near 55W early in the morning. Lows in the mid 30s to
mid 40s. Seas easing overnight. The high surf warning will come
to an end at 10 pm Sunday evening.  

Front moving east Monday but still offshore by late Monday 
afternoon. Rain reaching the coast in the afternoon with a 
chance of rain inland by late afternoon. Parent surface low way 
up in the Gulf of Alaska. Model 925 mb winds 30 to 40 knots 
along the coast with the front and weaker inland. This system 
will not produce winds like the system tonight. Felton

.LONG TERM /MONDAY NIGHT THROUGH FRIDAY/...
Models in good agreement with the front moving through Monday 
night and another front right on its heels for Tuesday. 
Atmospheric river setting up Tuesday night with the bulk of the 
moisture aimed at Vancouver Island. Warm front hanging over 
Western Washington. Model solutions becoming inconsistent 
Wednesday as an upper level trough digs south offshore. This 
will push the warm front north into British Columbia. Solutions 
for the trailing cold front range from stalling along the coast 
to stalling back near 130W Wednesday. Inconsistencies continue 
Thursday with solutions split between what is left of the front 
moving through to the front remaining stalled off the coast. 
There is better agreement Friday with another system moving 
across the Pacific into the area. In general chance pops for 
Wednesday and Thursday with rain along the coast and rain likely
inland Friday. 

Mild temperatures through the period. Highs near 60 Tuesday and
Thursday, lower to mid 60s Wednesday, then back down to the mid
50s with the rain Friday. Highs Wednesday will be close to
records in some locations. Record high for Seattle is 65 set in
1990. Current forecast high is 64. Felton

.AVIATION...
West to southwest flow aloft will increase this morning and become 
strong this afternoon and evening as a vigorous frontal system moves 
onshore into the region. Plentiful low level moisture and light 
surface winds have resulted in a mixed bag of ceilings this
morning from LIFR to MVFR for most areas along with areas of 
surface visibility below 3 statute miles. The low ceilings and 
reduced visibility are expected to scour out for most areas by 
18Z-20Z as low level east to southeasterly flow begins to 
increase ahead of the incoming system. Ceilings are expected to 
deteriorate back to MVFR in increasing rain along the coast 
after 00Z. Rain will spread to most of the interior by 06Z with 
ceilings near or just above 3000 feet. Strong southerly surface 
winds with gusts 35 to 45 knots will develop along the coast 
late this afternoon/evening with strong winds spreading to 
interior areas this evening and overnight.

KSEA...VFR ceilings persist at the terminal this morning, with 
lower clouds within the vicinity not expected to push into the 
terminal. Gusty surface winds will develop by late this afternoon
and increase through the evening. Rain is expected to arrive 
between 04-06Z with ceilings lowering to near or just above 
3000 feet. Surface winds light E/SE this morning will rise to 8 
to 12 knots this afternoon then quickly increase to southerly 15
to 25 knots gusting to 30 to 35 knots this evening and persisting
overnight before gradually easing Sunday morning. Low level 
wind shear is expected just ahead of and with the front this 
evening with 2000 foot winds becoming southerly at 45 to 50 
knots.

.MARINE...
An active weather pattern will continue into next week as a series 
of frontal systems impact area waters. A vigorous frontal system 
associated with 970 millibar surface low curling northeastward 
offshore tonight is expected to produce a period of storm force wind 
gusts over the coastal waters later today into tonight with gales 
across the remainder of the waters tonight. Seas will build to 21 to 
28 feet late tonight into Sunday before gradually subsiding Sunday 
night into Monday. Weak high pressure will briefly build over the 
waters on Monday before another deepening surface low moving toward 
Haida Gwaii pushes a front across the waters Monday night into 
Tuesday.

.HYDROLOGY...
River flooding is not expected through the next seven days. 
Multiple weather systems moving through the area in the next
week. While current precipitation forecasts are not expected to
cause flooding, this will saturate soils and raise base flows 
that could bring a concern with storm systems by late next 
week.
