Area Forecast Discussion
National Weather Service Seattle WA
402 PM PST Sun Nov 22 2026

.SYNOPSIS...
An upper-low will meander offshore bringing light lowland rain 
and mountain snow showers to the area tonight. Broad troughing
over the NE PAC will then open up the pattern on Monday to more
robust weather. A series of systems will bring widespread 
precipitation, gusty winds, and increase the potential for 
coastal flooding along with high surf throughout much of the 
upcoming week. 

&&

.SHORT TERM /TONIGHT THROUGH TUESDAY NIGHT/...
A stubborn low-center offshore will continue to spin light rain
and mountain snow to the area tonight. Hi-res models has most 
of the activity falling over the coast and Olympics and latest 
KLGX reflectivity scans are indicative of that trend. Snow 
levels will be around 2,000 to 3,000 ft as notable accumulations
are slated to fall across the Olympic Mountains. A winter 
weather advisory remains in place for this area including 
Hurricane Ridge where several inches of snow is in the 
forecast. Low temperatures tonight will fall into the mid 30s to
lower 40s.

The aforementioned low center is still on track to get ingested
into the mean flow on Monday. Broad troughing over the NE PAC
will begin to encompass much of the region as the parade of
disturbances commences. The first frontal system will spread
widespread rainfall across the area Monday night into Tuesday.
Snow levels will rise from 3,000 to 4,500 ft on Monday evening
to 4,000 to 5,500 ft Tuesday evening. As a result, snow is
forecast to fall at the higher passes such as Stevens but major
accumulation is not expected at this time. Gusty southeast winds
are also favored mainly for the coast, San Juan, and Island 
counties for both Monday and Tuesday. Gusts look exceed 40 mph
during this time for these locations as REFS probabilities are 
next to certain. Precipitation will become less steady Tuesday
night as the disturbance tracks off and away but showers will
linger. Temperatures in the short-term forecast will be near
seasonal with highs close to 50 F and lows in the 40s.

Coastal Flooding: Steadily larger astronomical high tides along
the Pacific coast will combine with increased surge and large 
waves to bring a return of more significant coastal flood 
concerns for the next few days. Larger waves and lower pressure 
will likely push water levels higher along the Grays Harbor 
County coastline, including places like Ocean Shores and 
Westport, where moderate coastal flooding is expected during 
Monday morning's high tide. Minor coastal flooding is also 
possible along the north coast and Strait of Juan de Fuca 
extending to Port Angeles. 


&&

.LONG TERM /WEDNESDAY THROUGH SUNDAY/...
Ensemble and deterministic guidance continue to highlight 
additional frontal systems entering the area throughout the long
term with another round of widespread rain, and breezy winds. 
Rainfall will likely be heaviest Wednesday night into Thursday, 
especially over the Olympics, where the Skokomish River is
forecast to ease into moderate flood stage. Additionally, 
confidence is increasing for the potential of moderate to major 
coastal flooding stages being reached along portions of the 
Grays Harbor County coastline during Wednesday tides, and high 
surf may only enhance these impacts. The tides increase more 
significantly for the Strait and Puget Sound coastlines later in
the week around Tuesday onward, and the potential for impacts 
in these locations will need to be closely monitored as we get 
into the range of total water level guidance.

.AVIATION...
VFR conditions are currently present most areas with shower 
activity. VFR will likely persist throughout much of the 
upcoming period however, heavier showers may reduce visibility 
at times, particularly for coastal terminals (KHQM & KUIL) late 
overnight into Monday morning. Surface winds south-southeasterly
5-10 kt with isolated gusts near 20 kt. Showers again into
Monday with locally breezy winds.

KSEA...VFR ceilings throughout the upcoming period. Winds out 
of the south-southeast 5-10 kt.

&&

.MARINE...
A low pressure system remains offshore this evening with the first 
of a series of fronts lifting across the area. Advisories for winds 
and seas over the coastal waters remain in effect as gusts to 30 kt 
and seas around 10-13 ft will remain. Rough conditions on the Grays 
Harbor bar will continue through the next 24 hours and likely 
through most of the week.

Winds will increase across the eastern Strait of Juan de Fuca and 
the coastal waters by early Monday with the next disturbance moving 
into the waters. This will bring frequent gale gusts to the eastern 
Strait and inner coastal waters, as well as strong gales to the 
outer coastal waters. Probabilistic guidance continues to reflect 60-
75% chance of storm force gusts over the outer coastal waters by 
late Monday afternoon, so a storm warning has been issued for the 
outer coastal waters. In addition to strong winds, the offshore low 
will continue to generate longer period swell that will enter the 
coastal waters. This will result in building seas with a mix of long 
period westerly and short period southerly components that will 
maintain steep and confused conditions for the majority of the week 
over the coastal waters. 

Another round of high end gales and possible storm force gusts will 
result late Wednesday into Thursday as the low tracks through the 
region. This could bring more widespread gales into the north 
interior waters and eastern Strait, but this remains dependent on 
the path of the low. Regardless, expect at least 20-25 kt winds over 
all waters into the Thanksgiving Day holiday, with gales likely 
(>70%) over the coastal waters and eastern Strait.  Ensemble 
guidance favors weak high pressure building over the waters Friday, 
bringing a brief respite from the long duration of significant 
winds. However, seas will continue to run high into the weekend as a 
result of the stormy conditions and constant parade of lows.   Cullen


&&

.HYDROLOGY...
Wetter systems moving into the region through this
week will start to bring some rises to area rivers. At this 
time, no widespread river flooding is expected, however the 
Skokomish River will likely reach flood stage around midweek and
possibly topping into moderate. A Hydrologic Outlook has been 
issued regarding the rainfall and potential river flooding for 
this timeframe.
