.SYNOPSIS...
Just a few post frontal showers today for Western Washington
with the area in between systems. Next system arriving Monday 
night with a warm front right behind it Tuesday. Warm front 
lifting north Tuesday night. Trailing cold front stalling 
offshore Wednesday producing possible record high temperatures. 
Cold front getting stretched out Wednesday night with an upper 
level trough digging south offshore. What is left of the front 
moving through Thursday night. An atmospheric river will set up 
over Western Washington Friday and Saturday. 

&&

.SHORT TERM /TODAY THROUGH TUESDAY/...
Quick hitting cold fronts like the one Saturday night almost
always cause trouble. Front well to the east early this morning.
Satellite imagery and doppler radar showing post frontal shower
activity over the area. Haven't seen any lightning strikes in
the last hour. Temperatures at 2 am/10z are still mild, in the
lower to mid 50s. 

Calmer weather today across the area with post frontal shower
activity drying up this afternoon. Still breezy in some
locations this morning but winds aloft are easing quickly and 
onshore surface gradients are weakening. Under mostly
cloudy/partly sunny skies temperatures will not warm up much
from the current readings with highs in the mid 50s.

Even though the winds have eased post frontal high surf
will keep dangerous conditions present along the beaches. The
high surf warning will remain in effect today.

Temporary upper level ridge moving through the area tonight. 
Low level flow going offshore with the approach of the next 
weather system. Some clearing overnight will allow temperatures
to drop into the mid 30s in the colder locations. Most places a
couple of degrees either side of 40. Seas subsiding overnight
into the 15 to 18 foot range. Current forecast has the high 
surf warning expiring at 10 pm tonight. 

Next frontal system approaching Western Washington Monday.
Parent low for the front well up into the Gulf of Alaska and 
moving north northwest. This will stretch out the front and 
cause it to become more north south oriented slowing down the 
eastward movement of the system. Rain reaching the coast early 
afternoon and over the interior by the Monday afternoon commute.
Highs again in the mid 50s. 

Front slowly moving through Western Washington Monday evening
with rain coming to an end after midnight. Windy conditions 
along the coast and over the Northwest Interior ( but nothing 
like we had Saturday ). Lows in the mid 40s to lower 50s. 

Next system moving quickly across the Pacific has a long fetch
of moisture with it, all the way back to 35N/150W. IVT values in
the 800-1000 km/m/s range, some of the higher numbers we have
seen so far this season. Rain moving back into the area during
the day. Highs in the mid 50s. Felton 

&&

.LONG TERM /TUESDAY NIGHT THROUGH SATURDAY/...
Extended models showing good consistency lifting Tuesday's warm
front to the north Tuesday night. Trailing cold front stalling
off the coast Wednesday as a deep upper level trough digs south
behind the cold front. This scenario puts Western Washington in
the warm sector Wednesday with record highs, in the lower to mid
60s, possible. 

Cold front stalled offshore Wednesday night getting stretched
out and weakened as the trough continues to dig south. What is
left of the front moving inland later Thursday into Thursday
night. 

Flow aloft consolidating offshore Friday with an atmospheric
river setting up over Western Washington for more rain. Another
upper level trough will dig south off the coast Friday night
into Saturday. This trough will be a little further east than
the one earlier in the week. This has the potential to pivot the
atmospheric river right over Western Washington keeping rain
going Friday night into Saturday. 

Snow levels remaining elevated during the extended period with
snow confined to the higher elevations well above pass levels.
Felton

.AVIATION...
The cold front has now traversed the area, leaving western 
Washington under mostly VFR conditions with scattered showers.
Terminals are reporting a scattered lower deck (2000-2500 ft), which 
may fill in to MVFR at times with the showers, but it should remain 
mostly scattered with a 15-25% chance of seeing MVFR conditions 
through this morning. Cloud coverage will slowly decrease throughout 
the day through high clouds will continue to stream overhead. Winds 
have peaked across the area but remain around 15-25 kt this morning 
with some gusts 20 to 35 kt. Winds start to ease below 10 kt after 
around 18-21Z this morning, then below 5 kt after 03Z Mon. Offshore 
flow will start to increase towards the very end of the TAF period 
around 12Z Mon.

KSEA...Mostly VFR conditions through the TAF period with a 20-30%

Showers and lower level clouds will start to decrease after 18-21Z

kt will slowly decrease today, with easing gusts and winds around 10

.MARINE...
Winds have peaked over the coastal waters early this morning and 
will continue to ease through the morning hours. Gale force winds 
over all the area waters will ease below thresholds this morning for 
a brief period of calmer winds through late tonight before the next 
frontal system begins to move across the coastal waters on Monday. 
Winds will begin to increase again early Monday morning, peaking in 
the afternoon the frontal system traverses the area. Additional 
headlines will be likely for all area waters, though Gale Watches 
have been issued for the coastal waters and East Strait of Juan de 
Fuca where confidence is highest in gale force gusts (50-80% chc). 
Winds will decrease again Monday evening but not for long as another 
system will be on its heels for another round of stronger winds 
overnight Monday and throughout the day Tuesday. Additional frontal 
systems will move through the waters mid to late week, likely 
bringing more rounds of widespread headlines to the area waters.

Seas remain on their way up early this morning but are expected to 
top out in the 20-25 ft range this afternoon. Seas will ease tonight 
into Monday to around 12-15 ft before the next system brings seas 
back to the 16-18 ft range on Tuesday. Seas will lower and rise 
through mid to late week but remain above 13 ft through the end of 
the week, with the next best chance for seas above 20 ft over this 
weekend.

.HYDROLOGY...
River flooding is not expected the next seven days. Multiple 
weather systems moving through the area in the next week. While 
current precipitation forecasts are not expected to cause 
flooding, this will saturate soils and raise base flows. If the 
atmospheric river stalls over the area late next week into next
weekend we might need to keep an eye on our old friend the 
Skokomish River. Felton
