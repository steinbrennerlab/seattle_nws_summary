.SYNOPSIS...
A weak system will brush the region tonight, bringing chances 
of light rain to the coast tonight and the interior early 
Tuesday. The pattern introduces more active weather mid to late 
week as a series of systems brings lowland rain, mountain snow, 
gusty winds, and the potential for high surf to western 
Washington. A brief break in the unsettled weather is possible 
Friday night into Saturday, before the next round of active 
weather moves in late in the weekend into the first half of next
week.

&&

.SHORT TERM /TONIGHT THROUGH WEDNESDAY NIGHT/...
An upper level ridge will continue to push inland western 
Washington this afternoon with dry conditions expected the 
remainder of the day. A cloudy day with high temperatures in the
low to mid 50s across much of the area. A weak system will 
brush over the region tonight into Tuesday, bringing a small 
chance of showers. The areas that may receive showers would be 
locations along the coast, the Olympics, and perhaps the 
Northwest Interior. Elsewhere, conditions should remain dry 
throughout the day on Tuesday. High temperatures will be a touch
warmer in the upper 50s, maybe low 60s for areas in the 
Southwest Interior. 

The weather pattern will shift to more active, with a series of
weather systems starting to approach the area starting on
Wednesday. A frontal system looks to move over the area
Wednesday afternoon, bringing widespread rain and high elevation
snow. Most of the rain activity will be focused in along the
coast, Olympics, and the North Cascades with these areas seeing
perhaps a half of inch of rain. Locations in the interior will
likely get rain shadowed and may see a shower or two,
especially areas like the Puget Sound. Snow levels will be
generally around 5000 to 5500, with moisture bringing a few
inches of snow in the higher peaks of the Olympics and Cascades.
Heading into Wednesday evening, we will also see an uptick in
southerly winds, primarily along the Pacific coast, with
gusts around 25 to 35 mph. Locations in the interior could see
gust up to 25 mph at times through the evening. Wind and
precipitation will begin to slowly decrease heading into
Thursday morning. Temps on Wednesday staying in the mid to upper
50s. 

Mazurkiewicz

.LONG TERM /THURSDAY THROUGH MONDAY/...
Ensemble guidance continues to favor a brief lull on Thursday before 
the more active pattern takes hold over the region late Thursday 
into Friday. This is shaping up to potentially bring a strong 
frontal system with multiple hazards including gusty winds, lowland 
rain, and high elevation snow, as well as high surf for the Pacific 
coastline. Snow levels around 5000 ft will support heavy snow across 
the higher mountain elevations, with 1-2 inches of rain in the lower 
mountain elevations. In addition, probabilistic guidance continues 
to indicate around a 50% chance of 40 mph or stronger wind gusts 
along the coast and areas from Whidbey Island northward. High surf 
potential also increases as a large, long period swell moves through 
the waters to the coastline, bringing risks for beach erosion and 
hazardous conditions along beaches and jetties.

Guidance begins to depict a wider range of potential outcomes toward 
the weekend, but these are largely centered around differences in 
timing of subsequent systems. If the upper ridge builds a bit more, 
this would direct more of the moisture into the B.C. coast. However, 
any ridge looks to be rather transient, allowing the next surge of 
moisture to return to the region by the end of the weekend. In any 
event, it's clear that we're entering into a more active period of 
weather late this week through the start of next week.    Cullen

.AVIATION...
VFR conditions continue this evening and should continue for most 
area terminals overnight and into Tuesday with a thick layer of high 
clouds moving across the area. This will help to limit fog potential 
overnight, but the more sheltered terminals (such as OLM and PWT) 
may see localized periods of fog. Winds have become mostly calm with 
light offshore flow overnight. N/NE winds 5 kt or less continue into 
the day on Tuesday. Cloud cover will also begin to decrease Tuesday 
evening.

KSEA...VFR conditions this evening with high OVC clouds over the 
terminal. Expect this trend to continue overnight, with cloud cover 
limiting any fog development. Light NE winds 5 kt or less, 
calm/variable at times.

.MARINE...
The primary marine issue remains the larger seas associated with the 
long period swell working through the coastal waters. Seas will 
remain around 8-10 feet, largest over the outer zones through 
tonight. Seas will continue to ease on Tuesday. Otherwise, attention 
turns to a shift to a much more active pattern beginning mid week. 
Expect widespread advisory winds across the coastal waters with 
probabilities greater than 75% on Wednesday as a frontal system 
moves through. Seas will also build late Wednesday into Thursday 
over the coastal waters, reaching around 12 to 14 ft. A stronger 
frontal system Friday will bring widespread advisory winds and 
around a 35% chance of gales to the coastal waters and the eastern 
Strait of Juan de Fuca. Expect seas to build more significantly with 
this system, bringing around a 20-30% chance of 20 ft seas over the 
coastal waters. Additional frontal systems may follow this weekend, 
maintaining an active pattern into the start of next week. 

Cullen/62

.HYDROLOGY...
River flooding is not expected through the next seven days. However, 
a series of increasingly moist weather systems will bring rain 
(and high mountain snow) to the region this week, especially 
Friday onward. While current forecasts are not expected to cause
flooding, this will begin to saturate soils and raise base 
flows that could bring a concern with subsequent storms systems.
