.SYNOPSIS...A frontal system moves across the area late today
into Sunday for steadier rain, gusty winds. Post-frontal showers
on Sunday. Unsettled conditions continue Monday with troughing
over the western US. Another low approaches the west coast
midweek with the majority of the precipitation focused south of
western Washington. 

&&

.SHORT TERM /TONIGHT THROUGH MONDAY NIGHT/...Increasing clouds
this afternoon with localized showers ahead of the next frontal
system approaching the region from offshore. Steadier rain 
moves onshore late this afternoon into this evening, moving into
Puget Sound tonight. Steadier precipitation then slides into 
the Cascades by Sunday morning. South winds also increase with 
this front, with the strongest winds along the coast and San 
Juan Islands. A Wind Advisory remains in effect for these areas 
with gusts of 50 to 55 MPH. A Wind Advisory has also been issued
for Whidbey Island and Admiralty Inlet region with southeast 
wind gusts of 40 to 50 MPH. Elsewhere, breezy conditions are 
expected, mainly ranging 25 to 40 MPH. Post-frontal showers 
develop on Sunday, resulting in unsettled conditions at times. 
In addition, weak instablilty (150-300 J/kg) may lead to 
localized thunderstorms, particularly along the coast and 
Olympic Peninsula. Otherwise, winds remain elevated on Sunday, 
but slowly ease during the afternoon hours. A Coastal Flood
Advisory has been issued for the San Juan Islands again Sunday
morning before tides gradually decrease through this week.

Upper troughing over the western US on Monday will keep a
limited threat for showers over the area, especially for the
Olympic Peninsula and Cascades. Highs in the mid to upper 50s 
through Sunday will cool on Monday to the low 50s. 

&&

.LONG TERM /TUESDAY THROUGH SATURDAY/...Drier conditions are
expected on Tuesday as troughing moves into the interior
Rockies. A deep surface low approaches the west coast on
Wednesday. Ensembles keep the majority of the precipitation
focused south of western Washington with this system, however a
chance of precipitation remains due to some lingering 
uncertainty in the proximity of the system. The system moves 
onshore across CA on Thursday, allowing for another period of 
drier conditions late week. Uncertainty increases further in 
ensembles by next weekend, but indications of troughing 
offshore may result in the return of a more active pattern. JD

.AVIATION...A mix of MVFR and VFR conditions this afternoon 
with some areas of low clouds associated with scattered showers 
across portions of the area, as well as increasing mid and high 
level clouds as a frontal system approaches the coast. Low-end 
VFR conditions should prevail for most through this evening 
before rain begins to arrive to the coast this evening and will 
move through the interior overnight. A brief period of MVFR cigs
(and vis along the coast in heavier rain) will be possible 
overnight, otherwise ceilings look to remain VFR (in the 
3500-4500 ft range) through much of Sunday as scattered showers 
continue. There is a low (15-20%) chance of isolated 
thunderstorms, mainly along the coast.

South/southeasterly winds 8-15 kt with gusts to 25 kt will increase 
tonight, with gusts up to 25-30 kt across the entire area by late 
tonight. Locally higher gusts 30-40 kt possible along the coast and 
through the northern interior overnight. Winds will peak overnight 
but remain elevated through much of Sunday with continued gusts of 
15-25 kt as winds will be very slow to decrease.

KSEA...VFR conditions with vicinity showers through this afternoon. 
The next front is slated to arrive tonight with steadier rainfall 
and the potential for a brief period of MVFR cigs overnight between 
around 09-15Z Mon. Surface winds remain S/SE. Winds 8-12 kt today, 
increasing to 10 to 15 kt overnight with gusts up to 25 kt possible 
through early Sunday. Winds will begin to slowly ease Sunday 
afternoon with VFR conditions continuing.

.MARINE...A strong low pressure system will send a cold front 
across the area waters as the low center tracks to the northeast
along the British Columbia coast. Winds will continue to 
increase across the area, with winds peaking overnight. Storm 
Warnings remain in effect for the coastal waters with peak wind 
gusts overnight forecast up to 40- 50 kt, particularly across 
the offshore coastal waters. Gale Warnings remain in effect 
across the most of the interior waters, with a Small Craft 
Advisory in effect for the Puget Sound. Winds will begin to 
decrease Sunday morning but will do so gradually, with most 
locations seeing Small Craft Advisory level winds through much 
of the day on Sunday. Winds will finally ease over the coastal 
waters and northern Salish Sea Sunday night.

Seas are slowly easing from their maximum last night, but will 
remain in the 16-20 ft range before the strong winds overnight raise 
seas back to around 20-22 ft. These short period waves will ease 
fairly quickly early Sunday morning to around 15-18 ft before the 
westerly long-period swell arrives late Sunday morning, with waves 
peaking in the afternoon/evening hours. Seas will rise to a maximum 
of around 20-22 ft at around 16 seconds, but could be as high as 25 
ft farther offshore.

With higher pressure building across the area on Monday, this will 
allow for a break in the activity, with winds remaining light and 
allowing for waves to decrease throughout the day, lowering to 
around 10-12 ft by early Tuesday.

A low pressure system will form over the open northeast Pacific and 
move off to the southeast of Washington Tuesday into Wednesday, 
making landfall over northern California late Wednesday. While 
somewhat distant, this system will allow winds to turn offshore and 
increase, as well as increasing waves for the next round of 
headlines mid-week. Calmer conditions under high pressure are then 
favored Thursday into the end of the week.
