.UPDATE...
A front is currently working its way throughout the region this
evening as light rainfall spreads across western WA. Steadier 
rainfall will break into the showers overnight before conditions
trend drier into Thursday morning. Precip amounts are not 
expected to be impactful with less than 0.25" expected for the

below along with an updated marine/aviation section.

.SYNOPSIS...
A weak frontal system moves across the area through
tonight. A stronger system arrives Friday bringing steadier rain
and locally gusty winds. Warm front moves northwards on Saturday
with an associated cold front bringing another round of rain and
gusty winds late Saturday into Sunday. A brief break early
Monday before additional systems arrive into midweek.

&&

.SHORT TERM /TONIGHT THROUGH FRIDAY NIGHT/...
A front is approaching the coast this afternoon with increasing
mid and high clouds over western Washington. The front moves 
onshore late this afternoon and moves into Puget Sound this 
evening into tonight. A few hours of steadier rain is expected 
with this system. In addition, south winds will peak around 15 
to 30 MPH along the immediate coast and Whidbey Island region 
this evening. Otherwise, this will be a weaker system with 
rainfall amounts up to 0.15" for most of Puget Sound, and 
0.25-0.5" along the coast. Temperatures peak in the 50s today.

There will be a break in the precipitation on Thursday, with 
even a mix of clouds and sunshine. However, the break will be 
relatively short as the next frontal system approaches the
region Thursday night with increasing rain early Friday.
Steadier rain continues on Friday for most areas, with the
heaviest QPF amounts focused in the Cascades and Olympic
Peninsula. South winds also increase early Friday. Winds are
expected to be strongest along the northern coast and from
Whidbey Island northwards. NBM probabilities for gusts over 40
MPH range 50-90% for the majority of these areas, with higher
probabilities on the REFS. Will need to monitor the potential
for any Wind Advisory in these aforementioned regions on
Friday. Winds elsewhere will be locally breezy, with gusts
peaking between 20 to 30 MPH, especially near the water. The
front weakens Friday night, but a warm front lifts northwards
overnight, resulting in continued rain at times. Highs will
reach the 50s again Friday.

&&

.LONG TERM /SATURDAY THROUGH WEDNESDAY/...
The majority of western Washington will be positioned in the 
warm sector on Saturday as the warm front lifts into BC during 
the morning. Most areas are expected to remain drier by Saturday
afternoon, with exception being the Olympic Peninsula and near 
the BC border where there remains lingering precip potential. 
Otherwise, highs will be warmer on Saturday, peaking in the 60s 
for most areas. 

A cold front progresses eastward Saturday night into Sunday
bringing additional steadier rain and gusty winds. The bulk of
the precip will be Saturday night, with precipitation more
confined to the Cascades by Sunday afternoon, other than a few
lingering showers. Another round of breezy south winds Saturday
night with the front, with the strongest winds again along the
coast and Whidbey Island northwards. However, winds will likely
be a bit stronger elsewhere with this front compared to Friday,
and thus more widespread gusts of 20 to 35 MPH may occur. In
addition, seas build to near 20 ft along the immediate coast on
Sunday, and will monitor for high surf conditions. Snow levels
initially around 8000 feet Saturday fall towards 4000-5000 feet
by later Sunday for higher elevation snow in the Cascades and
Olympics. A brief period of drier weather then arrives Sunday
night into Monday.

Uncertainty increases in ensembles later Monday into midweek.
However, there is potential for a longer duration rain event
during this period. Mean of ECMWF/CMC ensembles focus the 
moisture plume towards western Washington, with GEFS focused 
more into British Columbia. This will be something to monitor 
over the next few days with additional rain, winds, and 
increased waves likely at times next week. JD

.AVIATION...
Generally VFR this evening with areas of MVFR/IFR as rain showers 
continue to push inland with a cold frontal passage. Ceilings are 
expected to continue to lower this evening as heavier rain showers 
move inland, with a chance (30-35%) of IFR conditions over the 
Kitsap Peninsula and south of the Puget Sound. By early Thursday 
morning, the rain will have pushed east of the Cascades with MVFR 
ceilings expected to stick around across the region through much of 
Thursday morning. Models suggest VFR conditions returning by 
Thursday afternoon. Breezy south to southwest winds this evening 
from Puget Sound to the northern interior generally 5-12 kt, with 
gusts up to 20 kt possible between 04z-09z as the frontal system 
moves through the area. 

KSEA...VFR this evening, with heavier showers expected this evening 
that will likely bring MVFR ceilings. While showers are expected to 
move east after midnight, MVFR ceilings are favored to hang on 
through Thursday morning with a slight chance (15%) for brief IFR 
ceilings between 14z-19z. Improvement to VFR is expected by 20z. 
Surface winds will generally stay south to southwest, with briefly 
breezy winds up to 18 kt between 06z-10z with the frontal passage.

.MARINE...
An active marine pattern will continue into next week with multiple 
weather systems expected to move over area waters. Increased 
south/southwesterly winds over the coastal waters will continue to 
ease this evening as a frontal system moves eastward. Winds expected 
to also increase across the interior waters with the passage of the 
front, with small craft conditions continuing for portions of the 
Strait of Juan de Fuca and Admiralty Inlet into early Thursday 
morning. 

A stronger system will move over the waters Thursday night into 
Friday, bringing elevated winds and seas. Winds will increase area-
wide overnight. A Gale Watch has been issued for the coastal waters, 
East Strait, and Northern Inland Waters due to increasing 
probabilities for gale force winds gusts. Winds will ease towards 
Friday evening. Seas will also increase Thursday night, climbing to 
15-19 feet by Friday afternoon. 

The weekend will remain active, with an even stronger frontal system 
moving through the area. Widespread gale gusts are possible across 
the waters Saturday afternoon through Sunday afternoon. The highest 
chances for gales are for the coastal waters with a 60-85% chance, 
while the interior waters have a 50-60% chance. Additionally, seas 
will build through the day on Saturday and peak Sunday afternoon 
towards 20 feet. There is a 40-50 chance for seas to exceed 20 feet. 
Seas will start to ease late Sunday into Monday. 

Conditions look to remain hazardous going into next week, as 
additional frontal systems move near or through area waters. These 
frontal systems will bring additional chances for strong to gale-
force winds.

.HYDROLOGY...
River flooding is not expected through the next 
seven days. However, a series of increasingly wet weather 
systems will bring rain (and high mountain snow) to the region,
especially from Friday onward. While current forecasts are not 
expected to cause flooding, this will begin to saturate soils 
and raise base flows that could bring a concern with subsequent 
storms systems into next week.
