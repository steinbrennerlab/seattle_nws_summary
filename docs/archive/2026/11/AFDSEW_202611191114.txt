.SYNOPSIS...
Upper level low dissipating over Oregon today. Building upper 
level ridge offshore moving over the area tonight and remaining 
over Western Washington through Friday night. Ridge gets pinched
off Saturday with a weak system moving into the area Saturday 
night and Sunday. Warm front brushing the area Monday with the 
trailing cold front arriving Tuesday. Next system reaching the 
area Wednesday night into Thanksgiving day.

.SHORT TERM /TODAY THROUGH SATURDAY/...
Satellite imagery shows upper level low over Western Oregon 
with clouds spinning around the low over most of Western 
Washington. Doppler radar has a couple of light showers along 
the Strait of Juan de Fuca with another area of showers that are
probably not getting to the ground near Bellingham. A little 
bit of clearing in the South Sound allowed fog to form with 
visibilities in Tacoma and Olympia under a mile. Temperatures at
3 AM/11Z were in the mid 30s to mid 40s. 

Upper level low weakening and remaining over Western Oregon 
today. Could see some upslope showers near the Cascade crest but
for the most part just a lot of cloud cover associated with the
low at this point. Expecting a small increase in the fog
coverage this morning. The layer is shallow so even with the
cloud cover visibility restrictions going away by afternoon.
At least mostly cloudy skies keeping highs a couple of degrees 
either side of 50. 

Upper level low dissipating tonight. High amplitude upper level
ridge offshore building into Northern British Columbia and the
Yukon today will move east tonight with drying northerly flow
aloft by early Friday morning. Light flow in the lower levels
combining with the clearing skies to produce areas of fog early 
Friday morning with areas of freezing fog in the South Puget 
Sound and Southwest Interior. Lows in the upper 20s and 30s. 

Upper level ridge remaining over Western Washington Friday with
the ridge axis offshore and dry northerly flow aloft over the 
area. Light flow is the lower levels making for a tough call on 
when the fog will dissipate. Even though the layer is shallow 
could see areas of fog lasting into the early afternoon hours 
especially in the South Puget Sound. Highs in the mid 40s to 
lower 50s. 

Upper level ridge axis moving inland Friday night with the next
system moving inside of 130W by 12Z Saturday. System moving east
Saturday pinching off the upper level ridge north of the area.
Moving into the backside of the ridge along with the jet aimed
into Northern California will weaken the system as it moves into
Western Washington later Saturday. System will be weak enough to
call the precipitation type showers. Snow levels dropping below
the pass levels but at least during the day Saturday moisture
looks somewhat limited for the Cascades keeping new snow amounts
very light if any at all. Felton

.LONG TERM /SATURDAY NIGHT THROUGH WEDNESDAY/...
Extended models in good agreement with upper low off the coast
just spinning around Saturday night and Sunday before
dissipating Sunday night. A couple of weak impulses spinning 
out of the low moving through Western Washington keeping a 
chance of showers in the forecast. 

Warm front brushing the area Monday with the trailing cold front
arriving Tuesday. Parent upper level low associated with the 
fronts moving north which will stretch out and weaken the cold 
front as in moves inland Tuesday night. Deep surface low, around
960 mb, also associated with the cold front but the low is far 
enough to the northwest to confine the windy conditions to the 
coast Tuesday and Tuesday night. Breezy conditions over the 
interior just ahead and with the frontal passage Tuesday night. 

Another developing system right behind the cold front
approaching the area Wednesday night. Will have to keep an eye 
on this one with some of the solutions indicating a much further
east track than the low Tuesday night. There are some big 
differences, up to 15 mb, on the strength of the surface low. 
Right now most of the model solutions with the stronger low turn
the low north near 130W late Wednesday into Thanksgiving which 
would minimize the impacts over the area. 

In addition to all this we will be getting into the highest
tides of the month next week. The combination of wind, high
tides and low pressure will cause some coastal flooding problems
beginning as early as Tuesday. Felton

.MARINE...
Seas this morning hovering between 10-11 feet will continue to 
subside today below 10 feet, allowing the Small Craft Advisory over 
the coastal waters to expire later this morning. Weak high pressure 
will build over the waters today through Friday, for generally 
benign marine conditions. The weather pattern will become more 
active again starting this weekend, with headlines likely continuing 
into next week. A low pressure system spinning offshore this weekend 
will bring elevated seas and winds to the waters. Latest 
probabilities for small craft winds over the coastal waters exceed 
70%. There is a 50-80% for small craft winds through the Strait of 
Juan de Fuca. Additionally, models hint at 30-45% chance for gales 
over the outer coastal waters. Seas will build to 10-15 feet.  

A stronger system will impact the waters Monday through Tuesday. 
Increased offshore winds are highly likely to bring additional 
headlines to the waters. Confidence continues to increase with the 
each model run for gales and storm force winds over the coastal 
waters. Latest model guidance highlights a 50-75% chance for gales 
over the coastal waters starting Monday morning. Winds will peak on 
Tuesday with high confidence (90% and higher) for gales and a 35-50% 
chance of storm force winds over the coastal waters. Moreover, there 
is a 65-75% chance for gales through the Strait of Juan de Fuca, and 
a slight chance (35%) for storm force winds through the Central and 
East Strait. Seas also continue to trend upwards with combined seas 
now approaching 20 feet over the outer coastal waters.
