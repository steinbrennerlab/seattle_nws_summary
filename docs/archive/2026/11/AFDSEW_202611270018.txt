.SYNOPSIS...
Post-frontal showers will continue to subside this evening.
Precipitation chances will linger into Friday as a weaker system
brings light rain to the lowlands and light snow to the 
Cascades. A weak ridge building over the Pacific Northwest will
bring a brief break in precipitation this weekend. An active
weather pattern resumes next week with a series of systems
moving over the region.

&&

.SHORT TERM /TONIGHT THROUGH SATURDAY NIGHT/...
Thunderstorm activity has subsided this afternoon, with much of
the shower activity having pushed to the east. Lingering light
showers are possible into this evening across the lowlands and
Cascades, particularly the North Cascades, alongside a Puget 
Sound Convergence Zone. A Winter Storm Warning remains in effect
for the Northern Cascades for an additional 6 inches of snow
above 4000 feet. Additionally, winds still remain breezy this
afternoon with gusts to 20 mph.

A shortwave trough will move through Washington on Friday,
bringing another round of light lowland rain and mountain snow
beginning in the morning. The Cascade Passes will see some light
snow accumulations, generally 1 to 2 inches through Snoqualmie
and Stevens Passes.

Conditions will dry out Friday evening as an upper level ridge
building offshore begins to shift inland. Saturday will be
mostly dry, with the upper level ridge overhead. The ridge axis
will shift east Saturday night, opening the door for additional
precipitation as the next system approaches the Pacific
Northwest.

While astronomical tides peaked today, coastal flooding remains
a threat for many areas heading into the weekend. Areas along
the north interior coastline are favored to see the highest
degree of coastal flooding over the weekend, with lesser impacts
expected elsewhere.

&&

.LONG TERM /SUNDAY THROUGH THURSDAY/...
After a brief break, an active weather pattern resumes by the
beginning of next week. Guidance suggests an upper level trough
offshore on Sunday, which could potentially spread some
precipitation across the region. Another low pressure system is
on track to enter the region on Monday, though models are
struggling with the trajectory of the upper level low. The
European model has the low moving onshore over Washington, while
the GFS brings the low northward over British Columbia. Another
break in active weather is favored on Wednesday, with an upper
level ridge building over the Pacific Northwest. However, the
extended forecast remains fairly uncertain.

While any incoming storm systems next week are not expected to
be particularly strong, additional coastal flooding may occur
into next week due to high astronomical tides.

.MARINE...
Winds have eased across the area waters this afternoon, with Small 
Craft Advisories continuing across most of the coastal waters. Winds 
will continue to ease this evening across the area with advisories 
ending for the interior waters by late tonight. Seas will be slower 
to come down but should lower below 10 ft by mid-day tomorrow.

A weak system will approach the waters on Friday, though impacts 
continue to remain negligible. High pressure is favored to develop 
over the waters through the weekend, with seas and winds to ease, 
the exception is the Strait of Juan De Fuca, where a westerly push 
of winds is likely. 

The next impactful low pressure system looks to move across the area 
Monday into Tuesday for the next round of headlines for winds, and 
seas look to rise, potentially into the 15-20 ft range by early 
Tuesday. Calmer conditions look to be favored into the middle of 
next week.

.HYDROLOGY...
A strong frontal system brought roughly 3 to 6 inches of
rainfall along the Pacific Coast, with up to 9 to 11 inches of
liquid over the Olympic Mountains. While this caused rises to
most area rivers, most remained below flood stage. The exception
is the Skokomish River at Potlach, which peaked this afternoon
just under Major flood stage. River levels will continue to 
gradually fall tonight, with the Skokomish expected to recede
below flood stage by late Friday. Additional rises to the
Skokomish are possible next week with additional incoming
precipitation, but a large degree of uncertainty remains over
the extended forecast.
