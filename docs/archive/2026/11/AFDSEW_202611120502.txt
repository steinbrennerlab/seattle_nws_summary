.SYNOPSIS...A warm front has introduced a very mild air mass to
Western Washington with near record high temperatures expected 
today. A cold front will stall offshore into Thursday with 
mainly dry conditions for interior areas and light rain at times
near the coast. A series of frontal systems will bring a return
of widespread rain and occasionally windy conditions Friday 
into early next week.

&&

.SHORT TERM /TONIGHT THROUGH FRIDAY NIGHT/...Western Washington
sits in the warm sector today with a warm front north into B.C.
and a cold front stalled offshore. We are seeing very mild
conditions with temperatures in the 60s and a few spots 
breaking daily highs by a degree or two. Meanwhile, it's still 
windy along the north coast and north interior but winds will be
easing this afternoon - the Wind Advisory will expire at 4 PM 
today as planned. 

The cold front remains stalled offshore on Thursday with another
mild day planned for western WA. Forecast highs are in the lower
60s or around 5-10 degrees warmer than average. A few light
showers may clip the coast, otherwise it'll be dry in the
interior. 

A deep, stacked, low pressure center will shift E/SE toward WA 
on Friday, slowly pushing a front inland. While most of the rain
will be along the coast, the interior will also see light 
accumulations. A tight pressure gradient will lead to gusty 
winds along the coast with gusts to 35-45 MPH. It'll be gusty 
too across the Cascades, gaps, and foothills with east winds to
20-30 MPH. In addition, high tides and lower pressure may lead 
to minor coastal flooding Friday morning. The main area is the 
shoreline and beaches around the Salish Sea during the time of 
high tide. 33

&&

.LONG TERM /SATURDAY THROUGH WEDNESDAY/...Low pressure (from
Friday) will shift inland Friday night into Saturday, with
another strong Pacific frontal system right at its heels.
Coastal flooding remains a concern on Saturday morning too. This
second system will bring more lowland rain and gusty winds to 
the area Saturday/Saturday night. Highest winds will be along 
the coast with S/SE gusts to 40-50 MPH. It will be windy in the 
interior as well with winds 20-40 MPH. Troughing keeps the area 
wet through Sunday. Rain-wise, we're looking at another 1-2" 
along the coast this weekend with 0.50-1.00" in the interior. 
Additional rainfall in the Olympics will keep the Skokomish 
River running high with flooding possible. Moving forward, 
showers continue into Monday with weaker troughing overhead. A 
weak front heads to our south by midweek. 33

.AVIATION...
A stalled cold front will remain offshore tonight with shower 
activity continuing west of the Olympics well into Thursday. Gusty 
southwest winds continue in the north interior and the coast this 
evening, but will decrease through tonight and remain out of the 
south. A high cloud deck will remain intact tonight into Thursday. 
Only the coast/Olympics/Cascades have significant probabilities for 
ceilings less than VFR (MVFR for the coast, and IFR/LIFR for the

.MARINE...
The gale warning was cancelled early this evening for the east Strait 
of Juan de Fuca as winds have decreased well below gale/small craft 
criteria. The warning continues for the northern inland waters 
through 10 PM (as well as a small craft advisory for the Admiralty 
Inlet) as gusty winds continue east of Whidbey Island and up into 
Bellingham this evening.

Strong winds will continue to persist across the coastal waters and 
northern inland waters today as a cold front remains stalled 
offshore. This front will weaken as it slowly drifts onshore this 
evening, allowing winds to slowly ease this evening into early 
Thursday morning. Seas generally around 15 to 18 feet this afternoon 
will gradually ease to around 12 to 15 feet on Thursday, with severe 
bar conditions easing to rough into Thursday at Grays Harbor.

A pair of strong low pressure systems will move across the coastal 
waters late Thursday night into Friday, with the second moving 
through Saturday night into Sunday. Winds will begin to increase 
across the area waters by the afternoon Thursday. Gales will begin 
as early as Thursday afternoon, peaking overnight into early Friday 
morning. Guidance continues to indicate 50-70% chances for storm 
force gusts over the outer coastal waters, though higher end gale 
force winds continue to be more likely along the inner coastal 
waters. A Gale Watch remains in effect, likely to be upgraded with 
additional Small Craft Advisories elsewhere.

Seas once again will build over the coastal waters on Friday. With 
the low center tracking closer towards our area, that will send the 
largest waves farther south towards Oregon. That being said, very 
large seas are expected, with ensemble guidance continuing to show 
very high confidence in seas above 20 ft by Friday evening, with a 
25-35% chance of seas above 25 ft through Saturday morning. Dominant 
periods will be long but have come down slightly, in the 14-16 
second range. Seas look to briefly ease Saturday morning below 20 ft 
before another system will send another round of large, long period 
waves on Sunday with high confidence in seas above 20 ft with 
chances of seas exceeding 25 ft through early Monday.

With these systems, slight shifts in the low tracks will determine 
where the strongest wave generation/dynamic fetch regions will be. 
While it is looking more likely that the largest waves will be aimed 
towards Oregon and California from the Friday/Saturday system, the 
Sunday/Monday system favors a more northward track with the largest 
waves aimed towards the Washington coast. There is a 5-10% chance of 
waves exceeding 30 ft at the peak wave height Sunday night.

River flooding is not expected on the other rivers in the area
