.SYNOPSIS...
Dry conditions expected today with an upper level ridge
positioned over the region. Areas of fog will be the main
concern today and again Saturday morning before the next system
arrives by Saturday evening. This system will bring light 
lowland rain and mountain snow to western Washington. The 
pattern will become more active next week as a series of 
systems brings widespread precipitation, gusty winds, and the 
potential for minor coastal flooding. 

&&

.SHORT TERM /TODAY THROUGH SUNDAY/... 
A ridge will build into the region today as an upper level low
over Oregon and northern California moves off to the south and
east. Conditions are cool out there this morning with temperatures
trending in the low to mid 30s as of 1AM/09Z. A few spots, especially
areas south of the Sound, may see morning lows dip down into the
upper 20s. Areas of fog have also started to develop across the
Chehalis Valley this morning with light winds and ample moisture
in the low levels. Freezing fog is likely for colder spots across
the Southwest Interior overnight, with fog expected to expand 
further northward across Puget Sound through the early morning 
hours. Dry conditions are expected with the ridge overhead, 
with fog expected to lift and scatter by the afternoon. Afternoon
highs will be in the mid to upper 40s across the interior and 
into the low 50s along the coast today.

Another round of fog (and freezing fog) is expected by Saturday
morning, with lows expected to dip back into the upper 20s to 
mid 30s across the area. The ridge will then push further inland
in the morning, allowing for an upper level low to drop southward
from the NE Pacific and position itself offshore through Sunday.
This system will bring the next round of light rain to the 
lowlands and light snow to the mountains. Rain will approach the
coast by late Saturday morning and then gradually push inland 
into the interior by late afternoon. Overall rainfall amounts 
will remain rather light through Sunday, with only a few 
hundredths of an inch expected for the interior lowlands and 
around a tenth of an inch expected along the Central Coast. With
snow levels hovering around 2000 ft, could even see some light 
snow accumulations (less than 1 inch) at the Cascade passes. 
Afternoon high temperatures both days will be in the mid 40s to 
low 50s. Lastly, high astronomical tides over the weekend may 
bring the potential for some minor coastal flooding along the 
Pacific Coast and the Strait of Juan de Fuca by Sunday. 

&&

.LONG TERM /SUNDAY NIGHT THROUGH THURSDAY/...
The low situated offshore will eventually become absorbed into 
the mean flow on Monday as a broad upper level low drops down 
into the NE Pacific from the Aleutians. This low will then deepen
offshore Monday night into Tuesday and will bring a more active
pattern to western Washington as the system swings a number of 
disturbances across the region. Expect multiple rounds of widespread
lowland rain, mountain snow, and gusty winds through the second
half of the week. Additionally, high astronomical tides combined
with the weather impacts from these systems, will increase the 
threat for coastal flooding - most notably along the Pacific 
Coast as large waves build along the coast Tuesday into
Wednesday.

.MARINE...
Generally benign marine conditions today with weak high pressure 
over the waters. Fog may form over the interior waters, with highest 
chances over Puget Sound this morning. Latest high-res guidance 
suggests portions of the Central and South Sound has a 50-60% chance 
for seeing visibilities less than 1 NM. 

A low pressure system offshore will bring elevated seas and winds to 
the waters starting as early as tonight through this weekend. A 
Small Craft Advisory has been issued for the coastal waters and West 
Portion of the Strait of Juan de Fuca. Additionally, as the front 
associated with the parent low passes over the coastal waters, winds 
will increase toward gale force over the outer waters Saturday 
morning. With latest high-res probabilities around 50-70%, a Gale 
Watch has been issued for the outer coastal waters. As for the inner 
coastal waters, confidence is much lower for gale force gusts, but 
may see an occasional gale gust. Seas will build towards 10-13 feet 
by Sunday. 

An active weather pattern will continue next week. A stronger system 
will impact the waters Monday into Tuesday. Increased offshore winds 
are highly likely to bring additional headlines. Confidence 
continues to increase for the potential for gales and storm force 
winds over the coastal waters. Latest model guidance now highlights 
probabilities higher than 70% for gales starting late Monday 
morning. Winds look to peak early Tuesday morning, with high 
confidence (90% and higher) for gales and a 45-70% chance for storm 
force winds over the outer coastal waters. Additionally, there is a 
50-70% chance for gales through the Strait of Juan de Fuca on 
Tuesday and a slight chance (35-40%) for an isolated storm force 
gust through the Central and East Strait. Seas will build towards 20 
feet over the outer coastal waters, with probabilities suggesting a 
25-35% chance for seas exceeding 20 feet. 

Additional headlines are favored midweek into late in the week, with 
a 70-100% chance of small craft winds over the coastal waters and 
Strait of Juan de Fuca. Seas are likely to remain elevated as well, 
with probabilities above 70% for seas greater than 10 feet and a 40-
70% for seas greater than 15 feet.
