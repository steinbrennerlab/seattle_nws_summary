.SYNOPSIS...
An upper-level ridge will position over the region this evening
before waning over the weekend. Areas of fog will be the main 
concern until the next disturbance arrives Saturday evening with
light lowland rain and mountain snow showers. An active pattern
will lead into next week as a series of frontal systems bring 
widespread precipitation, breezy winds, and the potential for 
minor coastal flooding. 

&&

.SHORT TERM /TONIGHT THROUGH SUNDAY NIGHT/...
The latest forecast remains on track this evening with no
updates. An upper- level ridge axis will cross overhead tonight
as conditions remain "dry" and stable. Quotations were needed 
for the word dry in the previous sentence as dewpoint 
depressions will have little to no spread. REFS model soundings 
are showing a stout inversion developing tonight across much of 
W WA. This feature in concert with surface high surface pressure
and weak gradients will lead to develop of fog with freezing 
fog a possibility for portions of southern Puget Sound, 
southwest interior, and Kitsap Peninsula to name a few. 
Overnight lows will range between the upper 20s to upper 30s.

A foggy start to the day on Saturday but the pattern will begin
to change. An upper-low will position offshore on Saturday, forming
a Rex Block as it interacts with a high center in northern BC. 
This set up will allow for a sub 990-995 mb surface low to 
meander offshore the WA coast throughout much of the weekend. 
PoPs are favored to increase on Saturday afternoon as light 
lowland rain showers and mountain snow showers (snow levels 
around 2,500 to 3,000 ft) return to the forecast. Showers will
continue into Sunday as well but the bulk of the precipitation
will remain offshore before transitioning to steadier
precipitation overnight along the coast and Olympic Peninsula. 
Precipitation amounts are not expected to be impactful. Seasonal
temperatures are expected in the short-term with highs in the
upper 40s to near 50 F and overnight lows in the 30s.

High astronomical tides over the weekend may bring the 
potential for some minor coastal flooding along the Pacific 
Coast and the Strait of Juan de Fuca by Sunday.

.LONG TERM /MONDAY THROUGH FRIDAY/...
The aforementioned upper-low will finally get ingested into the
mean flow on Monday as a broad upper-trough encompasses much of
the NE PAC. Models are picking up on the potential for a well- 
organized frontal system deepening offshore Monday night into 
Tuesday. This will open up the pattern to a parade of 
disturbances, maintaining an active pattern into the latter half 
of next week. Here we could see widespread precipitation, breezy
winds and possible minor coastal flooding as surface low 
pressure interacts with high astronomical tide along with the 
potential for high surf.

.AVIATION...
Northerly flow aloft will become south to southwesterly by Saturday 
morning as a broad upper level low moves into the Washington 
offshore waters. The low level flow will become increasingly 
offshore into Saturday. Overall confidence in the ceiling forecast 
is somewhat low overnight into Saturday morning as a stratocumulus 
deck in place across portions of Western Washington has slowed low 
cloud reformation. Additionally, high level cloud arriving ahead of 
a trough offshore will act to lessen low cloud coverage. Patchy LIFR 
fog is still expected overnight in the more fog prone areas of the 
Southwest Interior and Kitsap Peninsula, but may need to be scaled 
back elsewhere. VFR ceilings are expected for Saturday afternoon 
after patchy fog dissipates with any MVFR ceilings likely restricted 
to coastal areas where showers are most likely.


KSEA...Latest guidance is scaling back fog and low cloud 
probabilities. Anything that forms will largely be a function of any 
brief clearing that occurs. Latest REFS has IFR ceiling chances 
around 30% overnight, but satellite trends shows some of the 
stratocu deck breaking up. Any low ceilings that form should be gone 
by 18Z Saturday with gradually lowering VFR ceilings for the 
remainder of the day. Surface winds light E/SE backing to a more 
northeasterly direction at 7 knots or less Saturday afternoon.

.MARINE...
Benign conditions with relatively light winds and seas around 5-6 ft 
will continue through this evening. Another round of patchy fog 
across the Puget Sound and interior Salish Sea will be possible 
again Saturday morning.

Over the coastal waters, winds will begin to increase overnight as a 
frontal system begins to approach the Pacific Northwest.
Winds will increase Saturday morning, peaking around mid-day. East 
to southeast winds will reach Small Craft Advisory criteria cross 
most of the coastal waters and the west and central portions of the 
Strait of Juan de Fuca. Occasional gale gusts are possible across 
the coastal waters, but confidence is highest in the strongest winds 
in the outermost waters north of James Island, where the watch was 
upgraded to a Gale Warning. Winds will stay below headline criteria 
for most of the inland waters, except over the East Strait of Juan 
de Fuca starting Saturday evening, where a Small Craft Advisory has 
been issued through early Sunday morning.

By Sunday, the low center well offshore deepens, which will increase 
the pressure gradients as it slowly creeps closer to the coast. 
Winds increase again across the coastal waters on Sunday and remain 
elevated through Sunday night.

A much stronger low pressure system is on track to arrive Monday 
into Tuesday with high confidence in strong southeasterly winds 
across the coastal waters and Strait of Juan de Fuca, with >80% 
chances for gales through Tuesday evening, as well as a 50-80% 
chance for storm force gusts across the outer coastal waters from 
late Monday through much of Tuesday. Winds will begin to ease from 
their peak Tuesday night into Wednesday but headlines will likely 
need to remain through the end of the week as low pressure systems 
continue to move through the area, keeping winds elevated.

Seas will rise early Saturday to around 8-13 ft, primarily from the 
southerly winds, before the longer period westerly swell arrives 
Saturday night into early Sunday, pushing seas back up to around 11-
14 ft. Seas will remain around 8-13 ft through Monday when the 
strong low will bring seas up to 15-20 ft by Tuesday. Ensemble 
guidance remains confident (60-80% chc) in seas above 20 ft, at 
least across the outermost coastal waters.
Seas will likely remain above 12-15 ft through the end of the week 
as conditions remain active.

.HYDROLOGY...
Wetter systems moving into the region next week will start to
bring some rises to area rivers. At this time, no widespread river
flooding is expected, however the Skokomish River will likely
cross into flood stage mid to late week.
