.SYNOPSIS...
A weak system brushing the region will bring chances of light 
rain to the area through the morning hours today. The weather 
pattern becomes more active starting Wednesday as a series of 
systems brings lowland rain, high elevation snow, gusty winds, 
and the potential for high surf to western Washington. A brief 
break in the unsettled weather is possible Friday night into 
Saturday, before additional systems arrive late in the weekend 
into early next week. 

&&

.SHORT TERM /TODAY THROUGH THURSDAY/...
A weak system brushing the region will continue to bring some 
light showers to western Washington through the morning hours 
today. Latest radar shows plenty of echoes along the coast and 
south of the area over Oregon, but with conditions remaining 
pretty dry in the low levels, observations indicate that not 
much rain has actually reached the ground this morning. Rain 
that has made it to the ground has been rather light so far and 
has only resulted in a few hundredths in spots. Showers will 
remain possible across the area through the morning, with 
conditions generally drying out by this afternoon as shower
coverage decreases. Afternoon highs will be a few degrees 
warmer today than yesterday, with temperatures expected to top 
out in the mid 50s to low 60s. Clearing skies this evening and 
the reintroduction of some moisture in the low levels will bring
back the potential for fog development across the area by early
Wednesday. Overnight lows will be in the mid 30s to low 40s, 
with a few spots across the South Sound approaching freezing.

The weather pattern becomes more active starting Wednesday as
the first in a series of systems moves across western Washington.
This system will bring lowland rain, high elevation snow, and 
gusty winds to the area. More details on each hazard follows 
below:

Rain: The bulk of the moisture with this system will be focused
over the Olympic Peninsula and the North Cascades. Rainfall 
amounts for the Pacific Coast are expected to range between a 
quarter of an inch to half an inch. Many spots across the 
interior lowlands will get rain shadowed, with only a few 
hundredths to a tenth of an inch of rain expected.

Mountain Snow: Snow levels generally be hovering between 5000- 
6000 ft will bring a few inches of snow to the higher peaks of 
the Olympics and North Cascades. Most likely snow amounts across
the higher terrain are between 1-3 inches, with some locally 
higher amounts possible near the higher peaks like Mt Baker.

Gusty Winds: Winds will increase as this system approaches the
region, with southerly winds expected to pick up along the coast
during the afternoon hours and increasing across the interior 
during the evening hours. Winds will be strongest along the Pacific
Coast, where wind gusts between 25-35 mph will be possible. Areas
across the interior will likely see wind gusts to 25 mph. 

Winds will ease and precipitation will taper overnight into 
Thursday, which will bring a quick break in between systems. 
Afternoon highs on Thursday will be in the mid to upper 50s. 

&&

.LONG TERM /THURSDAY NIGHT THROUGH MONDAY/...
A stronger system will move into western Washington Thursday night
into Friday, bringing another round of gusty winds, lowland 
rain, high elevation snow, and the potential for high surf along
the Pacific Coast. The bulk of the moisture with this system 
will again remain aimed at the Olympic Peninsula and the North/Central
Cascades. Snow levels around 5000 ft will climb to 5500-7000 ft
through the day on Friday and will support high elevation snow 
across the Olympics and Cascades, with 1-2 inches of rain
expected in the lower mountain elevations. The highest rainfall
amounts across the lowlands will be along the North Coast, 
where 1 to 2 inches of rain is expected into Saturday morning. 
Many areas across the interior lowlands will be rain shadowed 
again and generally look to receive a couple tenths of an inch  
of rain. Winds will become gusty as the system approaches, with 
probabilistic guidance indicating a 50% chance of wind gusts 
exceeding 40 mph along the Pacific Coast and a 40-50% chance of 
wind gusts exceeding 40 mph for areas from Whidbey Island 
northward. Increasing potential for high surf conditions 
developing along the Pacific coast could bring risks for beach 
erosion and hazardous conditions along beaches and jetties.

Ensemble clusters continue to hint at a brief break in the 
unsettled weather pattern early Saturday as a transient ridge 
of high pressure moves back over the region. Discrepancies 
remain in regards to the amplitude of the ridge at this time, 
however, it does look to aid in directing the bulk of the moisture
of the next system further north into British Columbia. Even 
though the bulk of the moisture will be focused to the north of
the border, the North Coast and the North Cascades could still 
be clipped by this system. 

The ridge does look to move inland rather quickly which would 
open the door for a wet system to move into the area Saturday 
night into Sunday. The pattern will remain active heading into 
early next week as additional systems look to arrive into the 
region.

.AVIATION...
A weak trough to the south and a stationary front to the west will 
keep most terminals covered with VFR broken or overcast decks 
through the majority of the day. The only exception is a few 
isolated instances of brief low IFR/LIFR ceilings or mist/fog in 
portions of the Kitsap/south interior. Showers will continue to move 
northward ahead of the surface trough to the south. These will 
primarily affect terminals west of I-5. Cloud coverage decreases 
this evening/overnight, which will open up another window for fog or 
low stratus Wednesday morning. Best chances for said conditions to 
develop are from the Kitsap to the south interior, and the Cascades 
from 12-15Z through 18-20Z. Winds will remain light out of the 
north/northeast around 5 kt; expect higher winds up to 10 kt in the 
mountains and the coast.

KSEA...VFR conditions expected for Tuesday with high ceilings, 
clearing by this evening. Some uncertainty at this time with the the 
northern extent of the potential LIFR conditions Wednesday morning, 
but LAMP guidance gives a chance of both LIFR ceilings and 
visibilities. Winds remaining out of the north/northeast at around 5 
kt.

.MARINE...
A stationary front remains offshore with a trough passing to the 
south. The main hazard for Tuesday remains elevated seas at 8 to 10 
ft with the longer swell. The inherited small craft advisory was 
extended to Tuesday afternoon as model guidance has the seas slowly 
dropping to 6 to 8 ft this evening/Wednesday. The weather then 
becomes more fall-like with a series of systems passing through, 
beginning mid week and continuing this weekend into early next week. 
Probabilities remain at 75 to 80% for wind gusts over 20 kt in the 
coastal waters, as well as a significant portion of the interior 
waters for gusty south winds. Friday's system will be stronger, with 
both wind and sea concerns. Majority of the waters will hazardous 
winds for small craft. The inner coast and east Strait of Juan de 
Fuca have a 35% chance of gusts reaching gale force. Seas will build 
Thursday into Friday, peaking at 15 to 18 ft with a longer swell. 
The Saturday/Sunday, and Monday/Tuesday systems has similar 
probabilities for small craft and gale gusts to the Friday system. 
Seas as a result will likely remain over 10 ft going into the 
weekend/early next week (12-15 ft).

.HYDROLOGY...
River flooding is not expected through the next seven days. 
However, a series of increasingly wet weather systems will 
bring rain (and high mountain snow) to the region this week, 
especially from Friday onward. While current forecasts are not 
expected to cause flooding, this will begin to saturate soils 
and raise base flows that could bring a concern with subsequent 
storms systems.
