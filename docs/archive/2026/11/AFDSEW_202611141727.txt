.SYNOPSIS...Another frontal system will bring a return of rain 
and windy conditions later today into Sunday. A trend toward 
drier and cooler conditions is expected toward the middle 
portion of next week as the storm track shifts south of Western 
Washington.

&&

.SHORT TERM /TODAY THROUGH MONDAY/...An upper trough axis is 
moving onshore over Western Washington early this morning as 
another, more vigorous system approaches the region just 
offshore. A combination of high tide and low atmospheric 
pressure will bring a renewed threat of minor coastal flooding 
to the San Juans and Western Whatcom county this morning. Seas 
in excess of 20 feet continue along coast beaches. Rain and wind
will increase along the coast later today with rain spreading 
inland this evening into the overnight hours. Windy conditions 
will also develop through today near the east entrance to the 
strait and adjacent areas with some gusts of 45-55 mph.

The front will shift east of the Cascades by early Sunday
afternoon with winds gradually easing and post-frontal showers
across the area. A cooler air mass will follow the front...so
there will be enough instability for isolated
thunderstorms...especially near the coast. Snow levels will 
fall to around 4000 feet Sunday night, but QPF will be light by 
this time for just light accumulation at some of the higher 
passes. An upper level trough will remain over the area on 
Monday for scattered showers mainly coast and higher terrain and
high temperatures back to around seasonal levels. 

&&

.LONG TERM /MONDAY NIGHT THROUGH FRIDAY/...Drier conditions 
return to the area Monday night into Tuesday as the upper trough
shifts eastward. Model solutions are falling into better 
agreement regarding the midweek system taking a further 
southward track and just brushing the southern portion of the 
CWA with some light precip on Wednesday. Ensembles favor cool 
and generally dry conditions to finish off next week with the 
potential for more active conditions returning thereafter.

.AVIATION...A mix of VFR and MVFR in places under stronger 
shower activity. Expecting most terminals to remain under a 
broken VFR layer as shower activity diminishes this morning and 
high clouds begin to fill in as the next system approaches the 
region. The next front will bring heavier rain to the coast this
evening, then moving through overnight into early Sunday 
morning across the interior. MVFR conditions are most likely 
overnight as the heavier rain moves through, then lifting to VFR
after 12-15Z Mon as rain transitions to showers. Some areas of 
localized/episodic lower ceilings cannot be ruled out, however, 
going into Monday morning.

South/southeasterly winds 8-15 kt with locally higher gusts will 
increase tonight, with gusts up to 25-30 kt across the entire area 
by late tonight. Locally higher gusts 30-40 kt possible along the 
coast and through the northern interior overnight. Winds will peak 
overnight but remain elevated through much of Sunday with continued 
gusts of 15-25 kt as winds will be very slow to decrease.

KSEA...VFR conditions with vicinity showers throughout the day. The 
next front is slated to arrive tonight with steadier rainfall, a 
brief period of MVFR cigs overnight, and gusty winds. Surface winds 
remain S/SE. Winds 8-12 kt today, increasing to 10 to 15 kt 
overnight with gusts up to 25 kt possible through early Sunday.

.MARINE...Numerous headlines remain in place as we're currently
in between systems. SCA advisories are in effect this morning 
but as the day progresses, strong winds are slated to become 
more widespread as Gales become common across interior waters 
(minus Puget Sound but a SCA at the least may be needed) and 
Storm Force winds arrive for all of the coastal waters. The 
culprit is a sub 965 mb surface low swinging a cold front across
the region this evening into the night hours. Along with winds,
high seas are forecast around 20 ft today with a dominant 
period of 17 to 18 seconds, highly energetic. Seas will 
gradually decrease to 15 to 20 ft by Sunday morning. An 
additional increase to 20 to 25 appears likely in the afternoon.
South winds will ease a bit on Sunday, but remain elevated 
especially for the Coastal Waters. 


Winds subside further Monday into early Tuesday before increasing 
again going into midweek with a stronger weather system moving 
southwards well offshore. Confidence is leading towards developing 
high pressure for next weekend as benign conditions appear to be on 
the horizon.

.HYDROLOGY...The potential for flooding continues on the 
Skokomish River with periods of heavy rainfall through the 
weekend. A Flood Watch remains in effect. 

River flooding is not expected on the other rivers in the area 
in the next 7 days at this time.
