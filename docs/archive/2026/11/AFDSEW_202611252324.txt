.SYNOPSIS...
A strong frontal system will impact Western Washington this evening 
into Thursday, bringing widespread rain, gusty winds, and increased 
threat for coastal flooding. Precipitation chances will linger into 
Friday with a weaker system. A brief break in precipitation this 
weekend as a weak ridge builds over the Pacific Northwest. An active 
weather pattern resumes next week.  


&&

.SHORT TERM /TONIGHT THROUGH FRIDAY NIGHT/...
Latest radar imagery shows rain continuing to move across portions 
of Western Washington this afternoon as a strong frontal system 
quickly prepares to push onshore starting this evening. Rain will 
start filling in along the coast this afternoon and spread into the 
interior later this evening. Breezy conditions are already present 
across the region, with latest obs showing gusts 20-30 mph. Winds 
will only continue to increase this evening and late tonight. The 
strongest winds will be concentrated along the immediate coastline, 
with gusts up to 60 mph. As a result, the Wind Advisory for the 
coastal communities for Grays Harbor, Jefferson, and Clallam 
Counties has been upgraded to a High Wind Warning. Winds Advisories 
remain in effect elsewhere. Across the Northern Interior and San 
Juan Islands gusts to 50 mph are expected. Along Puget Sound gusts 
to 45 mph are expected. Winds will gradually subside Thursday 
evening. 

Snow levels in the Cascades will range between 5000-6000 feet with 
lower snow levels in the Northern Cascades Thursday morning. The 
majority of the snow is expected to fall overnight into Thursday, 
with 8-16 inches possible in the Northern Cascades. Moreover, the 
atmosphere will become destabilized Thursday morning for the chances 
of scattered thunderstorms. Latest guidance suggests the highest 
chances (20-25%) are along the coast and the Olympic Peninsula. 
Elsewhere, chances for thunderstorms are slightly lower at 15% or 
less. Rain chances continue on Friday as a shortwave trough moves 
across Western Washington.

As has been the case in recent days, coastal flooding will continue 
to be the headline-making hazard as some of the years highest 
astronomical tides combine with storm surge and high surf to produce 
some significant tidal anomalies and impacts to coastal communities. 
Coastal flooding impacts are expected to peak along the coast today, 
with the greatest impacts for the inner waters expected early 
Thursday before astronomical tides gradually weaken.

.MARINE...
A strong frontal system will cross area waters this afternoon into 
Thursday morning, bringing another round of strong winds and 
elevated seas to area waters. A Storm Warning remains in effect over 
the coastal waters, with Gale Warnings covering nearly all inner 
water zones to cover gusty winds with the front as it pushes inland. 
Thunderstorms are also favored to develop over the coastal waters 
early Thursday and move inland towards the inner waters throughout 
the day, producing localized lightning, gusty winds, and small hail.

Winds will die down across the waters by Thursday evening, with a 
Small Craft Advisory covering the coastal waters for both winds and 
elevated seas. A weak weather system is now favored to move into the 
region on Friday, but impacts should be minimal. High pressure will 
likely build over the waters over the weekend, allowing winds and 
seas to ease outside of a weak westerly push through the Strait of 
Juan de Fuca late Saturday into Sunday. The next system moving into 
the region on Monday is expected to bring another round of breezy 
winds, mainly for the coastal waters and into the Strait of Juan de 
Fuca. Additional headlines are likely, with active weather 
continuing into next week.

Seas generally around 15 to 21 feet today will continue to stay 
within that range into Thursday, and will start subsiding late 
Thursday into Friday to around 10 to 15 feet. Seas will lower even 
further by late Friday into Saturday, where we could see seas get 
below 10 feet through the weekend. Seas are favored to rise above 10 
feet into next week.

.HYDROLOGY...
A frontal system will bring an additional 2 to 4 inches of rainfall 
to the region through Thursday, with snow levels rising as high as 
6000-7500 feet. The bulk of the moisture is expected over the 
Olympic Peninsula and along the coast. While area rivers will likely 
experience rises, no widespread river flooding is expected.

The exception is the Skokomish River, where a River Flood Warning 
remains in effect through Friday. The Skokomish will rise above 
flood stage later today and is expected to peak in moderate flood 
stage late tonight into early Thursday morning. The Skokomish is 
expected to fall below flood stage on Friday, with additional rises 
possible into next week.

The incoming heavy rainfall through Thursday may also pose a hazard 
to the Bear Gulch burn scar, which has been sensitive to 
precipitation so far this winter season. A handful of small 
landslides have already occurred on the burn scar, and additional 
landslides in this area cannot be ruled out this week as heavy rain 
continues over the Olympic Peninsula.
