.SYNOPSIS...
An upper-level ridge will position over the region this evening
before waning over the weekend. Areas of fog will be the main
concern until the next disturbance arrives Saturday evening with
light lowland rain and mountain snow showers. An active pattern
will lead into next week as a series of frontal systems bring
widespread precipitation, breezy winds, and the potential for
minor coastal flooding.

.SHORT TERM /TONIGHT THROUGH SATURDAY NIGHT/...
An upper-low over OR and N CA will dissipate as an upper-ridge
builds into the region tonight. Weak surface gradients, high
pressure, and longer nights will promote low clouds and fog 
development across much of western WA. Visibility could sink to 
a quarter- mile, especially in areas prone to foggy conditions 
such as river valleys. Overnight lows are forecast to drop into 
the upper upper 20s to upper 30s where freezing fog is likely 
for colder spots such as the southwest interior. 

As alluded to, a foggy start to the day on Friday but it'll 
give way to mostly clear skies in the afternoon. The upper-
ridge axis will be just offshore as yet another dry day is in 
store. High temperatures are on track to be slightly below 
average for mid-November with highs in the mid to upper 40s. 
Upper-ridge axis becoming firmly overhead Friday evening as the
pattern gently progresses. Friday night almost a carbon copy of
Thursday's with another round of foggy weather in concert with 
min temps in the upper 20s to upper 30s.

An upper-low will position offshore on Saturday, forming a Rex 
Block as it interacts with a high center in northern BC. This
set up will allow for a sub 990 mb surface low to meander 
offshore the WA coast throughout much of the weekend. PoPs are 
favored to increase on Saturday evening as light lowland rain 
showers and mountain snow showers (snow levels around 2,000 ft) 
return to the forecast. Precipitation amounts are expected to be
not impactful.

.LONG TERM /SUNDAY THROUGH THURSDAY/...
Aforementioned upper-low will continue into Sunday maintaining a
chance of showers. It'll finally get ingested into the mean flow
on Monday as a broad upper-trough encompasses much of the NE
PAC. Models are picking up on the potential for a well-organized
frontal system deepening offshore Monday night into Tuesday.
This will open up the pattern to a parade of disturbances,
maintaining an active pattern into the middle of next week. Here
we could see widespread precipitation, breezy winds and the 
potential for minor coastal flooding as surface low pressure 
interacts with high astronomical tide along.

.AVIATION...
An upper level ridge will build into the region tonight into Friday 
with northeasterly flow aloft becoming northerly. Light surface 
winds and mostly clear evening skies will be favorable for low cloud 
and fog formation overnight. Widespread LIFR ceilings and surface 
visibility below 3 statute miles are expected overnight into Friday 
morning across south Puget Sound and the Southwest Interior. Areas 
of similar conditions will be also be found in outlying river 
valleys, the Chehalis Gap, and around the central/north Sound. Most 
areas are expected to scatter out toward 19Z though the stratus may 
hang on longer in the extreme south Sound. High level moisture will 
begin to increase by afternoon and evening ahead of an approaching 
upper trough offshore.

.MARINE...
High pressure will build over the waters tonight through Friday
night for mostly benign conditions. Can't rule out areas of 
fog, particularly for the inland waters tonight and Friday 
night. The weather pattern will become active again on Saturday 
with headlines likely continuing into next week. A low pressure 
system spinning offshore this weekend will bring elevated seas 
and winds to the waters. Latest REFS probabilities for small 
craft gusts over the coastal waters and west/central Strait of 
Juan de Fuca exceed 90%. Additionally, models hint at 60-80% 
chance for gale gusts over the outer coastal waters. Seas will 
build to 10-15 feet. 

A stronger system will impact the waters Monday through Tuesday. 
Increased offshore winds are highly likely to bring additional 
headlines to the waters. Confidence continues to increase with the 
each model run for gales and possible storm force winds over 
the coastal waters. Winds will peak on Tuesday with high 
confidence (90% and higher) for gales and a 60-0% chance of 
storm force wind gusts over the coastal waters. Moreover, there
is a 65-75% chance for gales through the Strait of Juan de 
Fuca. Seas also continue to trend upwards with combined seas 
now approaching 20 feet over the outer coastal waters. An active
pattern will persist well into the middle of next week.
