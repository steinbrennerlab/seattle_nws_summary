.UPDATE...
Fog still apparent for areas across western WA, especially
within valleys. It'll gradually burn off as the morning progress
to increasing mid-high level cloud coverage. High today are
forecast to top out in the mid 40s to near 50 F. Light rain and
mountain snow showers remain favored to increase this
afternoon-evening by way of offshore low center. The previous
discussion remains below along with an updated aviation section.

.SYNOPSIS...
An upper level ridge over the area will shift inland today.
Areas of fog will be the main concern across western Washington 
this morning. The next disturbance will move into the region 
this afternoon and will bring light lowland rain and mountain 
snow to the area. The pattern becomes more active next week as 
a series of systems brings widespread precipitation, gusty 
winds, and the increasing potential for coastal flooding.

&&

.SHORT TERM /TODAY THROUGH MONDAY/...
An upper level ridge over the area this morning will push inland
today, making way for the next system to move into the region by
this afternoon and evening. Conditions are cool out there again 
this morning, with temperatures mainly trending in the upper 20s
to mid 30s as of 1AM/09Z. Areas of fog have also redeveloped 
across the area again overnight, and with temperatures near 
freezing, can expect to see a few areas of freezing fog across 
the region again this morning.

An upper level low will then drop southward from the NE Pacific
today and position itself offshore through Sunday. This system 
will bring the next round of light rain to the lowlands and 
light snow to the mountains. Rain will approach the coast by 
this afternoon and will then gradually push inland into the 
interior by this evening. Overall rainfall amounts will remain  
light through Sunday, with only a tenth to a quarter of an inch 
of rain expected along the coast and a few hundredths of an inch
for the interior - with many spots remaining dry. Snow levels 
will hover between 2000-3000 ft, so will see some light snow 
accumulations (less than half an inch) at the Cascade passes. 
Afternoon high temperatures today and Sunday will be in the mid 
40s to low 50s. Lastly, high astronomical tides over the weekend 
may bring the potential for some minor coastal flooding along 
the Pacific Coast and the Strait of Juan de Fuca by Sunday. 

The low situated offshore will eventually become absorbed into 
the mean flow on Monday as a broad upper level low drops down 
into the NE Pacific from the Aleutians. Another round of 
precipitation will push inland, but will largely remain focused
on the Olympic Peninsula. Snow amounts between 4-8 inches will 
be possible in the Olympics. Afternoon highs will be in mid 40s
to low 50s.

&&

.LONG TERM /MONDAY NIGHT THROUGH FRIDAY/...
The aforementioned low will then deepen offshore Monday night 
into Tuesday and will bring a more active pattern to western 
Washington as the system swings a number of disturbances across 
the region. Expect multiple rounds of widespread lowland rain, 
mountain snow, and gusty winds starting Tuesday and persisting 
through the second half of the week. The system moving across
the area on Tuesday will also bring another round of high surf 
to the coast. Additionally, high astronomical tides combined 
with the weather impacts from these systems, will increase the 
threat for coastal flooding - most notably along the Pacific 
Coast during high surf.

&&

.AVIATION...
Areas of fog still hanging around this morning but it's
gradually lifting to more widespread VFR by midday. High cloud 
coverage is also increasing. Showers with the front now arriving
at the coast and push inland towards 00Z. Overnight, as the 
showers push through ceilings will gradually lower to either a 
low-end VFR or MVFR level early Sunday morning, with the lowest 
ceilings in the south interior. Showers will continue Sunday 
with ceilings improving to VFR late in the morning. Winds will 
be out of the south/southeast 5-10 kt with a few gusts to 20 kt.

KSEA...VFR at the terminal and should remain this afternoon 
with MVFR becoming likely post- showers overnight (as early as 
09Z). Winds: southeast 5-10 kt with a few gusts to 20 kt later 
in the afternoon.

.MARINE...
A front will slow to a near stall over the coastal waters this 
weekend, with high pressure moving out. Couple areas of fog may 
linger in the interior waters this morning. The front will bring 
some showers (no thunder expected through the weekend). Winds remain 
on track to increase with the frontal system this afternoon today 
into Sunday. This system will primarily affect the coastal waters, 
and the east Strait of Juan de Fuca, with winds of 20-30 kt today. 
The northwest coastal water zone does still have the best chance of 
a few gale gusts this afternoon. The coastal system will deepen and 
keep winds gusty on Sunday and into Monday out of the southeast. A 
couple strong system remains on track to impact the waters Monday 
into Tuesday, and Wednesday. PRobabilities remain high for gale 
gusts in the coastal waters, and a portion of the east Strait of 
Juan de Fuca. There remains a 20-40% chance of wind gusts reaching 
storm force criteria with of the next week systems. 

Seas will build from 6 to 8 to 8 to 12 ft today, and continue to 
build on Monday up to 16 to 20 ft, before decreasing back to 10 to 
14 ft by the end of next week.

HPR

.HYDROLOGY...
Wetter systems moving into the region next week will start to
bring some rises to area rivers. At this time, no widespread river
flooding is expected, however the Skokomish River will likely
cross into flood stage late in the week.
