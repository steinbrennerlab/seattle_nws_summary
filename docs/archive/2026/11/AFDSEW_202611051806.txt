.SYNOPSIS...A strong frontal system will bring gusty winds and 
additional rainfall to western Washington tonight and Friday. A warm 
front will warm temperatures as it moves northwards into the area 
Saturday. A follow-up cold front Saturday night into Sunday will 
bring another round of rain and gusty winds. The weather pattern 
remains active moving through next week. 


&&

.SHORT TERM /TODAY THROUGH SATURDAY/...
Current satellite imagery shows typical autumn cloudy skies over W 
WA this morning. Radar shows that there are still some isolated 
showers out there, mainly over the Cascades although there are a 
couple of cells over the Olympic Peninsula. Lastly, what is very 
likely the leading edge of the system expected tonight is just 
starting to show up over the outer coastal waters.

A weak shortwave ridge will hold that system at bay briefly, keeping 
conditions dry for much of W WA today, but this feature shifts 
eastward fairly quickly...bringing activity to the coastline as 
early as this evening but more likely late tonight. Gusty winds will 
accompany the incoming front with particular concern for higher wind 
speeds along the coast and the North Interior /including the islands 
as well as portions of western Whatcom and Skagit counties/...where 
speeds could get as high as 20-30 mph with gusts to 50 mph. This has 
warranted an inherited Wind Advisory for these areas and the 
headline looks to be on track, so no morning update or revision is 
expected at this time. As this system mainly impacts the area 
through the day Friday, this is when the bulk of the rainfall is 
expected with amounts ranging one-half to three-quarters of an inch 
on the high end...mainly expected over the higher terrain and along 
the coast...to one tenth to one quarter of an inch most remaining 
lowland locations. Snow levels will rise throughout the event, 
starting the day off at around 5000-6000 ft and lifting to around 
9000 in the afternoon. As such, not much of a concern for high 
elevation snowfall at this time.

The front pushes out of the area very late Friday night, allowing 
for another weak, short-lived shortwave ridge to enter the area. 
This feature will not be terribly effective at dispelling clouds nor 
lingering post-frontal showers, but does provide a relative lull in 
activity for much of Saturday before the next frontal system comes 
knocking on the coastline come Saturday night.

Even though the short term will be active, temperatures are expected 
to remain mild with daytime highs still a few degrees above seasonal 
normals. Saturday will actually see a boost in temperatures with 
daytime highs that day getting into the 60s for most lowland 
locations. Overnight clouds will keep lows from sinking too much, 
mostly ranging from the mid 40s to around 50 for the entire short 
term period.

.AVIATION...
A mix of conditions ranging from VFR to LIFR this morning. Ceilings 
should gradually improve by this afternoon, giving way to widespread 
VFR as mid and high level clouds associated with a warm front 
offshore begin to thicken across the region. Ceilings will fall to 
MVFR late tonight and early Friday across portions of the Olympic 
Peninsula and North Interior as precipitation associated with the 
warm front spreads onshore. Gusty east to southeasterly surface 
winds will develop late tonight into Friday across portions of the 
coast and North Interior with gusts towards 25-35 kt. 

KSEA...Currently VFR at the terminal, but some low clouds still 
hanging around, so will opt to keep conditions lowered at MVFR for 
now. By 20Z, clouds should have lifted to VFR. SW winds this morning 
around 5-6 kt becoming SE this evening. Breezy SW winds developing 
Friday afternoon after 21Z.

.MARINE...
From Previous Discussion...An active period of weather across area 
waters is expected into early next week as a series of frontal 
systems cross the region. Winds will increase across the coastal 
waters, strait entrances, and northern inland waters later tonight 
into Friday as a warm front approaches the coastal waters and 
stalls. This system will shift onshore and weaken Friday night as an 
associated surface low makes landfall near the south end of Haida 
Gwaii. A complex and much deeper area of low pressure will lift 
northeastward offshore on Saturday night pushing a vigorous frontal 
system into the area. If models maintain their current trends, this 
system will likely generate gales across all area waters along with 
coastal seas easily crossing the 20 foot threshold. 

High pressure may briefly rebuild across the waters Sunday night 
into early Monday before another front arrives with the renewed 
potential for additional headlines.

.HYDROLOGY...River flooding is not expected through the next 
seven days. However, a series of increasingly wet weather 
systems will bring rain (and high mountain snow) to the region,
especially from Friday onward. While current forecasts are not 
expected to cause flooding, this will begin to saturate soils 
and raise base flows that could bring a concern with subsequent 
storms systems into next week.
