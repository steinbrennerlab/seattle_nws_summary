.SYNOPSIS...A weak ridge building over the Pacific Northwest 
will bring a brief break in precipitation today. An active 
weather pattern resumes early next week with a series of 
systems moving across Western Washington. 

&&

.SHORT TERM /TODAY THROUGH MONDAY/...High pressure will lead to
dry and stable conditions across western WA today. The low level
air mass remains moist with fog and stratus covering much of the
lowlands this morning. Mid and high level clouds will increase
this afternoon and overall we'll see mostly cloudy skies. Expect
highs near 50. 

Despite the higher pressure, high tides are still high enough
for minor coastal flooding in the interior this morning - a
Coastal Flood Advisory remains in effect. A Coastal Flood Watch
is also in effect for Sunday's high tide (San Juans) with 
potential for minor to moderate flooding impacts. 

Low pressure near 140W will track NE and off the coast on
Sunday, then shift inland on Monday bringing more rain and wind
to the region. Rain will be mostly light on Sunday, with wetter
conditions as the low moves inland on Monday. Snow levels 
remain fairly high for rain in the Olympics and high-elevation 
snow in the Cascades (over 4000 ft). Additional rainfall will
keep the Skokomish River running high with the potential for
renewed flooding. Elsewhere, we're looking at another 1-2" of
rain over the coast and up to 1" in the interior through both
days. This system will bring more wind too, especially on Monday
as the 980-ish low moves in. NBM mean gusts are in the 30s and
40s MPH with high-end gusts reaching the 50s along the coast and
NW Interior. And with lower pressure, stronger winds, and rain,
there's another threat of coastal flooding in the interior
around Monday's high tide. This system weakens and shifts north
into B.C. Monday night with rain and wind easing over western
WA. 33

.LONG TERM /MONDAY NIGHT THROUGH FRIDAY/...Weak high pressure
nudges inland on Tuesday for a break in the weather. However,
coastal flooding in the interior still remains a threat due to
high astronomical tides. A weak, splitting, system slowly breaks
up over the region Wednesday and Thursday for a chance of rain.
Low pressure dives to our south late in the week, keeping
western WA mainly dry. Temperatures look close to average
throughout. 33

.AVIATION...Northwesterly flow aloft shifting southwesterly by 
this afternoon as an upper level ridge axis traverses the area 
today. Most terminals reporting light and variable winds this 
early morning at the surface and these will persist until 
between 15-18Z before speeds increase to 4-8 kts with a more 
north to northeasterly direction this afternoon and evening. 
Light and variable winds returning to most terminals late 
tonight.

Largely MVFR to IFR conditions across W WA to start the day today as 
the combination of few clouds aloft and light winds at the surface 
have allowed for the formation of stratus and fog. Cigs within these 
categories are fairly consistent across most terminals this morning 
with the largest variations occuring within visibilities, running 
the gamut between 1/4SM with FG to P6SM over the past several 
hours. TAFs leaning pessimistic and given the variability, will 
likely remain in that vein for the 12Z issuance...the only glimmer 
of optimism may be to introduce TEMPO groups to where conditions 
have improved somewhat. Still on track for low level moisture to 
burn off at most locations by 20Z this afternoon, with widespread 
VFR conditions returning thereafter. Incoming high clouds in advance 
of the next weather system entering the area late Sunday may 
reintroduce cigs to the area tonight, however conditions looking to 
stay VFR overnight.

KSEA....Conditions bouncing between MVFR and IFR this early morning 
thanks to low clouds and varying visibilities and conditions likely 
to remain this way for much of the morning. Conditions becoming more

22Z time frame...remaining that way for the rest of the TAF period. 
Light and variable winds for much of the morning before becoming

.MARINE...Winds will remain lighter this morning before east 
winds develop this afternoon and continue through Sunday. Winds 
then shift southeast Sunday night ahead of the next storm 
system. Localized Small Craft Advisory wind gusts may occur 
Sunday along the western entrance of the Strait of Juan de Fuca 
and southern coastal waters, however will wait to put forward 
any headlines to see if these speeds become a little more 
widespread throughout their respective zones.

A strengthening weather system moves northwards across the Northeast 
Pacific Sunday and into the Coastal Waters on Monday. Uncertainty 
continues in regards to the exact track and strength of this weather 
system. At this time, south winds are expected to peak on Monday. 
Probabilities show 75 to 95% chance for gale force wind gusts over 
the Coastal Waters on Monday, with probabilities of 40 to 70% for 
the interior waters. Winds are expected to be strongest along the 
coastal waters, and there is a 20 to 40% probability for Storm 
force wind gusts during this period. Winds begin to ease on Tuesday, 
but remain elevated at times through the week 
as additional weather systems are possible. 

Seas generally 5 to 8 feet today and Sunday, then increasing with 
the next system on Monday, ranging 15 to 18 feet. JD/18
