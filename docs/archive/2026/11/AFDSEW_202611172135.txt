.SYNOPSIS...
A weather system offshore will move into Oregon and Northern
California on Wednesday and weaken. An upper level ridge 
building offshore on Thursday will move over the area on Friday.
Another system stalling offshore will bring a renewed chance of
precipitation over the weekend. A more active and impactful 
weather pattern is expected to develop next week.

&&

.SHORT TERM /TONIGHT THROUGH FRIDAY/...
Western Washington lies between weather systems this afternoon
with seasonal temperatures and some partial afternoon sunshine.
The next system poised offshore is slated to take a more 
southern track on Wednesday moving into Oregon and Northern 
California. Some of the wrap around moisture from it will spread
some light precip northward across the CWA during the day on 
Wednesday, but locations north of about Everett will probably 
measure little to nothing in the rain bucket. Conditions will
start to dry out again on Thursday with upper ridging offshore
moving overhead on Friday. Temperatures through the short term
forecast period will be near or slightly below seasonal norms.

&&

.LONG TERM /FRIDAY NIGHT THROUGH TUESDAY/...
Looking ahead toward the weekend, another upper trough is
expected to move into the coastal and/or offshore waters.
Ensembles remain at odds with regard to its specific track. 
Nonetheless, they generally agree that it will come ashore in 
a weakened state with minimal impacts. Overall QPF looks rather
light, but snow levels will be running around 2500 feet so 
anything that falls at the passes will be in the form of snow. 

A trend toward a more active pattern is beginning to take shape
toward the early and middle portion of next week. Models are
settling into the idea of a 160 to 180 knot jet core entering 
the central and eastern Pacific and helping carve out a rather 
deep mean trough position out around 140W. This is expected to 
send a series of more vigorous frontal systems into the region 
with more widespread precip, mountain snow, and windy conditions
at times. Details will come into better focus in the days ahead.

.AVIATION...
Cloud coverage will continue to scatter out this afternoon with weak 
high pressure inland, and a front off the coast. A couple terminals 
will retain MVFR cigs early this afternoon, but will quickly 
transition to VFR everywhere through the evening. The next front 
will arrive Wednesday morning with showers, arriving roughly from 15-
18Z. High clouds will fill in ahead of the showers from 10-14Z and 
from south to north. This will likely keep fog potential to a 
minimal (primarily in low-lying valleys) wide more widespread brief 
ceiling drops to MVFR likely after 21Z Wednesday afternoon. South 
winds this afternoon will transition to the northeast and northwest 
tonight into Wednesday with speeds around 5-10 kt. Gusts to 20-25 kt 
expected along the Olympic/Pacific coastlines.

KSEA...VFR through early Wednesday with skies clearing through the 
afternoon, and then refilling in the morning with showers beginning 
as early as 20Z. MVFR ceilings are favored briefly from 00Z Thursday 
through 03-05Z. Southwest winds will transition to the northeast this 
evening into Wednesday around 5-10 kt.

.MARINE...
Higher pressure will remain inland today with a low pressure system 
offshore and associated frontal system moving through tonight into 
Wednesday. Offshore winds are expected to increase tonight into 
Wednesday for the coastal waters and western two thirds of the 
Strait of Juan de Fuca. Inherited small craft advisories and gale 
warnings remain valid and were extended into Wednesday evening as 
winds and seas decrease. The strongest winds remain most likely to 
occur in the outer coastal waters. The weather will become more 
benign across the waters Thursday into Friday with light winds. 
Additional systems are expected to pass through Saturday into next 
week, with an increasing potential for storm-force gusts in the 
coastal waters (currently a 40-50% chance for such occurrence). 

Seas: down to 8 to 10 ft Tuesday, increasing back up to 10 to 14 ft 
on Wednesday, decreasing to 5 to 8 ft Thursday through Saturday 
before building back up to 10 to 14 ft early next week.

HPR

No river flooding expected in the next 7 days. However, more
widespread precipitation is expected to return early next week
for rises on area rivers.
