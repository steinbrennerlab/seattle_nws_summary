.SYNOPSIS...
Precipitation will linger today as a weak system brings light
rain to the lowlands and light snow to the mountains. A weak
ridge building over the Pacific Northwest will bring a brief
break in precipitation this Saturday. An active weather pattern
resumes next week with a series of systems moving across 
Western Washington. 

&&

.SHORT TERM /TONIGHT THROUGH SUNDAY NIGHT/...
Latest radar imagery shows precipitation continues to fill in 
from the south as a shortwave trough traverses Western
Washington, bringing light lowland rain and mountain snow. Expecting
precipitation to linger into the late afternoon hours, before
conditions dry out tonight. Drier conditions Saturday as a weak
upper-level ridge builds over the Pacific Northwest. Light to 
calm winds tonight into Saturday morning and residual moisture 
from recent rain will help promote fog development across 
portions of Western Washington, with highest confidence (70-90% 
chance) over the southern interior. As for Puget Sound, there is
a 25-40% chance, with higher chances in the river valleys. Fog 
expected to dissipate by the afternoon, however skies will 
remain overcast as mid and high level clouds move into the area.
The ridge axis will push east of the area on Sunday. 

A developing low pressure system offshore will bring some rain 
inland across Western Washington on Sunday. Parts of Western 
Washington will likely see windy conditions developing by Sunday
night, but the path of the low will define a lot of the 
details.

Coastal flooding remains a threat for many areas heading into 
the weekend. Areas along the north interior coastline are 
favored to see the highest degree of coastal flooding over the 
weekend, with lesser impacts expected elsewhere.

.LONG TERM /MONDAY THROUGH FRIDAY/...
Models continue to struggle on the exact path of the low
pressure system, which could have significant implications in
terms of the wind forecast. The latest GFS continues to take 
a weakening low into British Columbia, while the latest ECMWF 
has a 980 mb low making landfall towards Western Washington. 
Shall the low follow the GFS projected path, the strongest 
winds will stay primarily confined to the coast. However, shall 
the low follow the ECMWF, the low will bring the strongest 
winds towards the interior. 

Ensembles hint at quieter weather towards the middle of the week
with an upper-level ridge building and the storm track shifting
into California. 

Additional coastal flooding may occur into next week due to 
high astronomical tides, with highest concern on Monday.

.AVIATION...
A mix of ceilings this afternoon as a weak storm bring light
precipitation to the terminals through the afternoon. Conditions
will dry out tonight. Low ceilings expected to generally persist
into tomorrow. Southerly winds around 5 kt this afternoon will
become light to calm tonight into Saturday morning. With
light/calm winds tonight and residual moisture from recent
rain, fog may develop at the terminals. Highest confidence is
for the southern interior, where probabilities are 70-90% of
visibility less than 1SM. Confidence is lower for Puget Sound
terminals. However, there is a 25-40% chance for visibility 
below 1SM, especially in the river valleys.

.MARINE...
Winds will remain lighter through Saturday morning as a 
weak weather system moves into Oregon tonight. East winds develop 
Saturday afternoon and continue through Sunday, before becoming more 
southeast Sunday night ahead of the next storm system. Localized 
Small Craft Advisory wind gusts may occur Saturday night, and 
especially on Sunday along the western entrance of the Strait of 
Juan de Fuca, and southern coastal waters. 

A strengthening weather system moves northwards across the Northeast 
Pacific Sunday and into the Coastal Waters on Monday. Uncertainty 
continues in regards to the exact track and strength of this weather 
system. At this time, south winds are expected to peak on Monday. 
Probabilities show 75 to 95% chance for gale force wind gusts over 
the Coastal Waters on Monday, with probabilities of 40 to 70% for 
the interior waters. Winds are expected to be strongest along the 
Coastal Waters, and there is a 20 to 40% probability for Storm Force 
wind gusts during this period as well. Winds begin to ease on 
Tuesday, but remain elevated at times through the week as additional 
weather systems are possible. 

Seas subside below 10 feet tonight, ranging 5 to 8 feet Saturday and 
Sunday. Seas will increase with the next system on Monday, ranging 
15 to 18 feet. JD

.HYDROLOGY...
River levels will continue to gradually fall with the 
Skokomish expected to recede below flood stage on Saturday. 
Additional rises to the Skokomish are possible next week with 
additional incoming precipitation. River flooding is not expected 
elsewhere over the next 7 days. JD
