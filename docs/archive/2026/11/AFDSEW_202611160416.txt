.SYNOPSIS...
Post-frontal showers continue into Monday with upper
troughing across the Western US. A deep upper low moves
southwards offshore Wednesday and moves into California on
Thursday. Drier conditions Friday before troughing deepens
offshore into next weekend for the return of more active 
weather.

&&

.SHORT TERM /TONIGHT THROUGH TUESDAY NIGHT/...
The latest forecast remains on track this evening with no
updates. A post- frontal airmass is positioned over western 
Washington this evening with scattered showers and a few
lightning strikes. Shower activity will slowly diminish into 
Monday, although a few showers will linger on Monday due to 
continued troughing over the western US. Snow levels around 3000
to 4000 feet through Monday will result in light snowfall 
amounts for the higher elevations during times of heavier 
showers. Otherwise, drier conditions develop on Tuesday with 
some partial sunshine by the afternoon. Highs peak in the low to
mid 50s through Tuesday.

&&

.LONG TERM /WEDNESDAY THROUGH SUNDAY/...
A deep upper low in the NE Pacific dives southwards well 
offshore on Wednesday. The bulk of the energy associated with 
this system will be focused south of western Washington as the 
system moves onshore into California on Thursday. Precipitation 
spreads northwards into Washington on Wednesday, however, POPs 
mainly remain confined south of Seattle, and especially south of
WA/OR border due to the trajectory of the system. Easterly 
winds increase in the Cascade Valleys and gaps early Wednesday 
for breezy conditions, with NBM probabilities of gusts over 30 
MPH in areas around North Bend and Enumclaw around 30-60%. Drier
conditions by Thursday into Friday. Periods of clearing may 
result in fog development, mainly Friday AM. Ensembles suggest 
troughing deepens again offshore Saturday into next week, with 
some uncertainty into the exact positioning and depth of the 
trough. Highs remain near normal during this period in upper 40s
to low 50s. JD

.AVIATION...
Most terminals are experiencing VFR ceilings this evening under
scattered post-frontal showers, through under heavier showers 
some brief periods MVFR cigs are being observed. Scattered 
showers will remain overnight, particularly along the coast, but
the decreasing coverage may open up some more clearing and the 
potential for some patchy developing low clouds in spots by 
early Monday morning. There is a 15-35% chance of MVFR cigs by 
17Z Mon. A few lightning strikes have been observed along the 
coast, but should remain limited for the rest of the day (15% or
less chc). Southerly winds 10-15 kt (with gusts to 20 kt near 
BLI and the north interior) will slowly ease tonight into 
tomorrow.


KSEA...Primarily VFR conditions through the TAF period, with 
occasional brief MVFR periods under heavier showers. There is a low 
chance (15-20%, 15-18Z Mon) of MVFR cigs developing in the area 
around sunrise. Southerly winds 8-12 kt decreasing to around 5 kt 
overnight into Monday.

&&

.MARINE...
Winds are beginning to come down across the area as we remain 
in the post-frontal regime across the coastal waters. Winds 
continue to gust at times up to 20-25 kt across portions of the 
northern Puget Sound waters, as well as around Whidbey and 
Camano Islands, but in general winds are coming down such that 
zone headlines should be able to drop this afternoon across the 
interior waters. Winds will be light across all area waters on 
Monday as high pressure briefly builds over the area.

Seas will continue to increase this afternoon, peaking at around 18-
23 ft by this evening and overnight as longer period swell arrives 
at the coast. Seas will begin lower throughout the day Monday, 
reaching 9-12 ft by early Tuesday morning.

Winds look to increase once again Tuesday and Tuesday night as a low 
pressure system passes by off to the west and south. Winds will turn 
offshore, which may be locally enhanced near the Strait of Juan de 
Fuca and near Grays Harbor when the wind direction is aligned with 
the terrain gaps, mainly Tuesday night. This will also push seas 
back up to 12-15 ft Tuesday night into early Wednesday.

Calmer conditions will then follow as winds and seas ease during the 
day Wednesday, with another brief round of high pressure over the 
area Thursday into early Friday. Another series of systems look to 
move through the area Friday night into the weekend.
