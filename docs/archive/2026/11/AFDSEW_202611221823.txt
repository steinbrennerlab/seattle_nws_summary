.SYNOPSIS...
An upper-low will continue to remain offshore 
bringing light lowland rain and mountain snow to the area today. 
More active weather arrives through the week as a series of 
systems brings widespread precipitation, gusty winds, high 
surf, and the increasing potential for coastal flooding.

&&

.SHORT TERM /TODAY THROUGH TUESDAY/...
An upper level low remains parked offshore western Washington
this morning, which will continue to spin up showers into the 
area throughout the remainder of the day. Latest radar imagery
shows showers continuously moving onshore, with some showers,
especially over the coast could see a slight convective 
activity with them in the afternoon. Otherwise, most locations 
can expect on and off showers this afternoon. High temperatures 
generally in the mid to upper 40s for most interior locations. 
Additionally, a Coastal Flood Advisory remains in effect for the
Pacific Coast for the high tide this morning.

Upper level troughing will become more prominent into Monday,
with a wave of steadier precipitation entering the region.
Most of the QPF will be located in the Olympics and the
Cascades, with showers over the interior. Additionally, snow
levels will start off generally around 2000 to 3000 feet late
tonight into early Monday morning, rising to 4000 feet by Monday
afternoon. A Winter Weather Advisory remains in effect for the
Olympics through Monday afternoon, where Hurricane Ridge could
see around 5 to 10 inches of snow. Showers will continue 
throughout Monday with another front arriving to the area by 
Tuesday. 

A stronger front will move over western Washington on Tuesday
into Wednesday as the aforementioned upper level low deepens 
along the coast. Expect widespread lowland rain, along with
mountain snow as snow levels in the Cascades will be generally
around 3000 to 3500 feet. We'll also see locally breezy winds
across the area, more notably along the coast. This system will
also likely bring another round of high surf to the coast. 
Additionally, high astronomical tides combined with the weather
impacts from these systems, will increase the threat for 
coastal flooding, most notably along the Pacific Coast during 
high surf on Tuesday.

&&

.LONG TERM /TUESDAY NIGHT THROUGH SATURDAY/...
Latest ensembles
continue to highlight additional stronger systems entering the 
area Wednesday into Thursday, with another round of widespread
rain, breezy winds, and mountain snow. Rainfall will likely be
heaviest Wednesday night into Thursday, especially over the
Olympics, where the Skokomish River may have to be monitored as
rainfall drives it above flood stage. Additionally, confidence 
is increasing for the potential of moderate to major coastal 
flooding stages being reached along portions of the Grays Harbor
County coastline during Wednesday tides, and high surf may only
enhance these impacts. The tides increase more significantly 
for the Strait and Puget Sound coastlines later in the week 
around Tuesday onward, and the potential for impacts in these 
locations will need to be closely monitored as we get into the 
range of total water level guidance.

.MARINE...
A low pressure system offshore and occluded front will lift to the 
north in the coastal waters today. Showers will continue over most 
waters, with a slight chance of isolated thunderstorms over the 
coastal waters this afternoon and evening. Southeast winds of 20 to 
30 kt will continue in the coastal waters and east Strait of Juan de 
Fuca, where current small craft advisories will continue into 
tonight. Another advisory was added for Grays Harbor Bar as seas of 
9-12 ft will build today.

The pattern for the remainder of the week remains on track and will 
be active with several stronger disturbances moving through the 
waters. The next system late Sunday into Monday will bring 
widespread southeast winds of 20 to 30 kt with gusts to 35 kt over 
the previously mentioned waters. The Monday evening through late 
Tuesday is expected to remain the most impactful, with widespread 
gale gusts and a greater than 60% chance of wind gusts reaching 
storm force in the outer coastal waters. Another system passes 
through Wednesday into Thursday with another round of strong gales 
in the same waters with a 50% chance of gusts reaching storm force. 
Additional interior waters will likely see more wind impacts as 
ensembles have winds turning towards the south and southwest on 
Thanksgiving day. There is a brief break in winds Friday morning but 
it will remain breezy for the coastal waters into next weekend with 
wind gusts still likely breaking 20 kt.

Seas on Monday will build into Tuesday, reaching 18 to 22 ft. They 
will slightly drop off to 16 to 20 ft Wednesday and Thursday, then 
drop down to 10 to 12 ft Friday into next weekend.

HPR

.HYDROLOGY...
Wetter systems moving into the region through this
week  will start to bring some rises to area rivers. At this 
time, no widespread river flooding is expected, however the 
Skokomish River will likely cross into minor flood stage around 
midweek onward and continuing to be in flood stage through the 
weekend. A Hydrologic Outlook has been issued regarding the 
rainfall and potential river flooding for this timeframe.
