.SYNOPSIS...An upper-low will meander offshore bringing light 
lowland rain and mountain snow to the area this weekend. The 
pattern becomes more active next week as a series of systems 
brings widespread precipitation, gusty winds, and the increasing
potential for coastal flooding.

&&

.SHORT TERM /TONIGHT THROUGH MONDAY NIGHT/...The current
forecast remains on track tonight with no planned updates. 33

Previous discussion...A pattern change is beginning to unfold 
across the region as an upper-low meanders offshore. It'll 
continue to spin periods of light lowland rain showers and light
mountain snow into Sunday. A high center in northern BC is 
interacting with the aforementioned low, forming a blocking 
pattern leaving the upper-level progression rather stagnant for 
the next 24 to 30 hours. It'll become ingested within the mean 
flow on Sunday night into early Monday morning as broad 
troughing envelopes the NE PAC. During this time precipitation 
is slated to become steadier, starting over the Olympics before 
gradually spreading inland. Snow levels will start off around 
2,000-3,000 ft before rising into the day on Monday. As a 
result, a Winter Weather Advisory has been issued for the 
Olympics including Hurricane Ridge where 5-10" of snow is 
forecast throughout the duration of this event. Transient 
ridging on Monday evening may allow a brief break in the action 
before the next front arrives late overnight into Tuesday. 
Temperatures in the short term near seasonal with highs in the 
upper 40s to lower 50s. Overnight lows are to be in the 30s to 
lower 40s.

Coastal Flooding: The next round of larger high tides, coupled 
with increased surge and wave action, will bring a return of 
coastal flood concerns to the Pacific Coast with Sunday's high 
tide. While conditions on the north coast are more marginal, 
confidence is sufficiently high to warrant a high surf advisory 
for Sunday morning's high tide. Conditions will increase more 
sharply Monday onward, as larger waves and lower pressure may 
only combine to push water levels higher along the Pacific coast
through early part of next week.

&&

.LONG TERM /TUESDAY THROUGH SATURDAY/...The aforementioned 
upper-trough and it's associated low will then deepen offshore 
on Tuesday and will bring a more active pattern to western 
Washington as the system swings a number of disturbances across 
the region. Expect multiple rounds of widespread lowland rain, 
mountain snow, and gusty winds starting Tuesday and persisting 
through the second half of the week. The system moving across 
the area on Tuesday will also bring another round of high surf 
to the coast. Additionally, high astronomical tides combined 
with the weather impacts from these systems, will increase the 
threat for coastal flooding - most notably along the Pacific 
Coast during high surf.

Coastal Flooding: Confidence is increasing for the potential of
moderate to major coastal flooding stages being reached along 
portions of the Grays Harbor County coastline during the Tuesday
and Wednesday tides, and high surf may only enhance these 
impacts. The tides increase more significantly for the Strait 
and Puget Sound coastlines later in the week around Tuesday 
onward, and the potential for impacts in these locations will 
need to be closely monitored as we get into the range of total 
water level guidance.

&&

.AVIATION...East to southeasterly flow becoming southwesterly 
on Sunday as an upper-low meanders offshore. Light showers this
evening with a band moving across the region. Later tonight, as
showers progress, ceilings will gradually lower to either a 
low-end VFR or MVFR level, with the lowest ceilings in the 
south interior. VFR conditions are to return by Sunday afternoon
with lingering shower activity. 

KSEA...VFR conditions at the terminal this evening. MVFR
ceilings are favored to return late tonight into early Sunday
morning. They'll remain in place before rising to VFR around
midday. Surface winds becoming SE tonight between 5-10 kt;
trending more southerly and lighter into Sunday. 

&&

.MARINE...A passing system this evening will maintain advisory 
strength winds and steep, wind-driven seas over the coastal 
waters into early Sunday. Expect seas to build to around 12-14 
ft by early Sunday with a westerly swell arriving. 

After this, a very active marine pattern will unfold with a strong 
low offshore sending a series of disturbances across the waters 
during much of the week ahead. Late Sunday will bring widespread 
advisory winds with some potential for low-end gale gusts tomorrow 
night into Monday. Ensemble guidance suggests a better chance of 
gales (40-60%) Monday night into Tuesday over the coastal waters, 
with a 30% chance of storm gusts over the outer coastal waters. Yet 
another disturbance follows late Wednesday with perhaps the best 
chance of seeing storm force winds over the outer coastal waters. 
Meanwhile, seas will build through the week with high confidence in 
seas above 16 ft Tuesday and Wednesday, and around a 40% chance of 
20 ft seas. Even if seas don't build this big, the dominant period 
will be rather long Tuesday and Wednesday, which will lead to 
confused seas as winds increase and mix this swell with shorter 
period, locally generated seas. Cullen

&&

.HYDROLOGY...Wetter systems moving into the region next week 
will start to bring some rises to area rivers. At this time, no 
widespread river flooding is expected, however the Skokomish 
River will likely cross into minor flood stage around midweek 
onward.
