.SYNOPSIS...
Multiple Pacific storms will impact western Washington this 
week and weekend for gusty winds, lowland rain and high 
elevation mountain snow. Large swells will reach the coast on 
Friday.

&&

.SHORT TERM /TONIGHT THROUGH WEDNESDAY NIGHT/...
Our next Pacific frontal system is upon us with rain spreading 
inland this afternoon. The air mass is slightly unstable with a 
chance of thunderstorms across SW WA (mainly this afternoon and 
evening). Expect windy conditions along the coast and North 
interior with south winds gusting to 30-35 MPH. 

Another deep low spins over the NE Pacific on Tuesday and 
Wednesday for another round of gusty winds, lowland rain and
high elevation snow. Winds will be strongest along the coast and
North Interior with south/southeast wind gusts to 40-50 MPH - 
Wind Advisories are in effect. Winds will stay gusty through
Wednesday afternoon then ease Wednesday night. In addition,
minor coastal flooding is possible around the time of high tide
Wednesday morning for the San Juans and western Whatcom county.
33

.LONG TERM /THURSDAY THROUGH MONDAY/...
A trio of strong Pacific systems are poised to impact Western 
WA Friday through Sunday. The main impact will again be strong 
winds, especially over the coast where gusts may exceed 50 MPH. 
We may also see gusty winds to 30-40 MPH in the interior as 
well. These systems will also generate large swells that will 
reach the WA coast Friday with seas over 20 ft possible. 
Rainfall will be heavy at times along the coast and over the 
Olympics. This may bring the Skokomish River close to minor 
flood stage. We are also closely monitoring the storm tide 
predictions as well as a few locations may see minor coastal 
flooding on Saturday. The last system moves in on Sunday - more 
wind and rain - with the weather easing moving into Sunday night
and Monday. 33

.AVIATION...
A frontal system will continue to move across the 
terminals, with widespread rain bringing ceilings. Reduced 
visibilities have been observed with heavier pockets of rain 
down to to 3-5 mi. Overnight the ceilings are expected to lift 
up to low-end VFR, with an additional front passing through late
Tuesday with more rain and lowering ceilings/visibilities back 
down to MVFR. Winds will generally remain out of the southeast 
or southwest, with the strongest winds through Tuesday morning,
and again Tuesday evening. Gusts up to 20-30 kt with higher 
gusts to 35 kt along the coast.

KSEA...VFR conditions likely rebound this evening, but again
lower into MVFR with the next round of rain which will arrive 
around 15z Tuesday morning. Southerly surface winds easing
tonight, but increasing again Tuesday afternoon and evening with
gusts again to 25 kt.

&&

.MARINE...
A series of frontal systems will impact the waters 
through the week. Widespread rain will pass over the waters at 
times, with rates potentially becoming heavy enough to drop 
visibilities significantly for mariners. There is a 20% chance 
of thunder for portions of the coastal waters with the front 
moving through this evening, and again Tuesday afternoon into 
Wednesday. 

Widespread gusty southerly winds will continue for much of the week 
across the majority of the waters. Winds with the front moving 
through Monday afternoon will remain in the 20 to 30 kt range for 
the coastal waters and west Strait of Juan de Fuca, and Puget Sound 
through the Northern Inland Waters (including the east Strait of 
Juan de Fuca and Admiralty Inlet). A stronger system on Tuesday into 
Wednesday will produce widespread and frequent gale force gusts for 
the coastal waters and east strait. Wind gusts will likely remain 
above 20 kt through Friday with another stronger system producing 
additional gale gusts for the coastal waters and east Strait of Juan 
de Fuca, with a similar system on Saturday into Sunday. Models have 
winds finally decreasing below 20 kt early next week.

Seas: 10-12 ft tonight through Tuesday, increasing to 17 to 19 ft 
late Tuesday into Thursday before decreasing to 10 to 14 ft 
Thursday. Models have a significant push up to 20 to 23 ft Friday 
into the weekend with seas decreasing on Monday back down to 10 to 
14 ft.

.HYDROLOGY...
River flooding is not expected through Thursday. 
Atmospheric river hanging up over the Olympics Friday into 
Saturday. Current rainfall forecast for the South Slopes of the 
Olympics in the 3 to 4.5 inch range. Snow levels are not 
exceeding high, 5000 to 6500 feet, but add in the additional 
rainfall leading up to the event this amount of rain will bring 
a flooding threat to the Skokomish River at the end of the week.

River flooding not expected on the remainder of the rivers in 
the area Friday through Sunday. Felton
