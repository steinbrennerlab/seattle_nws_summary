.SYNOPSIS...
An active weather pattern will persist into the end of week as a 
series of disturbances moves through the area. This, coupled with 
high astronomical tides, will continue the threat for coastal 
flooding through the end of the week. Unsettled conditions are
expected to persist into early next week.

&&

.SHORT TERM /TODAY THROUGH FRIDAY/...
A frontal boundary is gradually pushing onshore into Western
Washington this morning as the next system is already quickly
approaching offshore. The overall forecast picture hasn't
changed much with rain and wind picking up along the coast by
early evening as a warm front lifts northward across the area. 
Rain will increase across the interior overnight with windy
conditions developing across the North Interior, San Juans, and
Admiralty Inlet area as well. Strongest winds will be focused
along the coast and across the North Interior with some gusts in
the 50 to 55 mph range, but Puget Sound will become windy as 
well with some gusts around 40 mph as the warm front shifts
inland early Thanksgiving morning. Storm total rainfall with
this next system remains in the same ballpark as previous
estimates with up to 2 inches for coastal areas, 2 to 4 inches
in the Olympics, 2 to 3 inches in the Cascades, and around a
inch for the interior lowlands outside of the Olympic rain
shadow. The trailing cold front will sweep inland Thursday
afternoon with post-frontal showers, perhaps an isolated
thunderstorm, and winds gradually easing. 

Another, weaker system is slated to arrive later in the day on
Friday as it rides over the top of an upper ridge building into
California. As has been the case in recent days, coastal
flooding will continue to be the headline-making hazard as some
of the years highest astronomical tides combine with storm 
surge and high surf to produce some significant tidal anomalies
and impacts to coastal communities. 

&&

.LONG TERM /FRIDAY NIGHT THROUGH TUESDAY/...
Ensembles are advertising a brief break with some weak upper
ridging over the weekend, but, even so, it would be tough to
completely remove any chance of precip for more than about 12
hours. Broad upper troughing returns to the offshore waters by
early next week with another parade of systems marching onshore
into the West Coast.

.AVIATION...Southwesterly flow aloft as showers continue over
western Washington this morning. Conditions mostly VFR at all
terminals early this morning, the exception being HQM, where
MVFR conditions are being observed in rain. Generally, expect
periods of rain throughout the day today with most terminals
remaining VFR. Could see a brief drop to MVFR/IFR in areas of
heavier showers, but with gusty winds today, ceilings should
stay VFR. The next front will arrive late this evening, with
strong winds. Winds will start to pick up after 22z-03z, with 
gusts starting along the coast around 40 to 50 kts. Terminals 
in the interior will see gusts up to 35, even 40 kts through 
the overnight hours. Ceilings will bounce to VFR/MVFR, with
lowered conditions in showers, although gusty S/SE winds should
kept most terminals at VFR.

KSEA...VFR conditions continuing at the terminal throughout this
afternoon with on and off showers. A front will move through
late this evening into Thursday, with strong winds and more
rain. S/SE winds will start to increase around 00z-03z, with
sustained winds 15 to 20 kts, with gusts potentially around 30
to 35 kts at times, lasting throughout the overnight and into 
early morning hours on Thursday. Ceilings will likely fall to
MVFR at times through the late evening, especially in heavier 
batches of rain. Winds will continue to remain gusty through
Thursday morning.

.MARINE...
A low pressure system remains offshore, and will continue to
bring strong winds and elevated seas to the waters today. A
Storm Warning remains in effect for all coastal water
zones today as a front moves in late tonight into Thursday
morning. Gale Warnings also remain in effect for the East and
West Entrance Strait of Juan De Fuca, and the Northern Inland 
Waters. Elsewhere, Small Craft Advisories will remain. Winds
will stay gusty tonight into Thursday morning for most waters,
with a notable difference in wind strength Thursday afternoon,
where additional headlines may be needed for the transition.
Additionally, there is a chance of an isolated thunderstorm or
two Thursday afternoon, mainly over the coastal waters.

An surface high pressure looks to develop over the region on
Friday, which would provide a brief break from winds and seas.
Additional systems over the weekend may drive winds and seas up
again.

Coastal seas generally 15 to 20 feet this morning will continue
to stay in this range throughout the remainder of the day. Seas
will start subsiding late Thursday into Friday to 10 to 15 feet.
Seas will lower even further by late Friday into Saturday, where
we could see seas get below 10 feet through the weekend.

Mazurkiewicz

.HYDROLOGY...
Ongoing rain will bring an additional 2 to 5 inches of rainfall to 
the region, with the highest rainfall totals over the Olympic 
Mountains. While no widespread river flooding is expected, the 
Skokomish River is still on track to reach flood stage mid week, 
topping out into moderate. A river flood watch remains in effect for 
Mason County from Wednesday morning through Sunday afternoon.
