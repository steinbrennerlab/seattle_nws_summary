An upper-low over OR and N CA will dissipate as an upper-ridge
builds into the region tonight. Weak surface gradients, high
pressure, and longer nights will promote low clouds and fog 
development across much of western WA. Visibility could sink to 
a quarter- mile, especially in areas prone to foggy conditions 
such as river valleys. Overnight lows are forecast to drop into 
the upper upper 20s to upper 30s where freezing fog is likely 
for colder spots such as the southwest interior. 

As alluded to, a foggy start to the day on Friday but it'll 
give way to mostly clear skies in the afternoon. The upper-
ridge axis will be just offshore as yet another dry day is in 
store. High temperatures are on track to be slightly below 
average for mid-November with highs in the mid to upper 40s. 
Upper-ridge axis becoming firmly overhead Friday evening as the
pattern gently progresses. Friday night almost a carbon copy of
Thursday's with another round of foggy weather in concert with 
min temps in the upper 20s to upper 30s.

An upper-low will position offshore on Saturday, forming a Rex 
Block as it interacts with a high center in northern BC. This
set up will allow for a sub 990 mb surface low to meander 
offshore the WA coast throughout much of the weekend. PoPs are 
favored to increase on Saturday evening as light lowland rain 
showers and mountain snow showers (snow levels around 2,000 ft) 
return to the forecast. Precipitation amounts are expected to be
not impactful.

Friday night for mostly benign conditions. Can't rule out areas

storm force wind gusts over the coastal waters. Moreover, there
is a 65-75% chance for gales through the Strait of Juan de 
Fuca. Seas also continue to trend upwards with combined seas 
now approaching 20 feet over the outer coastal waters. An active
pattern will persist well into the middle of next week.
