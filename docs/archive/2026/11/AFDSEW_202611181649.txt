.SYNOPSIS...
Upper level low well offshore will move move southeast and
weaken today and tonight. The low will move inland over Oregon
Thursday. Front associated with the low moving into Southwest
Washington later today and tonight. The front will dissipate
late tonight. Upper level ridge building offshore Thursday
afternoon will move over Western Washington Thursday night and
Friday. Another upper level low will develop off the Oregon 
coast over the weekend. That low will dissipate Monday. An 
organized front will reach the area Tuesday.

.SHORT TERM /TODAY THROUGH FRIDAY/...
The latest forecast remains on track this morning with no
updates. Upper level low moving southeast today ending up off 
the Central Oregon coast late this afternoon. Low level offshore
flow with the cross cascade gradient bottoming out around -8 mb
this morning. This will produce some breezy easterly winds in 
the foothills into the afternoon hours. Weakening front 
associated with the low moving into Southwest Washington late in
the day. Reports of light rain as far north as Salem and 
Tillamook with ceilings in the 7000 to 9000 foot range early 
this morning. These conditions will make their way into Western 
Washington with the light rain chances as far north as around 
Everett by late afternoon. Any rain that does reach the surface 
will be light, just a few hundredths. With the cloudy skies 
highs will be pretty uniform, near 50. 

Low not moving much tonight with what is left of the front
dissipating over the southern portion of the area. Chance for 
light rain from about Everett southward in the evening hours. 
Lows in the lower 30s to lower 40s.

Weak upper level low remaining over Western Oregon Thursday.
Like we saw Monday night into early Tuesday with the upper level
low in the vicinity you can't totally rule out showers but most
of the area will be dry Thursday. Cloud cover thinning out
enough overnight for areas of fog to develop over the Southwest
Interior and local river valleys in the morning. The fog will be
gone by afternoon. Highs again near 50. 

Upper level ridge building offshore Thursday afternoon will move
over Western Washington Thursday night into Friday. Dry north
northeasterly flow aloft over the area will clear skies out.
Another round of morning fog Friday. With the clear skies
freezing fog developing over the Southwest Interior early 
Friday morning. Fog dissipating by afternoon. Under sunny skies 
highs again near 50. Lows Friday morning in the upper 20s and 
30s.

.LONG TERM /FRIDAY NIGHT THROUGH TUESDAY/...
Extended models in a little bit better agreement this morning
with the upper level ridge getting pinched off over the area
Friday night and another upper level low stalling off the Oregon
coast for the weekend. Bands of moisture spinning around the 
upper level low moving towards Western Washington. The placement
of where the low stalls will be crucial to the weekend 
forecast. Right now a majority of the ensembles keep the low 
west of 130W. This will mean just glancing blows for Western 
Washington from the moisture bands spinning around the low. If 
the low ends up inside 130W much better chance for showers over 
the weekend. Models also in good agreement that the low 
dissipates Monday and an organized, but negatively tilted front,
arrives Tuesday. For the most part staying with chance pops for
the Saturday through Monday period with likely or higher pops 
Tuesday with the front. 

Snow levels around 2500 feet over the weekend so snow on all the
passes if the Cascades get any precipitation. Snow levels rising
to 3000 to 4000 feet Monday night and Tuesday. The orientation 
of the front Tuesday will produce easterly winds in the passes 
which could trap some cool air near the surface in the passes 
even though the snow level might be above the pass level.

Highs in the mid 40s to lower 50s with lows in the 30s through
the period. 

Felton

.MARINE...
A low pressure system spinning offshore continues to bring elevated 
seas and winds to the coastal waters. A Gale Warning remains in 
effect for the outer coastal waters and Small Craft Advisory for the 
inner coastal waters and West and Central Strait of Juan de Fuca. 
Seas will build this morning to 10-14 feet, then decrease below 10 
feet on Thursday. Grays Harbor Bar is now included in a Small Craft 
Advisory as seas look to build just above 10 feet. As the low 
pressure system continues its southeasterly trajectory into Oregon, 
weak high pressure will build over area waters on Thursday and 
Friday for benign marine conditions. Seas will decrease below 10 
feet and winds will remain below advisory criteria.

The pattern will become more active starting this weekend and will 
continue into next week. A low pressure system offshore dropping 
from the Gulf of Alaska and clip area waters this weekend, which 
will bring elevated seas and winds. At this time, latest guidance 
suggests there is a 70% chance or higher for small craft advisory 
winds over the coastal waters and a 50-60% through the Strait of 
Juan de Fuca. Seas expected to build 10-13 feet. A stronger system 
is set to impact the waters Monday and Tuesday. NBM guidance 
suggests a 70% or higher for gale winds across the coastal waters 
and a moderate (50-65% chance) of gales through the Strait of Juan 
de Fuca. Additionally, guidance is also hinting a 25-35% chance for 
storm force gusts across the coastal waters. Seas look to build 12-
16 feet.
