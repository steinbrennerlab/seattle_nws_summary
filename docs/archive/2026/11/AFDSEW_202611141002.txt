.SYNOPSIS...
Another frontal system will bring a return of rain and windy 
conditions later today into Sunday. A trend toward drier and 
cooler conditions is expected toward the middle portion of next
week as the storm track shifts south of Western Washington.

&&

.SHORT TERM /TODAY THROUGH MONDAY/...
An upper trough axis is moving onshore over Western Washington 
early this morning as another, more vigorous system approaches 
the region just offshore. A combination of high tide and low 
atmospheric pressure will bring a renewed threat of minor 
coastal flooding to the San Juans and Western Whatcom county 
this morning. Seas in excess of 20 feet continue along coast 
beaches. Rain and wind will increase along the coast later this 
afternoon with rain spreading inland this evening into the 
overnight hours. Windy conditions will also develop tonight near
the east entrance to the strait and adjacent areas with some 
gusts of 45-55 mph.

The front will shift east of the Cascades by early Sunday
afternoon with winds gradually easing and post-frontal showers
across the area. A cooler air mass will follow the front...so
there will be enough instability for isolated thunderstorms...especially
near the coast. Snow levels will fall to around 4000 feet Sunday
night, but QPF will be light by this time for just light
accumulation at some of the higher passes. An upper level trough
will remain over the area on Monday for scattered showers mainly
coast and higher terrain and high temperatures back to around
seasonal levels. 

&&

.LONG TERM /MONDAY NIGHT THROUGH FRIDAY/...
Drier conditions return to the area Monday night into Tuesday as
the upper trough shifts eastward. Model solutions are falling
into better agreement regarding the midweek system taking a
further southward track and just brushing the southern portion
of the CWA with some light precip on Wednesday. Ensembles favor
cool and generally dry conditions to finish off next week with
the potential for more active conditions returning thereafter.

.AVIATION...
Southerly flow aloft this morning turning southwest later today as a 
frontal system pushes towards the region. Mostly VFR so far with 
areas of MVFR under heavier shower activity (coast and Olympic 
Peninsula). A vigorous front is slated to arrive this evening along 
the coast before spreading inland as the night progress. Shower 
activity will transition to steadier rainfall as a mix of VFR/MVFR 
conditions become more widespread. Southerly surface winds with 
gusts up to 25-30 kt across the entire area by late tonight. Winds 
will remain breezy into Sunday but less widespread. Areas of MVFR 
conditions will lead into Sunday morning as well.  

KSEA...VFR conditions with vicinity showers throughout the day. The 
next front is slated to arrive tonight with steadier rainfall and 
gusty winds. Surface winds remain southerly, fluctuating between SE 
at times. Gusts up to 25 kt possible late tonight into early Sunday 
morning.

.MARINE...
Numerous headlines remain in place as we're currently in between 
systems. SCA advisories are in effect this morning but as the day 
progresses, strong winds are slated to become more widespread as 
Gales become common across interior waters (minus Puget Sound but a 
SCA at the least may be needed) and Storm Force winds arrive for all 
of the coastal waters. The culprit is a sub 965 mb surface low 
swinging a cold front across the region this evening into the night 
hours. Along with winds, high seas are forecast around 20 ft today 
with a dominant period of 17 to 18 seconds, highly energetic. Seas 
will gradually decrease to 15 to 20 ft by Sunday morning. An 
additional increase to 20 to 25 appears likely in the afternoon. 
South winds will ease a bit on Sunday, but remain elevated 
especially for the Coastal Waters. 


Winds subside further Monday into early Tuesday before increasing 
again going into midweek with a stronger weather system moving 
southwards well offshore. Confidence is leading towards developing 
high pressure for next weekend as benign conditions appear to be on 
the horizon.

.HYDROLOGY...
The potential for flooding continues on the Skokomish River 
with periods of heavy rainfall through the weekend. A Flood 
Watch remains in effect. 

River flooding is not expected on the other rivers in the area 
in the next 7 days at this time.
