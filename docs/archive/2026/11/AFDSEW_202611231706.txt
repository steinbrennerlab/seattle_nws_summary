.UPDATE...
No changes made in this morning's update. Recent KLGX/KATX scans
are indicative of light rain showers and mountain snow streaming
across the area, mainly along the coast and Olympics but a few 
are being observed in more interior portions of W WA. They'll 
continue at times before steadier precipitation arrives by this 
evening. Near-seasonal temperatures are in the forecast with 
highs in the upper 40s to near 50 F. A flurry of headlines 
remain in place including winter weather advisories for the
Olympics and North Cascades, coastal flood advisories, and wind
advisories all either in effect currently or starting later
today. The previous discussion remain below along with an
updated aviation section. 

&&

.SYNOPSIS...
A series of systems will bring widespread precipitation, gusty 
winds, and increase the potential for coastal flooding along 
with high surf throughout much of the upcoming week.

&&

.SHORT TERM /TODAY THROUGH WEDNESDAY/...
Recent radar imagery shows a bulk of showers moving onshore
early this morning, with most of the precipitation focused on
the Olympic Peninsula. An upper level low will continue to
meander offshore, providing shower activity throughout the
early afternoon. A Coastal Flood Warning remains in effect for
the coastline in Grays Harbor county where larger waves and 
lower pressure will most likely bring moderate to major coastal 
flooding, during this morning's hide tide, especially to places
like Westport and Ocean Shores. Additionally, a Coastal Flood
Advisory remains in effect for the north coast and along the
Strait of Juan De fuca, for minor coastal flooding this morning
and afternoon. Snow levels generally hovering around 2000 to 
3000 feet through this afternoon, where notable accumulation's 
are expected in the Olympics, around 4 to 8 inches. A Winter 
Weather Advisory remains in effect until early evening, where 
snow levels will rise to 3000 to 4500 feet. 

A frontal system will start to move into the area late this
evening into Tuesday, spreading widespread rainfall across the 
region. Along with rain, snow levels will gradually rise from 
3000 to 4500 feet late this evening to generally around 5000 
feet into Tuesday. Snow accumulations may be possible at the 
higher passes such as Stevens Pass, generally around 1 to 3 
inches. Areas in the North Cascades, like Washington pass, could
see 4 to 8 inches of snow, therefore a Winter Weather Advisory 
remains in effect through Tuesday.

Additionally, winds will become gusty across the region, with
gusts up to 40 mph likely across areas along the coast, where a
Wind Advisory remains through Tuesday afternoon. Precipitation
will taper throughout the afternoon, with high temperatures in
the low 50s. The tides increase more significantly for the 
Strait and Puget Sound coastlines later in the week around 
Tuesday onward, and the potential for impacts in these locations
will need to be closely monitored as we get into the range of 
total water level guidance.

The active weather pattern continues, with yet another frontal
system moving into the area Wednesday into Thursday with more
batches of widespread lowland rain, breezy winds, and mountain
snow.

&&

.LONG TERM /WEDNESDAY NIGHT THROUGH SUNDAY/...
Latest ensembles
continue to highlight heavier rainfall Wednesday night into 
Thursday, especially over the Olympics, where the Skokomish 
River is forecasted to reach moderate flood stage. Confidence 
continues to increase for the potential of moderate to major 
coastal flooding stages being reached along portions of the 
Grays Harbor County coastline during Wednesday tides, with high 
surf possibly enhancing these impacts. We will continue to
monitor this system as it gets closer with new model guidance.

&&

.AVIATION...
A warm front will pass through the terminals this morning with a low 
pressure centered off the coast. The cold front will follow later 
today with another frontal system trailing into Tuesday. Bands of 
rain will continue to push north this morning, with a break in the 
interior later this morning, and rain continuing along the coast 
into the afternoon. The next system brings more widespread rain 
inland tonight/Tuesday. Visibility reductions down to MVFR are 
possible with the heaviest bands of rain. Ceilings are likely to 
remain VFR through tonight. Mountains likely see lower ceiling 
obstructions. Southeast winds will continue, with most seeing 
5-10 kt, and higher gusts in the mountains and along the coast 
(approaching 30 kt and the Pacific coast seeing 35-40 kt most of
Tuesday). Winds aloft will be stronger and out of the 
southwest, so there will be some potential for wind shear 
(especially Tuesday morning). 

KSEA...VFR conditions anticipated for today, with a 30-40% chance of 
MVFR Tuesday morning from 09Z-12Z. Rain showers with the first round 
will continue through 15Z, with the next wave coming in after 00Z 
this evening. Winds stay out of the southeast 5-10 kt. Few gusts to 
20 kt may mix in (especially Tuesday morning).

.MARINE...
Low pressure will remain offshore for much of the week, with several 
fronts moving through the waters. The first front is moving through 
the waters this morning, and southeast winds will increase through 
the day today into Tuesday with the combo of systems passing 
through. Greatest impact (in terms of wind) remains the outer 
coastal waters, where a storm warning will go into effect for gusts 
up to 55 kt. The inner coastal waters and east Strait of Juan de 
Fuca areas have gale warnings, with  wind gusts up to 40 kt. There 
are also small craft advisories for adjacent areas of the 
central/west strait, northern inland waters and the Admiralty Inlet, 
where some sheltering from land will keep winds down to 20-30 kt. 

Another frontal system will pass through Wednesday into Thursday 
with widespread winds across all waters as winds switch to the south 
and southwest. Once again the coastal waters will have the best 
chance for gusts up to storm force, while the east strait and north 
waters have the chance of seeing gusts up to gale force. Remaining 
waters will likely see small craft impacts. There will also be a 30% 
chance of thunderstorms with activity in the coastal waters. The 
ensembles favor high pressure rebuilding into the end of the week 
and weekend, with Friday being a break from winds before becoming 
breezy again next weekend. 

Seas: 10-12 ft seas Monday will increase to 18 to 22 ft Tuesday 
through Thursday (with periods becoming longer midweek at 16 to 18 
seconds). They will decrease to 10 to 12 ft Friday through the 
weekend. A small craft advisory is also in effect for Grays Harbor 
bar for rough bar conditions, which may become severe midweek as 
steeper seas move closer to the shoreline.

HPR

&&

.HYDROLOGY...
Wetter systems moving into the region through this 
week will start to bring some rises to area rivers. At this 
time, no widespread river flooding is expected, however the 
Skokomish River will likely reach flood stage around midweek and
possibly topping into moderate. A Hydrologic Outlook has been 
issued regarding the rainfall and potential river flooding for 
this timeframe.
