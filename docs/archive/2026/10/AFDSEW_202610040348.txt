.SYNOPSIS...
High pressure building overhead and weak offshore flow will maintain 
mild and dry conditions this weekend. A system to the north will 
flatten the ridge by the beginning of next week, with near-zonal 
flow aloft and onshore flow at the surface maintaining seasonable 
conditions with morning clouds and afternoon sun breaks.

&&

.SHORT TERM /TONIGHT THROUGH MONDAY NIGHT/...

The current upper level ridge will see its axis shift eastward 
over E WA Sunday. At the surface thermally induced surface
trough along the coast tonight will shift inland Sunday. Sunday
the warmest day for the interior with highs in the mid 60s to
mid 70s. Ridging overhead will flatten out into zonal flow
Sunday night. This change will help to facilitate a return to 
onshore flow later Sunday and as such bringing daytime highs 
more in line with seasonal normals Monday...generally in the 60s
for most lowland locations. Overnight lows remain generally 
static, ranging from the upper 40s to lower 50s.

No precip expected in the short term.

18/Felton

&&

.LONG TERM /TUESDAY THROUGH SATURDAY/...
Ridging builds over the eastern Pacific slowly Tuesday and into the 
midweek period, which may allow for daytime highs to rebound a 
handful of degrees back into the upper 60s to lower 70s by Thursday 
and overnight lows remaining consistent with what is expected in the 
short term. The ridge is then forced to retrograde as an upper level 
low from BC drags a frontal system over the CWA Friday and into 
Saturday. As this falls into day 6 and 7 territory, no surprise that 
ensemble members have a wide array of opinions of how well this 
front holds together and how much potential precip it will bring. 
Ensemble mean generally leaning toward light precip with PoPs for 
this time frame generally ranging 20-50 pct. This seems like a 
reasonable solution for now. The main impact from this system will 
be to drag temps solidly back into Climo...if not slightly 
below...as daytime highs will range in the upper 50s to lower 60s by 
Saturday.
