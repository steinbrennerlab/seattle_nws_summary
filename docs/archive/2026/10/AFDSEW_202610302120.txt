.SYNOPSIS...
An upper level low will continue to weaken into Saturday, with

&&

.SHORT TERM /TONIGHT THROUGH SUNDAY NIGHT/...
Recent radar imagery shows a bulk of showers moving in from the
south, with some areas over the Puget Sound seeing continuous
rainfall. High temperatures in the low to mid 50s across much
of the interior. Showers will continue this afternoon before 
decreasing in coverage and intensity by the overnight hours. An
upper level low will start to move southwards and weaken, taking
away the showery influence over western Washington. We'll start
a dry and cool trend heading into the weekend, with afternoon
temps staying in the low 50s. Overnight lows, especially tonight
into Saturday morning, will cool, with areas in the Southwest
Interior seeing below freezing temperatures in the upper 20s and
low 30s. Therefore, the Freeze Watch has been upgraded to a
Freeze Warning for those locations. Crops and sensitive
vegetation may die as a result of temperatures that low, so 
take the proper precautions to protect things like that if 
needed. Besides the cold temperatures, should be dry for any 
trick or treaters. 

Dry and cool conditions through Sunday over the region. 
Overnight low temperatures will be flirting with freezing 
temperatures as well, in the low 30s. 

Mazurkiewicz

.LONG TERM /MONDAY THROUGH FRIDAY/...
Ensemble guidance remains in reasonably good agreement for the first 
half of next week with an upper ridge remaining generally over the 
region for the start of next week. This will maintain mostly dry 
conditions, though a portion of ensemble members bring a weak 
disturbance into the ridge enough to bring some light precipitation 
chances (20-30%) west of Puget Sound. Another weak disturbance 
brings similar chances toward midweek. A more notable shift in the 
pattern becomes more likely late in the week with a stronger front 
approaching Western Washington, but it's worth noting that there's 
significantly increasing spread in the potential evolution of the 
pattern by that time.                        Cullen

&&

.AVIATION...
Another round of steadier rain is rotating through the region this 
afternoon with two main areas - one across central Puget Sound and 
another along the coast. Each of this is bringing fairly widespread 
rain which is lowering ceilings and restricting visibility into the 
MVFR to IFR categories. While there was one small embedded 
thunderstorm in the coastal line, the chances for more lightning 
remains too low (10-15%) for TAF inclusion. After these next few 
areas pivot across the area through this evening, shower activity 
should subside with gradual clearing. However, the light winds and 
clearing skies bring likely IFR conditions in fog/low stratus for 
most terminals late tonight through Saturday morning. Return to VFR 
conditions likely as fog scatters and lifts into the afternoon 
tomorrow.

KSEA...MVFR conditions are expected in steadier rain this afternoon. 
Northerly winds will become east/southeast by late evening, with 
rain tapering early tonight. This should bring a gradual clearing 
trend, but this will result in IFR fog/low stratus Saturday morning 
at the terminal lasting through late morning or midday.      Cullen

.MARINE...Weak surface low pressure over the coastal waters 
will remain in place into Saturday before dissipating as high 
pressure builds across the interior of Western Washington early 
in the coming week. Southeasterly winds over the coastal waters
this afternoon will reach 15 to 20 kts at times, but should
remain under small craft advisory criteria. Winds look to remain
generally benign and light across the area waters through the
first half of next week. 

Coastal seas 4 to 6 feet through the weekend. Seas will build to
slowly build late Sunday into Monday, back around 10 to 11 feet
as a larger swell train approaches.
