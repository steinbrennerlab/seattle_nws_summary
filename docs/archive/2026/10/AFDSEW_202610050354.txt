.SYNOPSIS...
Upper level ridging over western Washington will flatten out as a 
system passes to the north with near zonal flow aloft expected by 
Monday. This will keep temperatures near seasonable normals as well 
as return onshore flow to the area. The pattern of morning clouds 
giving way to afternoon sun will persist at least through mid-week. 
An approaching frontal system for the end of the week could bring 
the next chances for precipitation, however some uncertainty exists.

&&

.SHORT TERM /TONIGHT THROUGH TUESDAY NIGHT/...

Forecast remains consistent in that the upper ridge will continue to 
shift eastward today with the ridge axis making it to the Rockies by 
this evening. Coupled with a trough passing to the north, zonal flow 
aloft will set up overnight, helping to bring a return to onshore 
flow at the surface. Forecast dilemma for the immediate short term 
is will the return of onshore flow exacerbate the morning fog issues 
the area has been having. Currently agreeing with NBM that potential 
for widespread fog likely to be mitigated by the clouds that will 
move in overnight. Sure, they will be stratus-y, but with the ridge 
gone, they should not make it all the way to the ground. Otherwise, 
dry conditions remain in place for the short term.

The import of cooler marine air will shave a handful of degrees off 
of daytime highs, making them a bit more seasonable. Highs both 
Monday and Tuesday are expected to range from the low to upper 60s, 
with a few isolated spots in the SW interior shaking hands with 70.

.LONG TERM /WEDNESDAY THROUGH SUNDAY/...
A building upper level ridge off the coast of California mid-week 
will nudge zonal flow to a little more northwesterly by Wednesday 
evening. This will give daytime highs a bump of about a degree or 
two, ranging in the mid 60s to lower 70s. More widespread 70-plus 
temps can be found on Thursday as this minor ridging gets a little 
more time to work. Coastal sites may not see much change in 
temperatures, however interior lowland spots will range from the 
upper 60s to mid 70s, with the SW interior being on the higher end 
of that scale.

Models remain in agreement about a frontal system moving its way 
through W WA Friday...but all the details remain up in the air. 
Deterministic GFS has pushed FROPA back to Friday evening or Friday 
night while deterministic ECMWF remains consistent, bringing the 
system through Friday afternoon and evening. Most ensemble members 
favoring the ECMWF timing...providing enough sway to cause the NBM 
to reflect this thinking in its forecast. This seems reasonable 
enough for now, but no matter the timing, models seem to be in 
hearty agreement that this front looks to fall apart as it moves 
through. While higher elevations should still see something with 
PoPs coming in at 60-70 pct for the event, PoPs in the lowlands are 
considerably lower, still largely ranging 20-40 pct. It is still 
some time out though, and plenty of opportunity for additional 
variances to emerge. This system will cool temperatures regardless 
if it rains, with daytime highs for Friday and the weekend in the 
mid 50s to lower 60s...with some mid 60s possible, mainly on Friday.
