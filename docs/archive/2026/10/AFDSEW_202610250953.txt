.SYNOPSIS...
A frontal system will continue to bring widespread precipitation and 
breezy conditions to Western Washington today. Unsettled conditions 
are favored into next week. Light showers will linger early next 
week. Another system will arrive midweek, bringing widespread rain, 
mountain snow, and cooler temperatures to the region. 

&&

.SHORT TERM /TODAY THROUGH TUESDAY/...
Rain continues to move across Western Washington early this 
morning as a low pressure system moves southward offshore. 
Latest radar imagery shows pockets of moderate to heavy rainfall
moving across the region. The bulk of the precipitation will 
fall over the coast and the Olympic Peninsula with 1.00-1.50 
inches. Other locations will see amounts ranging 0.10-0.20 
inches east of I-5 and 0.25-0.50 inches west of I-5. Southerly 
winds are expected to become breezy along the coast, San Juan 
Islands, and the Olympic Peninsula this morning and ease late 
tonight. Latest high-res guidance suggests wind gusts up to 35 
mph. High temperatures in the low to mid 50s today. 

Models have come to a better agreement on the projected path of the 
low. The center of the low will remain offshore today and move 
inland Monday morning near the Oregon/Washington border. This will 
result in light showers lingering into Monday. A weak shortwave
will slide down from British Columbia on Tuesday for another 
chance of light precipitation.

29

&&

.LONG TERM /TUESDAY NIGHT THROUGH SATURDAY/...
There is good agreement among models that a low pressure system will 
slide down from the British Columbia coast late Tuesday towards
Western Washington, bringing another round of widespread 
precipitation through at least Thursday. Additionally, a colder 
air mass will accompany this system. Latest guidance suggests 
high temperatures will only peak into the upper 40s to low 50s 
and overnight temperatures will dip into the 30s, with upper 20s
possible in the valleys by Thursday. Snow levels will also 
decrease below 3500 ft with this system, bringing light snow 
accumulations to the passes. Latest guidance suggests the passes
may receive around 4 inches of snow, while the higher mountain 
peaks may see around 7 inches. Models start to struggle Saturday
on the upper-level pattern. Some members have a weak ridge 
building behind the low pressure system, however the majority of
members at this time favor an upper-level trough to remain over
Western Washington.

.AVIATION...
Southwest flow aloft will become southerly later today then 
southeasterly overnight as an upper low offshore moves onshore over 
northwest Oregon. Ceilings will be a mixed bag across the region 
today as bands of precipitation associated with the system offshore 
spread into the region. MVFR ceilings will be mostly confined to the 
coastal areas with primarily VFR for interior terminals. Ceilings 
may briefly drop to MVFR thresholds for interior areas mainly south 
of KSEA this morning.  


KSEA...Mainly lower end VFR expected today with periods of light 
rain moving through the area into this afternoon. Precipitation 
should begin to decrease toward this evening. Surface winds E/NE 4 
to 7 knots.

.MARINE...
A gradually weakening surface low will shift southward over the 
offshore waters today before moving onshore over the northern Oregon 
coast on Monday morning. Small craft advisories remain in effect for 
a combination of wind and seas over the coastal waters into this 
evening. Small craft advisories also remain in effect for easterlies 
in the strait. Winds and seas will be ease and subside tonight as 
the low offshore further weakens and moves ashore over Oregon. 
Another front will reach the waters on Wednesday.
