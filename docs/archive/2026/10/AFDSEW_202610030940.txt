.SYNOPSIS...
High pressure building overhead and weak offshore flow will
maintain mild and dry conditions through the weekend. A system
to the north will flatten the ridge by the beginning of next
week, with near-zonal flow aloft and onshore flow at the 
surface maintaining seasonable conditions with morning clouds 
and afternoon sun breaks.

&&

.SHORT TERM /TODAY THROUGH MONDAY/...
As a weak frontal system continues to progress east of the
Cascades, high pressure will continue to build over western
Washington over the weekend. Weak offshore flow will develop
this morning, helping to burn off a shallow marine layer
expanding inland early this morning. Conditions across much of
the lowlands will become sunny by the afternoon, with highs
reaching the upper 60s to lower 70s. As high pressure aloft
continues, offshore flow will limit a marine push early Sunday
morning, with additional warming from a thermally induced
surface trough bringing highs Sunday afternoon into the 70s for
most inland areas away from the water. Areas along the Cascade 
foothills and valleys will inch closer to 80 degrees, with some 
areas along the Rainier Paradise corridor reaching the lower 
80s.

The upper level ridge will flatten by Monday as a weather system
moves inland over British Columbia, with near-zonal flow
developing over the Pacific Northwest into early next week. This
will bring a return to onshore flow, which will bring a stronger
morning marine push of clouds inland and a return to more
seasonable temperatures in the 60s to lower 70s.

&&

.LONG TERM /MONDAY NIGHT THROUGH FRIDAY/...
A flattened upper level ridge is favored to remain over the
region through much of next week, maintaining near-normal
temperatures and periods of morning clouds with afternoon sun
breaks. Most areas will see high temperatures in the mid 60s to
lower 70s, with a dry week on tap across western Washington.
Ensembles continue to hint at a possible pattern change with
wetter conditions developing by the end of next week, though 
confidence remains low over the timing as deterministic models 
continue to delay the onset of this potential next system. For 
now, expect dry, cloudy, and seasonable conditions with fairly 
little change day to day throughout next week.

conditions improve toward 19-20z to VFR as offshore flow increases. 
An additional round of low clouds/fog is in store overnight Saturday 
into Sunday morning.
