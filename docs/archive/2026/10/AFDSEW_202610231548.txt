.SYNOPSIS...
High pressure aloft will provide another day of dry conditions
for Western Washington today. An area of low pressure moving
southward from the British Columbia coast will bring some rain

.SHORT TERM /TODAY THROUGH SUNDAY/...
No major updates to the forecast have been made this morning.
See below for an updated aviation discussion. 

High pressure aloft will briefly rebuild into Western Washington
today for a dry day ahead and near seasonal temperatures. An
area of low pressure sliding south from the British Columbia 
coast is still slated to begin to affect the area later 
Saturday into Sunday. Models still remain at odds with respect
to its track, but there's enough consensus to warrant likely to
categorical PoPs for the coast and Olympic Peninsula Saturday 
night into Sunday. Some QPF is expected to make it into Puget 
Sound on Sunday, but present thinking is that it will remain 
rather light. Temperatures through the short term period will 
hover within a couple degrees of normal for the final week of 
October. 

&&

.LONG TERM /SUNDAY NIGHT THROUGH THURSDAY/...
Upper troughing remains over the Pacific Northwest Sunday night
into Monday for a lingering chance of precip...mostly focused on
the southern half of the CWA. The upper trough is expected to
shift eastward on Tuesday, but confidence in the forecast trails
off by the middle of the coming week as ensembles begin to
diverge somewhat from what they featured just a day ago. The GFS
suite of solutions have a stronger influence of upper troughing
over the region while the ECMWF height anomalies would keep
broad upper troughing just offshore of California. To be clear,
neither of these is a particularly wet scenario for Western
Washington, but it lends enough uncertainty to retain some 
generic low chance PoPs for much of the CWA...and that's 
exactly what the NBM is using in the forecast. Temperatures are
expected to remain generally seasonal, but chances for some 
sub-freezing overnight lows in the Southwest Interior and 
outlying areas increase toward the middle/later portion of next 
week.
