.SYNOPSIS...An upper low will continue to linger over Western 
Washington through Friday for periodic showers in the lowlands,

.SHORT TERM /TONIGHT THROUGH FRIDAY NIGHT/...
An upper level low continues to hover offshore maintaining 
showers around western Washington this afternoon. Cooler 
temperatures in the low 50s within the post frontal airmass. 
Additionally, there is chance (15-20%) of a thunderstorm or two 
late this afternoon due to the unstable environment. 
Thunderstorm chances greatly decrease early evening as the sun 
sets. Snow showers continue in the Cascades but has greatly 
decreased in coverage since this morning, and will continue as 
the Winter Weather Advisory remains out until 5PM. Most of the 
accumulations will be in and around Mount Rainier. 

The aforementioned upper level low will not move much into
Thursday and Friday, keeping scattered showers around in the
lowlands and light snow accumulations in the Cascades. Overall 
precipitation will trend downwards, but can't rule out stray 
showers at times with most of the area remaining somewhat dry. 
Afternoon temps in the low to mid 50s. 

&&

.LONG TERM /SATURDAY THROUGH WEDNESDAY/...
Ensembles are agreeing in the low weakening into the weekend,
with conditions becoming drier over western Washington. There
may be a shower or two in the Cascades, but overall most of the
interior looks to be dry over the weekend with high temperatures
in the mid 50s. Overnight lows look to become cooler in the low
to mid 30s, with some locations even seeing mid to upper 20s,
particularly in the Southwest Interior. Weak ridging will
allow conditions to likely remain dry heading into midweek.

.AVIATION...
An upper low remains over the region with widespread rain showers 
across the area through the evening. Expect continued breezy winds 
from the south/southeast with gusts to 20 kt this evening. An 
isolated thunderstorm remains possible (15% chance), but more likely 
impacts will be heavy rain and reduced ceilings/visibility under 
stronger showers as they move across the area. Otherwise, little 
change through the night with gradually decreasing showers after 
this evening. Stratus becomes increasingly likely with 30-40% 
likelihood for MVFR ceilings across much of the interior Thursday 
morning, while guidance suggests IFR conditions at KOLM.

KSEA...VFR conditions expected to prevail with gusty southerly winds 
through the evening. Showers remain around the terminal area and 
could bring a brief lowering if these move over the airport. 
Otherwise, winds easing overnight with showers decreasing. The 
potential for MVFR ceilings increases by 12z through the morning 
Thursday.

&&

.MARINE...
Seas have peaked across the coastal waters earlier this morning, but 
remain quite steep with dominant periods around 8 to 9 seconds. As a 
result, a small craft advisory remains in effect for these areas 
through the evening. Expect both the seas to decrease and the 
dominant period to lengthen, so this should be good to expire by the 
end of the night. Elsewhere, conditions on the Grays Harbor bar are 
expected to worsen around a very strong ebb this evening as seas 
steepen and build, so an advisory has been issued here as well.

Looking ahead, seas will likely build early next week back to around 
10 ft with the arrival of a long period swell into the coastal 
waters.
