.SYNOPSIS...
Mostly clear conditions are present this afternoon outside of
some scattered clouds throughout Puget Sound. An area of low
pressure will be dropping south from the British Columbia coast
late Saturday night into Sunday for a round of light rain. Drier
conditions resume by the middle of next week as the low offshore
progresses inland and east. 

&&

.SHORT TERM /TONIGHT THROUGH SUNDAY NIGHT/...
Mostly clear skies this afternoon and evening will give way to
mostly cloudy skies overnight. With low pressure and cooler
temperatures approaching, overnight lows will dip into the mid
30s in the Cascades and adjacent foothills with low 40s
throughout Puget Sound. A frontal system dropping south from B.C
will introduce rain along the coast tomorrow afternoon,
spreading inland overnight into Sunday morning. The bulk of the
rain will be focused along the Olympic Peninsula and generally
west of the Cascades. Up to three quarters of an inch of rain 
is expected along the coast and windward slopes of the Olympics 
with only a few hundredths to a tenth of an inch in the Puget 
Sound region.

Showers will taper off late Sunday morning, leaving cloudy skies
and highs in the mid to upper 50s. 

&&

.LONG TERM /MONDAY THROUGH FRIDAY/...
The pattern is becoming more consolidated in the models for
early next week. It is suggestive of broad troughing remaining
offshore through the middle of the week, which will keep cooler
and cloudier conditions in the forecast. A weak impulse moving
through will generate some slight chance PoPs for the area
Tuesday afternoon through late Tuesday night. Significant rain
is not expected beyond another few hundredths of an inch. Colder
overnight lows will accompany this system and bring temperatures
overnight back down the upper 30s and low 40s. Wednesday 
through Friday look to be more on the drier side as the low and 
associated weak front move inland, leaving the area in an 
onshore flow regime. Afternoon highs will consistently be in the
mid to upper 50s through the week. 

The 8-14 day hazards outlook still suggests a 20-40% chance of
heavier rain, stronger winds and possibly mountain snow in the
October 31 to November 6 timeframe. Stay tuned to the forecast
as more details on emerge on a possibly more active pattern.

.AVIATION...
Generally VFR with some lingering clouds around 3000-4000 feet 
causing localized pockets of MVFR east of the Puget Sound. 
Conditions across western Washington are expected to reach VFR as 
clouds continue to erode into the evening. Mostly clear skies will 
be short-lived, with high clouds building in from the northwest 
later tonight. The increased cloud cover may work to inhibit fog and 
low cloud development for most areas, although IFR/LIFR is possible 
for more fog-prone terminals early Saturday morning including KHQM, 
KOLM, and KPWT. An approaching frontal system will bring increasing 
light showers into western Washington generally north of KPWT-KBFI 
Saturday afternoon, with a frontal system on track to sweep across 
the area late Saturday into Sunday, bringing more widespread 
MVFR conditions in rain.

Surface winds generally northerly around 5-10 kt this afternoon, 
becoming light and variable overnight. Light south to southeast 
winds will develop Saturday in the wake of an approaching frontal 
system.

KSEA...VFR this afternoon with lingering clouds right around 3000 
feet. Clouds expected to gradually erode into the evening, with 
increasing clouds into early Saturday morning. The high clouds will 
likely limit the development of fog and low clouds around SeaTac, 
with VFR expected through the TAF period. Surface winds generally 
northerly 5 to 9 kt this afternoon, becoming light overnight and 
switching to the southeast to southwest early Saturday.

.MARINE...
Northwesterly winds and seas will continue to ease into the evening, 
with SCA conditions winding down over the outer coastal waters. A 
more benign pattern will settle in over area waters into Saturday, 
with waves subsiding to around 4 to 6 feet. 

By Sunday, a low pressure system will move southward over the region 
and bring a frontal system over the waters. Winds and seas look to 
potentially ramp up again with this system, with southeasterly SCA 
winds possible and waves once again encroaching 10 to 12 feet across 
the outer coastal waters Sunday. 

Unsettled conditions are expected to continue into next week, with 
additional systems expected to cross area waters. Seas look to 
remain around 4 to 7 feet with fairly benign winds, though 
conditions next week may change if models continue to lean towards 
bringing in continued weather systems.
