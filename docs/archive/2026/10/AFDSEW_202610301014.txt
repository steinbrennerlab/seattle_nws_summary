.SYNOPSIS...
Weakening upper level low off the north coast will elongate and
continue to weaken through Saturday. What is left of the low

.AVIATION...
An upper level trough will remain over the coastal and offshore 
waters today and Saturday producing south to southwest flow aloft 
over Western Washington. Surface winds at many terminals generally 
light and variable while those reporting winds are mainly seeing 
northeasterly to easterly with speeds ranging 4-8 kts. As the day 
progresses, a slight shift to easterly to southeasterly winds is 
expected, although winds likely will not get beyond the previously 
mentioned 4-8 kts range.

Shower activity over W WA has eased considerably, two bands still 
remain, one moving over the Puget Sound while the other remains just 
off the coast over the nearshore waters. Impacts to terminals have 
been minimal as widespread VFR conditions persist, although 
locations more prone to lower cigs such as OLM and HQM have seen 
conditions erode to MVFR or IFR. As another round of showers is 
expected to pick up this afternoon, widespread MVFR conditions are 
expected to emerge. Activity is expected to taper off in the early 
evening with widespread VFR conditions returning, although some 
isolated examples of MVFR could linger.

KSEA...Ceilings are expected to remain at or above 4000 to 5000 feet 
for much of the remainder of the morning. A general deterioration to 
MVFR ceilings is expected between 16Z and 21Z as showers increase 
once again. Wind discussion above applies.

.MARINE...
Weak surface low pressure over the coastal waters will remain in 
place into Saturday before dissipating as high pressure builds 
across the interior of Western Washington early in the coming week. 
East or southeast winds across the coastal waters and near the west 
entrance to the strait will occasionally reach 15 to 20 knots over 
the next couple of days until the offshore low finally dissipates. 
Otherwise, winds into early next week look to remain rather light 
across area waters. 

Coastal seas generally 4 to 6 feet today and into the weekend. They 
are expected to build back to around 10 feet Monday as another 
larger swell train reaches the waters.
