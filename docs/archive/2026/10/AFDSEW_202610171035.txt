.SHORT TERM /TODAY THROUGH MONDAY/...
Satellite imagery and doppler radar shows weakening front has 
moved into the Northwest Interior in the last hour. Rain along 
the coast is coming to an end while over the interior as of 3 am
the rain is confined to the San Juans and Skagit county 
northward. Temperatures with the cloud cover were in the 50s. 

Front will continue to weaken and move into the Cascades in the
next three hours. Most of the rainfall with the front this
morning will be from Snohomish county northward with just a
chance for showers King county southward. Post frontal 
convergence zone forming quickly between 12z and 15z. Both the 
KUIL-KBLI and KOLM-KBLI gradients have increased from near 1 mb 
to near 2.5 mb in the last two hours. Convergence zone setting 
up over Snohomish and Northern King county will weaken and move 
into the Cascades late morning/midday. Outside of the 
convergence zone not much in the way of cool air behind the 
front leading to minimal post frontal shower activity. Skies 
clearing this afternoon with partly to mostly sunny skies across
the area. With the sunshine highs near or slightly above 
normal, in the upper 50s to mid 60s. 

Not much going on tonight. Zonal flow aloft with surface high
pressure building into the area. Light gradients overnight
brings up the potential for some fog formation early Sunday
morning especially in the fog prone locations like the Southwest
Interior. Lows in the mid 30s to mid 40s. 

Surface high pressure remaining over Western Washington Sunday.
Flow aloft becoming southwesterly with an upper level trough
kicking out of the Gulf of Alaska digging south well offshore.
This will spread some high clouds over the area during the day.
Highs a little cooler with the cool morning to start things 
off, in the mid 50s to lower 60s. 

Upper level trough continuing to dig south Sunday night into
Monday with the associated frontal system moving by to the west
Monday. Rain chances confined to the coastal waters but with the
system in the vicinity plenty of middle and high level cloud
cover by Monday. Lows in the mid 30s to mid 40s. Highs Monday
again in the mid 50s to lower 60s. Felton

.LONG TERM /MONDAY NIGHT THROUGH FRIDAY/...
Models in good agreement with the upper level trough dropping
down the California coast through Tuesday leaving Western
Washington in a lull. Surface gradients going northerly for
another dry day Tuesday with highs near normal, mid 50s to lower
60s. Cool mornings both Tuesday and Wednesday with the coldest
locations near freezing. Another round of frost advisories may
be needed.

Upper level trough approaching the coast Wednesday. Frontal
system associated with the trough still well offshore Wednesday
afternoon. Mostly sunny skies with highs a touch warmer, upper
50s to mid 60s. 

Front slowing down and splitting Wednesday night with what is
left of the system moving inland Thursday for a chance of
showers. Lows in the mid 30s to mid 40s. Highs in the mid to
upper 50s. 

Possible post frontal convergence zone Thursday night. Weak 
upper level trough moving over the area hanging around through 
Friday. Chance of showers Thursday night becoming confined to 
the Cascades Friday. Little change in temperatures from 
Thursday's highs and lows. 

Quick note on the 77 degree day in Seattle Friday. This was the
second warmest day on record for this late in the year. The only
day warmer was the record setting 88 degree day back in 2022.
The warmest days on record from here until March are 74 degree 
days on October 17, 1989, November 3 2010 and November 4, 1949. 
77 degrees is the normal high in Seattle from August 19 through 
25. The normal high for Seattle yesterday is 60 degrees. Felton
