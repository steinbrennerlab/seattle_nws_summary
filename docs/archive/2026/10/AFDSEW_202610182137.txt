.SYNOPSIS...
A trough will be departing south and well offshore tomorrow into
Tuesday, with zonal flow through the middle of the week. The
next few mornings will be on the colder side for potential
frosts, namely in the south Puget Sound and Chehalis Valley. A
weak system is possible late in the week for light rain. 

&&

.SHORT TERM /TONIGHT THROUGH TUESDAY NIGHT/...
Clear skies overhead this afternoon with highs presently in the
mid to upper 50s. High clouds are moving onshore courtesy of the
upper level trough transiting south, but far offshore. Low
temperatures tomorrow morning will be in the mid 30s for areas
including Olympia and south through the I-5 corridor. Mid 40s
are expected elsewhere. Lows decrease a few degrees further into
Tuesday morning for frost potential in the aforementioned areas
but also the Cascades and valleys. Patchy morning fog will 
remain possible through early in the week and will be more 
likely in the valleys and typically fog prone locations. 

&&

.LONG TERM /WEDNESDAY THROUGH SUNDAY/...
Extended models and the ensembles are favoring Gulf of Alaska
troughing to build on the deeper low moving into southern
California Wednesday and beyond. Positioning of the low becomes
less clear by Friday into next weekend but the indication as of
now isn't for much in the way of impactful weather. Some light
rain may be possible Thursday into Friday. Snow levels during
that time come down to around 4,000-4,500 feet, so some light
snow flurries may be possible at these elevations and higher if
precipitation does materialize.

.AVIATION...
Southwesterly flow aloft is expected tonight into Monday as an upper 
trough digs southward offshore. Surface flow is northerly or weakly 
offshore today. Increasing high level moisture later tonight is 
expected to limit areal coverage of low clouds on Monday morning 
with the exception of more fog prone locations in the Southwest 
Interior and extreme southern Puget Sound.

KSEA...VFR conditions will continue for the remainder of the 
afternoon and evening. High and mid level moisture spreads in 
the region later today and tonight ahead of upper trough digging 
southward offshore. The expectation remains that this will limit fog 
development tonight and will keep low ceilings/vis out of upcoming 
TAF for now. Surface winds N/NE 7 to 10 knots becoming light and 
variable late tonight into Monday A.M.

.MARINE...
A low pressure system passing southward well offshore will keep 
winds northerly or weakly offshore into Monday. Weak high pressure 
will remain across the waters into the middle of the coming week for 
rather quiet conditions. A larger swell train near 10-12 feet 
generated by active weather well offshore is expected to reach the 
coastal waters around Thursday along with an increase in onshore 
flow. A front may reach the waters toward the end of the week.
