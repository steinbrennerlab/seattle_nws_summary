the pattern overnight and the current forecast remains on track.

Previous discussion...An upper level low continues to hover 
offshore maintaining showers around western Washington this 
afternoon. Cooler temperatures in the low 50s within the post 
frontal airmass. Additionally, there is chance (15-20%) of a 
thunderstorm or two late this afternoon due to the unstable 
environment. Thunderstorm chances greatly decrease early evening
as the sun sets. Snow showers continue in the Cascades but has 
greatly decreased in coverage since this morning, and will 
continue as the Winter Weather Advisory remains out until 5PM. 
Most of the accumulations will be in and around Mount Rainier. 

The aforementioned upper level low will not move much into
Thursday and Friday, keeping scattered showers around in the
lowlands and light snow accumulations in the Cascades. Overall 
precipitation will trend downwards, but can't rule out stray 
showers at times with most of the area remaining somewhat dry. 
Afternoon temps in the low to mid 50s. 

&&

.LONG TERM /SATURDAY THROUGH WEDNESDAY/...Ensembles are 
agreeing in the low weakening into the weekend, with conditions 
becoming drier over western Washington. There may be a shower or
two in the Cascades, but overall most of the interior looks to 
be dry over the weekend with high temperatures in the mid 50s. 
Overnight lows look to become cooler in the low to mid 30s, with
some locations even seeing mid to upper 20s, particularly in 
the Southwest Interior. Weak ridging will allow conditions to 
likely remain dry heading into midweek.

.AVIATION...An upper level trough will remain just offshore of 
Western Washington into Thursday producing southwest flow aloft.
The air mass is somewhat unstable. Showers will continue across
the region...most numerous near the coast. With low level winds
easing overnight, areas of MVFR ceilings are expected to form 
toward daybreak before lifting again Thursday afternoon.

KSEA...A general decrease in shower activity is expected overnight 
with most activity south and west of the terminal. As surface winds 
ease somewhat overnight, an general lowering of ceilings is expected 
with periods of higher end MVFR 12Z-18Z before lifting back to VFR 
Thursday afternoon. Surface winds E/SE easing to 5 to 7 knots this 
evening and continuing into Thursday morning.

.MARINE...Seas have peaked across the coastal waters earlier 
this morning, but remain quite steep with dominant periods 
around 8 to 9 seconds. As a result, a small craft advisory 
remains in effect for these areas through the evening. Expect 
both the seas to decrease and the dominant period to lengthen, 
so this should be good to expire by the end of the night. 
Elsewhere, conditions on the Grays Harbor bar are expected to 
worsen around a very strong ebb this evening as seas steepen and
build, so an advisory has been issued here as well.

Looking ahead, seas will likely build early next week back to around 
10 ft with the arrival of a long period swell into the coastal 
waters.
