.SYNOPSIS...
High pressure aloft will provide another day of dry conditions
for Western Washington today. An area of low pressure moving
southward from the British Columbia coast will bring some rain 
to the region later Saturday into the early portion of next 
week. Drier conditions are expected to return by the middle of 
next week as the low pressure system moves eastward. 

&&

.SHORT TERM /TODAY THROUGH SUNDAY/...
High pressure aloft will briefly rebuild into Western Washington
today for a dry day ahead and near seasonal temperatures. An
area of low pressure sliding south from the British Columbia 
coast is still slated to begin to affect the area later 
Saturday into Sunday. Models still remain at odds with respect
to its track, but there's enough consensus to warrant likely to
categorical PoPs for the coast and Olympic Peninsula Saturday 
night into Sunday. Some QPF is expected to make it into Puget 
Sound on Sunday, but present thinking is that it will remain 
rather light. Temperatures through the short term period will 
hover within a couple degrees of normal for the final week of 
October. 

&&

.LONG TERM /SUNDAY NIGHT THROUGH THURSDAY/...
Upper troughing remains over the Pacific Northwest Sunday night
into Monday for a lingering chance of precip...mostly focused on
the southern half of the CWA. The upper trough is expected to
shift eastward on Tuesday, but confidence in the forecast trails
off by the middle of the coming week as ensembles begin to
diverge somewhat from what they featured just a day ago. The GFS
suite of solutions have a stronger influence of upper troughing
over the region while the ECMWF height anomalies would keep
broad upper troughing just offshore of California. To be clear,
neither of these is a particularly wet scenario for Western
Washington, but it lends enough uncertainty to retain some 
generic low chance PoPs for much of the CWA...and that's 
exactly what the NBM is using in the forecast. Temperatures are
expected to remain generally seasonal, but chances for some 
sub-freezing overnight lows in the Southwest Interior and 
outlying areas increase toward the middle/later portion of next 
week.

.AVIATION...
Northwesterly flow aloft in the wake of an exiting trough expected 
to remain that way throughout the day and into Saturday. Surface 
winds at all terminals light and variable this early morning with 
northerly winds expected to develop by this afternoon with speeds 
ranging 4-8 kts. Light and variable winds expected again tonight.

Much like the past few days, most of W WA remains VFR with ridging 
building in. The clear skies that come with that are also 
responsible for the islands of MVFR/IFR/LIFR thanks to low cigs and 
fog. Some mid-level clouds helping to prevent fog at some of the 
terminals, however the terminals that have been the most problematic 
this past week...OLM and BLI...showing enough hints that while 
IFR/LIFR may not be the dominant conditions, confidence is not there 
to remove fog entirely. Thus, will opt for TEMPO groups to cover 
worst case scenario. Any low cigs that develop should be gone by 
18Z, with widespread VFR conditions returning.

KSEA...Cigs expected to sink a little further, but generally will 
remain between 4000-5000 ft. Forecast confidence on cigs any lower 
than this still hovers around 30 pct, so will leave that out of the 
forecast for the time being. Clearing skies expected around 18Z with 
VFR persisting into overnight tonight.

.MARINE...
Northwesterly winds will ease throughout the day, although SCA level 
winds still expected throughout this morning. Seas expected to 
behave similarly. That said, SCA looks on track with no alterations 
to end time needed. Nearshore seas will take a little longer to 
ease, so rough bar SCA for Grays Harbor also on track.

Winds and seas will ease and subside by this evening/tonight, with a 
more benign pattern settling in over area waters into Saturday. By 
Sunday, a low pressure system will move southward over the region, 
though some uncertainty remains over the storm track. Winds and seas 
look to potentially ramp up again with waves once again encroaching 
on 10 ft or a little higher Sunday. Will wait for current headlines 
to expire before contemplating next round, if needed. Seas expected
