.MARINE...
Areas of fog have have developed across portions of the interior 
waters and along the Strait of Juan de Fuca this morning and have 
become dense in spots. Fog may bring visibilities down to 1 nautical 
mile or less through the mid-morning hours, before scattering by 
late morning/early afternoon. 

Offshore flow will increase this morning as a thermal trough 
develops along the coast. Winds will peak this morning, but remain 
offshore through early Friday as the trough gets pushed inland by an 
incoming frontal system. This system will force winds to switch to 
onshore on Friday and increase Friday night into Saturday. The 
strongest winds will be most likely through the Strait of Juan de 
Fuca where there is a 35-45% chance of seeing up to low-end gale 
force gusts, but other locations such as the outer coastal waters 
and through Puget Sound and Hood Canal have 40-70% chances of seeing 
wind gusts over 20 kt. Winds look to decrease as the system departs 
Saturday evening. Winds look to remain relatively benign Sunday 
through early next week as systems are favored to pass by well 
offshore.

Seas remain 4 to 6 ft through tonight, increasing to around 8 to 
near 10 ft Friday through the weekend. The forecast gets more 
uncertain on Monday as a low pressure system moves through well 
offshore, but could generate and send larger waves to the coastal 
waters. The most likely forecast remains in the 8 to 10 ft range, 
but waves could get as large as 13 to 16 ft depending on the exact 
track of this system. EPS and GEFS show a 20-30% chance of seas 
exceeding 12 ft Monday into Tuesday.
