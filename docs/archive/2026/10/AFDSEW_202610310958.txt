.SYNOPSIS...
Drier conditions are expected over the weekend as an upper low
offshore weakens and moves inland well to the south of the region.
An upper level ridge moving into the region on Sunday will keep
conditions dry and temperatures seasonable across western Washington
into Monday. The first half of next week will remain mostly 
dry, with chances of precipitation increasing mid to late week 
as a series of systems approaches the Pacific Northwest.

&&

.SHORT TERM /TODAY THROUGH MONDAY/...
An upper level low offshore will continue to bring showers and
some isolated thunderstorms to the coastal waters through the
early morning hours. Showers will gradually taper across the 
waters through the day as the low weakens and exits the area. 
Otherwise, expect cool conditions for areas across the interior 
this morning as skies continue to clear. Morning lows are expected
to approach freezing for many areas south of the Sound, but 
particularly across the Chehalis River Valley. Areas of clearing
have also started to see some fog development overnight, so 
could even see some spots of dense fog this morning. Morning 
temperatures will remain a couple degrees warmer for areas north
of Seattle- however, with temperatures expected to dip into the
mid 30s, could see some areas of frost development as well. 
Freeze warnings remain in effect for areas along the Chehalis 
River Valley and frost advisories remain in effect for areas 
north of Seattle and along the foothills of the Cascades through
mid morning. Any areas of fog are expected to scatter by late 
morning.

Overall conditions will be drier today and Sunday as the low 
offshore exits the area and an upper level ridge moves into the 
region. Cool overnight lows in the 30s will bring areas of 
additional frost development to the area on Sunday morning and 
moisture in the low levels will likely promote another round of
fog development. High temperatures will be in the low to mid 
50s both days. 

The upper level ridge axis will push further inland on Monday,
maintaining mostly dry conditions for western Washington for the
start of next week. A few ensemble members do continue to hint 
at a weak frontal system brushing the region, which may bring 
some light precipitation chances (roughly 20 to 30%) to the 
coast through the day. Morning lows will be in the 30s and 
afternoon highs will be in the low to mid 50s.

&&

.LONG TERM /MONDAY NIGHT THROUGH FRIDAY/...
Conditions largely look to remain drier through the first part 
of next week, though a splitting system may bring some light 
rain to portions of the area on Tuesday. A return to a more 
unsettled pattern looks more favorable mid to late week as a 
series of systems approaches western Washington. At this time, 
a weak system looks to move across the area on Wednesday, with a
stronger system on Friday looking to bring more notable chances
of precipitation. However, it is worth noting that there still 
remains a decent spread amongst ensemble solutions for the second
half of the week. Morning lows will be in the mid 30s to mid 
40s and afternoon highs will be in the mid to upper 50s
throughout the period.

.AVIATION...
Surface low pressure will continue to dig southward off the shore, 
with high pressure rebuilding in southern B.C. over the weekend. 
Showers with isolated thunderstorms will continue over the coastal 
waters through the morning. Remaining interior terminals will dry 
out today. The clear skies in the interior has led to areas of frost 
and/or fog this morning. The densest fog has been concentrated in 
the south sound/south interior with quarter mile visibilities from 
Shelton to Chehalis. Additional areas of fog and mist are present 
north of this and will linger through roughly 18Z as high clouds 
linger through the day. Additional fog/mist development tonight is 
likely, and may be more widespread in coverage in the Puget 
Sound/south interior. Light northeast to southeast winds at 5 kt or 
less will prevail.

KSEA...Conditions drying out today. A patch of mist/fog has 
developed to the east of the terminal near Renton, and north near 
BFI. With winds remaining calm or light out of the east, the lowest 
visibilities will likely remain outside of the terminal, though 
there remains the potential for some of it to spread close to SeaTac 
this morning through 18Z. High clouds will pass through the day with 
another round of fog appearing more likely tonight/Sunday morning.

.MARINE...
A surface low will begin to dig southward across the waters today. 
High pressure will rebuild inland. This will lead to a brief window 
of offshore flow through the weekend, with gustier winds to 20 kt 
this morning in the coastal waters diminishing quickly. Shower and 
thunderstorm activity will continue in the coastal waters through 
the morning, but will wind down through the day. Hazardous winds are 
not expected at this time through Wednesday, when the next system 
passes through. Another system may also skirt the waters late next 
week/weekend.

Seas will hover at 4-6 ft through the weekend, but build back to 10 
to 11 ft Monday as swells increase out of the west. Seas drop back 
down to 6-8 ft Tuesday through Thursday but build back to 10-14 ft 
late Thursday through next weekend.
