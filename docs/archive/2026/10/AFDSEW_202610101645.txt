.SYNOPSIS...
Cool upper level low moving over the area this morning will 
slowly move southeast into Eastern Oregon by Sunday morning. 
Weak convergence over the Puget Sound ending this morning. Dry 
northeasterly flow aloft tonight through Monday with high 
pressure nosing into British Columbia. Ridge moves over Western 
Washington Tuesday and weakens with a weather system moving by 
to the north Tuesday night into Wednesday. Upper level ridge 
building over Western Washington from the south Wednesday night 
through Friday. Low level flow turning offshore producing a 
couple of warm days the latter part of next week.

.SHORT TERM /TODAY THROUGH MONDAY/...
The latest forecast remains on track this morning with minor
updates to the Aviation and Marine sections below. Cool upper 
level low moving over the area today. Weak convergence producing
the shower activity will dissipate this morning. Low not strong
enough to create any shower activity this afternoon. Main 
result of the low overhead will be the cool daytime temperatures
with highs in the mid and upper 50s. Seattle high Friday was 58
degrees, coolest high since May 15th ( 51 degrees ). Going for 
57 degrees in Seattle today. 

Upper level ridge nosing into British Columbia combining with
the upper level low drifting southeast producing dry
northeasterly flow aloft tonight and Sunday. Skies clearing 
later this afternoon into tonight. With the clearing skies lows 
dropping into the mid 30s to mid 40s. The frost advisory from 
midnight to 8 am Sunday for portions of King, Pierce, Lewis and
Thurston counties remains in effect. Highs warming back to near
normal, upper 50s to mid 60s.

Upper level low sinking south Sunday night and Monday. Upper
level ridge remaining over Southern British Columbia with a
continuation of the dry northeasterly flow aloft. Low
temperatures even cooler Sunday night/Monday morning with most
places in the 30s. The last time Olympia had a low of freezing
or below was May 17th ( 32 degrees ). Another frost advisory 
will be issued covering a larger area, most areas south of 
Seattle plus the Hood Canal and North Coast for Monday morning. 
Highs Monday again in the upper 50s to mid 60s. Felton

.LONG TERM /MONDAY NIGHT THROUGH FRIDAY/...
Upper level ridge axis moving south over Western Washington
Monday night into Tuesday. Weak atmospheric river settling up
Tuesday night into Wednesday aimed at Vancouver Island. A few of
the ensemble solutions have Western Washington on the southern
edge of this feature. Enough solutions to have a chance of rain
in the forecast Tuesday night and Wednesday. Lows in the mid 
30s to lower 40s Tuesday and in the 40s Wednesday. Highs both 
days in the upper 50s to mid 60s. 

Upper level ridge building in from the south Wednesday night
into Thursday. 500 mb heights rising to the lower to mid 580
dms. Low level flow turning offshore as a front stalls well off
the coast. Right now it looks like a couple of warm days for
Western Washington with the warmer locations getting into the
lower 70s. Nowhere near records but still at least 5 degrees
above normal. Lows in the 40s. 

Felton

Northwest flow aloft will become northeasterly late today as an 
upper level low sinks southward over the Pacific Northwest. A mix of

.MARINE...
The small craft for the interior coastal waters has been extended 
this morning through 11 AM, as observations have shown seas have 
receding slower than expected and continue to hover around 10 feet. 

A weak surface low over the coastal waters this morning will shift 
southward today. High pressure building over the interior of British 
Columbia will lead to increasing northerly or offshore flow tonight 
into Sunday. Winds will be briefly enhanced in the Northern Inland 
Waters near the San Juans and off the coast in the central and 
southern coastal waters zones, with potential for wind gusts to 
approach Small Craft criteria tonight into early Sunday morning.

Winds ease early next week as high pressure over interior British 
Columbia weakens and broad surface ridging remains centered well 
offshore.  Another frontal system will reach the waters Tuesday 
night into Wednesday. 

Coastal seas of 10 to 12 feet will subside later today and are then 
expected to remain under 10 feet well into the upcoming week.
