.SYNOPSIS...
Showers remain possible over western Washington into Saturday as low 
pressure moves through the area. Winds and seas will also slowly 
ease during this time. Overnight lows this weekend may fall enough 
for localized frost and freeze potential. The next front to impact 
the area expected late Tuesday into Wednesday for more rain and 
breezy winds.

.SHORT TERM /TONIGHT THROUGH SUNDAY NIGHT/...
Current radar showing two main bands of precipitation this evening: 
the first along the Whatcom/Skagit county border and another 
stretching over the southern half of Snohomish county down into 
portions of northern King. This second one mainly due to a lingering 
convergence zone. Latest satellite shows low clouds favoring most of 
the CWA east of the Sound , although there is also another patch 
from the northern coast stretching eastward to the Olympics.

Current weather is in line with inherited forecast and as such, no 
need for any evening updates. For additional forecast details, 
please refer to the Previous Discussion section below.  18

From Previous Discussion...Rain has mostly ended across western 
Washington with the exception of a post-frontal convergence zone 
centered over Seattle and extending east into the Cascades. This 
feature will slowly shift south and eastward and weaken this evening 
as the winds slowly ease. Winds across the area remain in the 10-20 
mph range with gusts between 20-35 mph. Winds are peaking now and 
will slowly ease this evening. High temperatures today in the upper 
50s to low 60s will cool into the upper 30s to mid 40s.

Another weak low pressure system will move across the area on
Saturday, keeping chances for scattered showers in the forecast
through tomorrow afternoon. High temperatures remain seasonably
cool, into the mid to upper 50s. Skies look to clear out 
Saturday evening. As the low moves south, offshore flow will 
begin to pick up Saturday evening with breezy northeasterly 
winds from western Whatcom county and through the San Juans with
gusts up to around 25 mph. Elsewhere, across southwestern 
Washington, clear skies and mostly light winds Saturday night 
will promote efficient cooling, with lows reaching from the mid
40s in the metro area to mid 30s in the sheltered valley 
locations. Our first frost advisory for the season has been 
issued for portions of the SW WA Cascades and the middle 
Chehalis river valley. The upper reaches near Olympia and 
Chehalis could potentially get fog, so there is greater 
uncertainty there. The winds would be the limiting factor for 
the foothills and valleys farther north into King and Snohomish 
counties, but trends will have to be monitored for expansion of 
the frost advisory.

Sunday looks to be drier and sunnier as high pressure builds
back in over the area. Breezy north/northeasterly winds look to
continue with gusts up to 20 mph, especially over the waters.
Highs in the upper 50s to low 60s. Cool conditions continue
Sunday night with the potential for more frost.

.LONG TERM /MONDAY THROUGH FRIDAY/...
Ensembles continue to highlight drier but cooler conditions
into Monday. Morning lows Monday will likely reflect the
previous couple of nights, in the mid to upper 30s for most
areas. Could see potentially another round of frost and freeze
potential, particularly in the Cascade valley and southwest
interior locations. Otherwise, Monday will be mostly dry with 
high temperatures in the low 60s. A weak system looks to cross 
over the area Tuesday into Wednesday for a round of light rain. 
Beyond the middle of the week, there is uncertainty in the 
pattern but generally, guidance indicates that a return to high 
pressure and mild conditions is favored.

.AVIATION...
Post-frontal onshore flow tonight with westerly flow over western 
WA. Showers are mainly within the convergence zone over Snohomish 
and King counties this evening. However, we will see increasing 
shower activity overnight and into Saturday as an upper low moves 
overhead. Most of this activity will be over the Cascades. Ceilings 
will be a mixed bag with VFR and MVFR in showers. Drier weather 
Saturday night as the upper low shifts to the E/SE. 33

.MARINE...
Strong onshore flow continues this evening behind a frontal passage 
that occurred early this morning. Small Craft Advisories remain in 
effect most of the interior waters except Puget Sound, and the 
Central/Eastern Strait of Juan de Fuca where Gale Warnings remain in 
effect. Winds through the interior waters are peaking now and will 
ease below thresholds this evening.

Over the coastal waters, Small Craft Advisories continue through mid-
day Saturday. Winds will slowly ease overnight into Saturday 
morning, but seas look to remain above 10 feet through mid-day 
Saturday. Seas will remain choppy and locally-wind driven with the 
dominant period being in the 8-10 second range.

A weak low pressure system will move through on Saturday, behind 
which winds will turn offshore Saturday evening. Winds will briefly 
be enhanced slightly in the Northern Inland Waters near the San 
Juans and off the coast in the central and southern coastal waters 
zones, with potential for wind gusts to approach Small Craft 
criteria overnight into early Sunday morning.

Winds ease next week as high pressure builds back in from well 
offshore. The next low pressure system look to move across the area 
Tuesday night into Wednesday, but there remains some uncertainty in 
timing as this is later than previously forecast.

After Saturday, seas look to remain in the 5-8 ft range Sunday 
through the first part of next week.
