.SYNOPSIS...
A low pressure system will descend from British Columbia tonight 
into Sunday, bringing precipitation to Western Washington.

.SHORT TERM /TODAY THROUGH MONDAY/...
High clouds starting to stream overhead across Western Washington 
early this morning. Temperatures continue to drop as well, with
the latest observations showing temperatures in the low 30s to 
low 40s. A frontal system associated associated with a low 
pressure system just off the coast of British Columbia will move
south into Western Washington this evening. Light precipitation
is expected with this system, with rain reaching the coastline 
this evening and then filling into the interior by Sunday 
morning. The bulk of the precipitation is expected along the 
coast and the Olympics. The parent upper level low is expected 
to continue its southward track Sunday into Monday. The majority
of models favor the center of the low pressure system moving into
Oregon, which should render drier conditions for the region. 
However, shall center of the low track more towards the 
Oregon/Washington border, it may keep light showers in the
forecast for Monday.

.LONG TERM /MONDAY NIGHT THROUGH FRIDAY/...
Conditions look to remain generally unsettled into Friday. 
There is general agreement among ensemble guidance a broad upper
level trough will remain situated over the region Tuesday with 
a low pressure system to the north. Models are now trending 
wetter on Wednesday and Thursday, as the low pressure system 
moves southward. Cooler temperatures expected next week, with 
the coldest temperatures expected late in the week; high 
temperatures in the low to mid 50s and low temperatures in the 
low 30s to low 40s.

.AVIATION...
Westerly flow aloft will shift southwesterly and increase today 
as an upper trough digs southward into the Washington offshore 
waters. Areas of LIFR low clouds and surface visibilities 
below 3SM along the coast and across the Southwest Interior will 
dissipate by around 18Z as high and mid level moisture increases 
areawide ahead of the incoming system. Ceilings will lower to 
MVFR in light rain at times along the coast and across the far 
North Interior by around 00Z. After low clouds dissipate 
this morning, ceilings are expected to remain VFR across the 
interior from around KPAE southward into this evening. 

KSEA...Confidence in VFR conditions this morning remains fairly high 
as temp/dew point trend and arrival of high clouds with incoming 
system limit any low cloud development. High and mid level clouds 
will lower and thicken through the day, but are expected to remain 
well within VFR thresholds. Surface winds southeasterly generally 7 
knots or less this morning are expected to back to a more E/NE 
direction later this afternoon and evening as offshore system gets a 
little closer to the region.

.MARINE...
A surface low southwest of Haida Gwaii will shift southward today 
into tonight and an associated occluded front will clip the coastal 
waters on Sunday morning. Increasing easterly or southeasterly 
gradients will lead to small craft advisory winds across the coastal 
waters as well as the Strait of Juan de Fuca. Seas will build to 10 
to 12 feet over the coastal waters late today into Sunday. The 
surface low is expected to move onshore over northwest Oregon 
Sunday night into Monday with winds easing and seas subsiding back 
below 10 feet.  Another frontal system will shift southward into 
area waters on Wednesday.
