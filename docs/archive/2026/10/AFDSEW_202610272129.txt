.SYNOPSIS...
Troughing will persist over the Pacific Northwest
the remainder of today with unsettled conditions and periodic 
showers. A frontal system will arrive tonight into Wednesday 
for steadier rainfall and mountain snow. An upper low then 
lingers over Western Washington into late week for showers and
cooler temperatures. 

&&

.SHORT TERM /TONIGHT THROUGH THURSDAY NIGHT/...
Ongoing shower activity will continue throughout most of the
area this afternoon and evening, ahead of an approaching frontal
system. High temperatures this afternoon generally in the mid to
upper 50s across the interior, with low 50s for areas along the
water. Showers will turn into more steadier rainfall starting
tonight and continuing into Wednesday morning. Southwest winds
will also increase later this evening into Wednesday, peaking
between 20 to 30 mph generally in the areas of the Southwest
Interior through Shelton. As rainfall becomes steadier, we will
also see the first widespread mountain snow event of the season
unfold. A Winter Weather Advisory continues for all of the
Cascade zones, with snow levels 4000 to 4500 feet dropping to
3000 to 3500 feet by Wednesday morning. Snowfall accumulations 
generally around 4 to 8 inches for Stevens Pass - with 
Snoqualmie pass seeing around 1 to 3 inches. Areas in the 
Southern Cascade Zone such as White Pass and Paradise will 
likely see higher amounts, around 6 to 12 inches of snow. Those 
traveling will want to be aware of the conditions and weather by
checking with WSDOT for updates. 

Upper level troughing will continue Wednesday into Thursday for
spotty showers and cooler temperatures, with high temps ranging
in the low to mid 50s for most areas. 

&&

.LONG TERM /FRIDAY THROUGH TUESDAY/...
Ensemble guidance continues to show troughing across the Pacific
Northwest through Friday, which keeps scattered showers in the
forecast. Snow levels hover around 2500 to 4000 feet on Friday,
so could continue to see light snow accumulations in the
Cascades. The upper level trough looks to slowly move out of the
area over the weekend, with a drying trend over the weekend into
early next week. High temperatures look to remain in the mid 
50s over the weekend. with overnight lows approaching low to 
mid 30s. Will continue to monitor the threat of frost or freeze 
conditions.

.MARINE...
Northwesterly winds along the outer coastal waters will linger into 
Wednesday, with wind gusts approaching 25 kt at times and a SCA 
still in effect until late tonight. Seas will be between 8-9 feet 
overnight. Another frontal system will move into the waters tonight 
into Wednesday, increasing winds and seas across the area. 
Increasing onshore flow will bring a brief push of small craft 
westerly winds to the central and eastern Strait late this afternoon 
into this evening. Southerly winds are picking up along the Puget 
Sound waters this afternoon, with latest guidance highlighting a 60-
80% chance of gusts exceeding small craft criteria particularly in 
the waters from Seattle south.

Winds will ease again across the area waters on Wednesday as the 
accompanying surface low drops into the area and tracks southward 
along the coastal waters. This low will allow for flow to turn back 
offshore, with offshore flow persisting the second part of the week. 

Sea conditions slowly subsiding back to 4-6 ft on Thursday and 
remain that way into the weekend.
