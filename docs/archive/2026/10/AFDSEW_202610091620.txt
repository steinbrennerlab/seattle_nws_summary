.SYNOPSIS...
A frontal system will be moving inland this morning, bringing
rain, breezy winds and elevated seas to the area today. Cooler
temperatures are expected to follow this front through the
weekend and into early next week. Overnight lows over the
weekend may be enough for localized frost and freeze potential.
The next front to impact the area looks to arrive on Tuesday for
more rain and breezy winds. 

&&

.SHORT TERM /TODAY THROUGH SUNDAY/...
A cold front continues to traverse western Washington this
morning. The bulk of the precip has moved through most of the
interior, with showers over the Cascades continuing. With snow 
levels today remain between 5000 and 5500 feet, with a dusting 
of snow still probable in the higher terrain of the North 
Cascades. 

Breezy westerly to southwesterly winds will impact much of the
area during the morning. A concurrent westerly push through the
Strait of Juan de Fuca will transition the winds through Puget
Sound from westerly to northerly by late this morning. This also
means that there will likely be convergence zone activity
setting up early this afternoon in central/south King County.
Outside of convergence zone activity, expect the showers to
become more scattered through the afternoon and evening hours.
The strongest gusts (25-35 mph) look to be primarily focused in
the northern inland waters, and the Pacific coast. However,
there is around a 40-50% chance of wind gusts greater than 35
mph between Port Angeles and Port Townsend, potentially as far
east as Whidbey Island this evening. Winds region-wide will be
easing tonight into Saturday. 

Cooler temperatures are in store behind this front for the
weekend for both the highs and lows. Highs will be in the upper
50s to low 60s and overnight lows will be in the upper 30s to
low 40s. The mid to upper 30s are most favored in the Cascades
and valley locations. Frost and freeze potential is still in the
forecast particularly for Sunday and Monday morning. Those with
agricultural interest or those with otherwise sensitive
vegetation will want to monitor the forecast.

.LONG TERM /SUNDAY NIGHT THROUGH THURSDAY/...
Sunday and Monday night will be cold, with daytime highs
continuing in the low 60s. This seems to be a fairly steady
expectation through the middle of the week. That said, the
models are suggestive of another front moving in Tuesday into
Wednesday which could bring about another round of rain and
breezy winds. Beyond the middle of the week, there is
uncertainty in the pattern but they indicate generally that a
return to high pressure and mild conditions is favored.

.AVIATION...
A frontal system will move through the area this morning. Post 
frontal convergence zone setting up over the Central Puget Sound
this afternoon will bring MVFR/IFR ceilings. The convergence
zone will dissipating this evening with an overall improvement
to VFR. Another convergence zone will set up Saturday morning 
over the Central Sound for a brief period of MVFR/IFR
conditions. Southwesterly flow aloft this morning becoming 
northwesterly this afternoon and northeasterly Saturday morning.

LIFR/IFR ceilings this morning will lift to MVFR this afternoon
and VFR eventually this evening. Ceilings in the convergence 
zone lowering back down to MVFR Saturday morning. 

KSEA...MVFR ceilings this morning will slowly lift back to to
VFR late afternoon. Latest guidance indicates a convergence zone
developing near the terminal later this afternoon (19z-22z)
which could bring a period of lowered conditions MVFR/IFR.  
Southerly winds this morning 10 to 15 kt will switch to 
northerly 6 to 10 kt this afternoon. Winds coming back around to
southeasterly 6 to 10 kt this evening.

.MARINE...
A front has moved inland into Washington this morning. A weak 
surface low will move in behind the front tonight and Saturday. 
High pressure will build back into the coastal waters Sunday 
with lower pressure inland. Another system will possibly arrive 
Tuesday night.

Low-end gale force winds continue across the coastal waters,
with gales expected to develop later this morning in the Central
and Eastern Strait of Juan de Fuca through this evening. Small 
craft advisory winds over the remainder of the waters except the
Puget Sound through this evening. Small craft advisory winds 
continuing over the coastal waters later this evening through 
Saturday.

Seas over the inner coastal waters building to 9 to 12 feet
later today and tonight subsiding to 7 to 10 ft Saturday
morning. Seas over the outer coastal waters 9 to 14 feet today
into tonight subsiding to 8 to 11 feet Saturday.
