.SYNOPSIS...
Upper level low over Eastern Oregon combined with upper level
ridge over Central British Columbia giving Western Washington
dry northeasterly flow aloft through Monday. The ridge will move
over Western Washington Tuesday. Atmospheric river moving 
inland north of the area Tuesday night and Wednesday. Upper 
level ridge building Thursday will move east Friday. Low level 
offshore flow Thursday and Friday producing above normal 
high temperatures. Next system possibility moving into the area
Friday night into Saturday.

.SHORT TERM /TODAY THROUGH TUESDAY/...
Satellite imagery shows mostly clear skies with a patch of 
stratus from about Renton to Everett. Folks over Northern 
Washington got a good view of the aurora between midnight and 2 
am. Temperatures at 3 am/10z were in the mid 30s to near 50. 

Light flow in the lower levels this morning with patchy fog
developing before sunrise. The stratus and patchy fog will
dissipate by mid morning leaving sunny skies over the area. High
temperatures near normal, upper 50s to mid 60s. 

Little change in the pattern tonight into Monday with dry
northeasterly flow aloft and light flow in the lower levels.
Monday morning will be the coldest morning since May with lows
in the 30s and lower 40s. A frost advisory has already been
issued for most of the area south of Seattle plus the Hood Canal
and North Coast. Plenty of sunshine Monday with highs again in
the upper 50s to mid 60s. 

Upper level ridge to the north moving over Western Washington
Monday night into Tuesday. Slight increase in onshore flow
overnight plus some high clouds keeping lows in the mid 30s to 
mid 40s. High clouds remaining over the area during the day
Tuesday with highs in the mid 50s to mid 60s. Cooler high
temperatures to the north with thicker high cloud cover. Felton

.LONG TERM /TUESDAY NIGHT THROUGH SATURDAY/...
Atmospheric river aimed at Vancouver Island and Central British
Columbia Tuesday night into Wednesday. Slight chance of rain
along the coast and near the Canadian border. Lows in the 40s 
and highs in the 60s. 

Models in good agreement with a strong upper level ridge 
building Wednesday night and Thursday. 500 mb heights in the mid
to upper 580 dms by Thursday night. Low level flow turning
offshore warming highs into the 60s and lower 70s. Lows 
remaining in the 40s. 

Upper level ridge moving off to the east Friday with low level
offshore flow continuing. Friday looks to be the warmest day
with highs in the mid 60s to mid 70s. No records expected Friday
with October 16th in 2022 being one of the warmest October days
on record ( Seattle high that day was 88, the second warmest 
October day ). Lows in the 40s and lower 50s. 

Upper level ridge well to the east by Friday night. This opens
the door for a weakening warm front to possibly reach the area 
Friday night into Saturday. For now just chance or slight chance
pops. Highs much cooler, in the lower to mid 60s. Felton

.AVIATION...
Northeast flow aloft continues over Western Washington today as the 
region remains between an upper trough east of the Cascades and an 
upper ridge centered well offshore. Low level moisture and light 
surface winds have led to areas of LIFR conditions in fog and low 
clouds around Lake Washington as well as outlying river valleys. Low 
clouds will lift and scatter by around 18Z. VFR is expected areawide 
this afternoon with valley fog and low clouds reforming again 
tonight. 

KSEA...Valley fog is forming east of the terminal this morning. The 
general expectation is that it will not impact the terminal 
directly, but it will be in the vicinity through 18Z or so. 
Otherwise, VFR is expected through the day ahead. Surface winds 
light E/NE this morning will become northerly and rise to 7 to 11 
knots this afternoon.

.MARINE...
High pressure over the interior of British Columbia will lead to 
northerly or weak offshore flow today. The ridge over interior 
British Columbia will weaken tonight for light winds across area 
waters. A weak frontal system will move across the waters late 
Tuesday night into Wednesday. High pressure rebuilds across the area 
on Thursday with offshore flow developing. Seas are expected to 
remain below 10 feet across the coastal waters through the period.
