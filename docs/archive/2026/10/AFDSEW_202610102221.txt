.SYNOPSIS...
A low pressure system will slowly move southeastward tonight, 
with scattered showers tapering off by this evening. High 
pressure building overhead will bring drier conditions and near-
normal temperatures into early next week. Cooler and unsettled 
conditions may return mid-week with a series of systems moving 
inland aimed at Vancouver Island. High pressure will return by 
the end of the week, allowing for drier and warmer conditions 
Thursday and Friday.

.SHORT TERM /TONIGHT THROUGH MONDAY NIGHT/...
A low pressure system will slowly meander southeastward into 
Sunday, with wrap around moisture maintaining light shower 
activity across the Pacific Northwest through the evening. Most 
areas across western Washington will see high temperatures shy 
of 60 degrees today. As the upper low moves southeastward, dry 
northeast flow aloft filling in behind will bring in cooler air
with lows expected to dip into the mid 30s to mid 40s. This 
will likely allow frost to form early Sunday morning for 
portions of the southern Cascade foothills and valleys and areas
south of the Olympics, and a Frost Advisory remains in effect 
for Sunday morning.

Weak ridging will build into British Columbia on Sunday with
continued dry northeast flow aloft over western Washington, 
bringing slightly warmer daytime temperatures in the mid 50s to
lower 60s. Skies will be mostly clear Sunday into Sunday night,
with even cooler overnight temperatures generally in the 30s for
most areas. Frost will likely be more widespread early Monday
morning, and a Frost Advisory remains in effect for most areas
south of Seattle and along the Hood Canal and North Coast for 
Monday morning.

The upper level ridge axis over British Columbia will shift 
southward over the Pacific Northwest on Monday, bringing more
warming to the region. Highs will peak near normal under sunny
skies, reaching the upper 50s to mid 60s.

&&

.LONG TERM /TUESDAY THROUGH SATURDAY/...
Cloud cover will increase over western Washington on Tuesday as
a weak atmospheric river brings ample moisture to British 
Columbia. While the majority of the precipitation is expected to
stay north, chances for rain increase on Tuesday along the 
coast and over higher terrain. The decaying atmospheric river 
system is set to shift southward late Tuesday into Wednesday, 
bringing increasing chances for rain across western Washington. 
However, the path of the atmospheric river remains fairly 
uncertain at this time, with many ensemble members keeping 
western Washington on the dry side.

A high pressure ridge will then build into the Pacific Northwest
on Thursday as another atmospheric river system traverses well
to the north. This will bring a few days of warming and drying,
with low level offshore flow and highs reaching the lower 70s by
Friday. Some uncertainty remains over how long the high pressure
will remain in place, with potential for troughing to break down
the ridge by the weekend and bring in some more rainfall and 
cooler temperatures.

.MARINE...
A low pressure system over the area will move southwards today. High 
pressure building over the interior of British Columbia will lead to 
increasing northerly or offshore flow tonight into Sunday. This will 
result in enhanced winds over the Northern Inland Waters near the 
San Juan Islands, for which a Small Craft Advisory has been issued. 
Additionally northerly winds will increase across Puget Sound Sunday 
afternoon, with the 70-80% possibility for wind gusts to exceed 21 
kt. Winds will ease early next week as high pressure over British 
Columbia weakens and broad surface ridging remaining offshore. A 
weak frontal system will move across the waters late Tuesday night 
into Wednesday. 

Seas will hover around 6-9 feet this weekend, decreasing to 4-6 feet 
early next week. Seas will briefly build on Wednesday but will 
remain below 10 feet.
