.SYNOPSIS...
A weather system offshore will drift southwards 
into Monday and move onshore across NW Oregon resulting in

increased lowland rain and mountain snowfall into late week. 
Cooler temperatures next weekend.

.SHORT TERM /TONIGHT THROUGH TUESDAY NIGHT/...
Steadier precipitation continues this afternoon over portions 
of the Cascades and coast as a low pressure system offshore 
slowly drifts southwards. Unsettled conditions with periodic 
showers continue through Monday as the system weakens and moves 
onshore into NW Oregon by Monday afternoon. Snow levels remain 
near 5000 feet for high elevation mountain snowfall in heavier 
showers. Otherwise, winds will slowly subside for breezy areas 
of the coast and Admiralty Inlet into tonight. Highs remain in 
the 50s for most areas this afternoon and again Monday.

Troughing persists over the Pacific Northwest on Tuesday, with
lingering moisture and a few showers over the Cascades
throughout the day. The next frontal system also approaches from
BC later Tuesday into Tuesday night bringing increasing
precipitation and locally breezy winds. Highs continue in the
mid 50s Tuesday.

&&

.LONG TERM /WEDNESDAY THROUGH SUNDAY/...
The aforementioned
frontal system moves across Western Washington Tuesday night
into Wednesday. This looks to be the first noticeable mountain
snowfall event of the season as colder air filters into the area
with this system. Snow levels during the bulk of the
precipitation ranges between 3500 to 4500 feet, allowing for 
snow at the higher elevation Cascade Passes. NBM probabilities 
for greater than 6 inches of snow (24 hours ending 500 PM 
Wednesday) is approximately 70% at Heather Meadows (Mt. Baker), 
15% at Rainy Pass, 20% at Stevens Pass, and 90% at Paradise, 
with most of the snowfall between Tuesday evening into early 
Wednesday. Will need to monitor the need for any headlines for 
this event, both due to early season, and higher snowfall 
amounts above 4500-5000 feet. Otherwise, lowland rainfall is 
expected on Wednesday, with a 15% probability for thunderstorms 
for most of the lowlands. 

The associated upper low will then deepen offshore Thursday and
linger over the WA/OR waters into late week for continued 
unsettled conditions. Ensembles are generally consistent the 
troughing digs even further southwards late Friday into next 
weekend, lowering the precipitation potential. However, cooler 
temperatures are then expected over the weekend, and will need 
to monitor any frost or freeze concerns during the morning. JD

.MARINE...
A gradually weakening surface low over the offshore 
waters will continue to make its way southward tonight before 
moving onshore over the northern Oregon coast on Monday. Small 
craft advisories remain in effect for a combination of wind and 
seas over the coastal waters through tonight. Small craft 
advisories also remain in effect for east/southeasterlies along 
the Strait of Juan de Fuca. Winds will ease tonight and flip 
back to onshore on Monday as the low weakens and continues 
southward. 

Another front will reach the waters on Wednesday as another low 
moves into the region and southward along the coastal waters. 
This system will likely bring the next round of headlines to 
portions of the area waters as well as the return of offshore
flow to the waters through the second half of the week. 

Seas will continue to hover between 9-12 ft today and will gradually
subside to 3-6 ft on Monday. Seas will then briefly build back 
up to 7-9 ft near midweek, before subsiding back to 4-6 ft on 
Thursday and persisting between this range over the weekend.
