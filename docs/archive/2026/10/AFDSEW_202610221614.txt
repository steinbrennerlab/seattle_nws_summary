.SYNOPSIS...
A dissipating frontal system will spread some clouds across
Western Washington today along with cooler temperatures. High
pressure briefly rebuilds into the region on Friday before an
area of low pressure brings a chance of rain to the area over
the weekend into early next week. Dry conditions return areawide
by the middle of next week.

&&

.SHORT TERM /TODAY THROUGH SATURDAY/...
No major updates have been made to the forecast. Please see
below for an update to the aviation and marine sections of the
discussion. 

A weak front will fall apart as it comes ashore today. We'll 
see a few clouds and cooler temperatures today, but that'll be 
about the extent of it. High pressure briefly rebuilds into the 
area on Friday for a little more in the way of sunshine, but 
cooler temperatures (really, just more seasonal) will remain in 
place. 

Forecast uncertainty starts to ramp later Saturday and beyond.
Anomalously strong upper ridging building into southeastern
Alaska over the weekend will aid in cutting off an upper low 
sliding southward down the British Columbia coast. And the models 
continue to really struggle with the path of the low. In a general 
sense, the ECMWF suite of ensembles are more aggressive with 
bringing some precip to the area and the GFS less so. The NBM is 
something of a compromise and spreads at least a chance of rain to 
the northwest half of the CWA by Saturday evening. 

&&

.LONG TERM /SATURDAY NIGHT THROUGH WEDNESDAY/...
Most of the forecast area retains at least chance PoPs Sunday
into Monday as the models keep an upper trough over the region
before eventually kicking it eastward. Beyond that, eerily 
quiet conditions are slated to return. Ensemble 500 millibar 
height plots from the Euro, GFS, and Canadian at days 6 through 
8 keep anomalously strong upper ridging over the Gulf of Alaska 
and broad troughing just offshore of California. That's a recipe
for a pretty quiet period of weather here in Western 
Washington.

.AVIATION...
Areas of IFR to LIFR this morning in lingering fog, with some areas 
along the central Puget Sound maintaining VFR conditions out of the 
low stratus and fog. A weak frontal system will bring light showers 
to the region this morning, with persisting northerly winds 10 kt or 
less. Conditions will likely improve by the afternoon today after 
the weak frontal passage, with all terminals expected to return to 
VFR between 18z-21z. Models suggest MVFR to low end VFR ceilings may 
linger south and east of the Puget Sound this afternoon, though 
confidence is low with dry northerly flow. Calm conditions overnight 
will likely result in another round of MVFR to IFR/localized LIFR in 
low stratus and fog development early Friday morning. Surface winds 
early Friday morning expected to shift southeasterly, switching back 
to northerly Friday afternoon.

KSEA...VFR with a FEW002 cloud deck floating near the terminal. 
Conditions are expected to remain VFR this morning and into the 
afternoon. Low end VFR ceilings (4000-5000 feet) may linger after 
the passage of a weak frontal system this afternoon, though with dry 
northerly flow, this may not materialize. Surface winds generally 
northerly around 10 kt today, lightening overnight and switching 
southeasterly early Friday morning. Fog potential remains low early 
Friday morning with VFR conditions favored for now.

.MARINE...
Wind and seas will both increase today as a front moves inland. 
Highest wind and seas will be over the Coastal Waters where a Small 
Craft Advisory remains in effect. Expect building seas to 10-15 ft 
Thursday night. An eye will need to be kept on the western two-
thirds of the Strait, as speeds there may get close to SCA criteria, 
but expecting that they will remain just under. Also for monitoring, 
seas at Grays Harbor Bar may also get close to rough, however 
forecast keeps waves peaking in the 7-9 ft range, again, just shy of 
criteria. 

Winds and seas will ease and subside on Friday. The flow will turn 
offshore this weekend as a deeper surface low spins south over the 
offshore waters. The low will weaken as it moves inland early next 
week.
