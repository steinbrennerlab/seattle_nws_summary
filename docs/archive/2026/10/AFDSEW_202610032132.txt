.SYNOPSIS...
High pressure building overhead and weak offshore flow will maintain 
mild and dry conditions this weekend. A system to the north will 
flatten the ridge by the beginning of next week, with near-zonal 
flow aloft and onshore flow at the surface maintaining seasonable 
conditions with morning clouds and afternoon sun breaks.

&&

.SHORT TERM /TONIGHT THROUGH MONDAY NIGHT/...
Most of W WA has seen clear skies for much of the tail end of the 
morning and the bulk of the early afternoon hours. As such, 
temperatures in these areas are on track with the 
forecast...generally in the mid 60s to lower 70s. However, there are 
still areas where low-level clouds are clinging tenaciously: the 
shores of the eastern Strait around Port Angeles, the Chehalis 
valley and the I-5 corridor stretching from Tacoma to Everett. 
Tacoma has since started to warm quickly, bouncing to 66 in the past 
hour...while Seattle and Everett continue to report temps in the 
upper 50s to lower 60s as of 1 PM PDT. Both satellite and a look out 
the WFO window indicates that these clouds will continue to break up 
and as such there is still some room for warming today. However, 
afternoon highs probably going to run 2-4 degrees cooler than 
forecast.

The current upper level ridge will see its axis shift eastward 
tonight and over E WA Sunday. Once this happens, any ridging in 
place overhead will flatten out into zonal flow. This change will 
help to facilitate a return to onshore flow and as such bringing 
daytime highs more in line with seasonal normals...generally in the 
lower to mid 60s for most lowland locations. This could result in 
high temps Sunday being a degree or two cooler than yesterday in 
most locations. However, for locations that were clouded over today, 
Sunday may be either on par with today or perhaps a degree or two 
warmer. The persistent pattern of morning clouds/afternoon sun 
however could prove to offset this. Ultimately, sided with the 
majority and trended the forecast down a degree or two from today, 
with temps generally in the mid 60s to around 70. Monday sees a 
touch more cooling with lowland temps now fully confined in the mid 
to upper 60s. Overnight lows remain generally static, ranging from 
the upper 40s to lower 50s.

No precip expected in the short term.

.LONG TERM /TUESDAY THROUGH SATURDAY/...
Ridging builds over the eastern Pacific slowly Tuesday and into the 
midweek period, which may allow for daytime highs to rebound a 
handful of degrees back into the upper 60s to lower 70s by Thursday 
and overnight lows remaining consistent with what is expected in the 
short term. The ridge is then forced to retrograde as an upper level 
low from BC drags a frontal system over the CWA Friday and into 
Saturday. As this falls into day 6 and 7 territory, no surprise that 
ensemble members have a wide array of opinions of how well this 
front holds together and how much potential precip it will bring. 
Ensemble mean generally leaning toward light precip with PoPs for 
this time frame generally ranging 20-50 pct. This seems like a 
reasonable solution for now. The main impact from this system will 
be to drag temps solidly back into Climo...if not slightly 
below...as daytime highs will range in the upper 50s to lower 60s by 
Saturday.
