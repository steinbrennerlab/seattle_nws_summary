.SYNOPSIS...A frontal system will continue to bring widespread 
precipitation and breezy conditions to Western Washington today.
Unsettled conditions are favored into next week. Light showers 
will linger early next week. Another system will arrive midweek,
bringing widespread rain, mountain snow, and cooler 
temperatures to the region. 

&&

.SHORT TERM /TODAY THROUGH TUESDAY/...Steadier precipitation 
continues to move from Puget Sound east into the Cascades with
scattered showers along the coast and offshore. Rainfall amounts
over the last 24 hours have ranged from 0.50-1.50" along the
majority of the Olympic Peninsula, and 0.20-0.50" for Puget
Sound, with lower amounts into the Cascades. Showers at times
will continue through today with a system lingering offshore.
Southeast winds remain a bit breezy at times along the coast,
San Juan Islands, and Admiralty Inlet into the afternoon with
gusts up to 25 to 35 MPH. High temperatures in the low to mid 
50s today. 

Models have come to a better agreement on the projected path of the 
low. The center of the low will remain offshore today and move 
inland Monday morning near the Oregon/Washington border. This will 
result in light showers lingering into Monday. A weak shortwave
will slide down from British Columbia on Tuesday for another 
chance of light precipitation.

JD/29

&&

.LONG TERM /TUESDAY NIGHT THROUGH SATURDAY/...There is good 
agreement among models that a low pressure system will slide 
down from the British Columbia coast late Tuesday towards 
Western Washington, bringing another round of widespread 
precipitation through at least Thursday. Additionally, a colder 
air mass will accompany this system. Latest guidance suggests 
high temperatures will only peak into the upper 40s to low 50s 
and overnight temperatures will dip into the 30s, with upper 20s
possible in the valleys by Thursday. Snow levels will also 
decrease below 3500 ft with this system, bringing light snow 
accumulations to the passes. Latest guidance suggests the passes
may receive around 4 inches of snow, while the higher mountain 
peaks may see around 7 inches. Models start to struggle Saturday
on the upper-level pattern. Some members have a weak ridge 
building behind the low pressure system, however the majority of
members at this time favor an upper-level trough to remain over
Western Washington.

.AVIATION...Southwest flow aloft will become southerly later 
today then southeasterly overnight as an upper low offshore 
moves onshore over northwest Oregon. Ceilings are a mixed bag

associated with the system continue to move across the region. 
MVFR ceilings will be mostly confined to the coastal areas with 
primarily VFR for interior terminals. Ceilings may briefly drop

KSEA...Mainly lower end VFR expected today with periods of rain

.MARINE...A gradually weakening surface low will shift 
southward over the offshore waters today before moving onshore 
over the northern Oregon coast on Monday morning. Small craft 
advisories remain in effect for a combination of wind and seas 
over the coastal waters into this evening. Small craft 
advisories also remain in effect for easterlies in the strait. 
Winds and seas will be ease and subside tonight as the low 
offshore further weakens and moves ashore over Oregon. Another 
front will reach the waters on Wednesday, likely bringing the 
next round of more widespread headlines to the area waters. 

Seas between 9-12 ft today will gradually subside towards 4 to 
6 ft on Monday.
