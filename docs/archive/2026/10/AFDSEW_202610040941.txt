.SYNOPSIS...
High pressure overhead and a thermal trough shifting inland
today will provide one more day of mild temperatures. A system
to the north will flatten the ridge to start off next week, with
near-zonal flow aloft and onshore flow at the surface 
maintaining seasonable conditions with morning clouds and 
afternoon sun breaks throughout the week. Chances for rain
increase by next Friday, though confidence remains low.

&&

.SHORT TERM /TODAY THROUGH TUESDAY/...
Despite high pressure overhead forcing light offshore flow,
lower levels have not quite dried out enough to prevent another
round of low stratus expanding across the lowlands this 
morning. Although slightly drier, low clouds may potentially 
linger into the early afternoon once again today. While 
temperatures today will once again depend on the timing of the 
eroding stratus, a thermal trough shifting inland will aid in 
warmer temperatures. Highs across the lowlands are favored to 
reach the low to mid 70s, with some areas along the Cascade 
foothills and valleys reaching the upper 70s to even lower 80s. 

This brief warm-up will be short-lived, however, as the upper 
level ridge flattens tonight as a weather system moves inland 
over British Columbia. This will bring a return to onshore flow 
as near-zonal flow develops aloft, bringing more seasonable 
temperatures alongside morning low clouds and afternoon sun 
breaks. There will be little change day to day between Monday 
and Tuesday, with dry conditions, highs generally in the mid 
60s to lower 70s, and lows in the upper 40s to lower 50s.

&&

.LONG TERM /TUESDAY NIGHT THROUGH SATURDAY/...
Much of next week will bring more of the same as zonal flow 
aloft and onshore flow at the surface maintain near- normal 
temperatures and morning clouds. There will be little change day
to day through Thursday, with highs once again in the mid 60s 
to lower 70s and lows in the upper 40s to lower 50s.

Forecast models have come into slightly better agreement over
cooler and wetter conditions on Friday as a trough passes over
the Pacific Northwest. However, models continue to hint that the
incoming frontal system and associated precipitation may break
apart as the system moves into western Washington, which would
by no means bring a wash out. Chances for rain increase Friday
afternoon into Saturday, though the intensity and coverage of
the potential incoming rainfall remain uncertain at this time.

.MARINE...
One final round of offshore flow today as a thermal trough 
forms along the coast. Winds will turn onshore again Sunday 
night into Monday as low pressure shifts inland and surface high
pressure builds offshore. Onshore winds will be strongest 
through the Strait of Juan de Fuca as a SCA remains in effect 
for the central/eastern zones. The NBM has a 50-80% chance of 
SCA gusts being met while the REFS is a bit more bullish with 
70-90% chance, increasing forecaster confidence. Westerly pushes
are favored throughout the upcoming week of varying strength. A
more organized system appears favorable towards the week's end.
