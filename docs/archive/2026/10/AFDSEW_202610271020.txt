.SYNOPSIS...
Upper level trough moving through the area today. Upper level
low moving south over British Columbia today and tonight will 
veer southwest off Vancouver Island Wednesday. The low will 
drift south Wednesday night and Thursday ending up off the 
Washington coast Thursday night. The low will remain off the 
coast Friday then weaken and move inland Saturday. Upper level 
ridge building offshore Sunday moving over Western Washington 
Monday.

.SHORT TERM /TODAY THROUGH THURSDAY/...
If we are going to be dealing with slow moving upper level lows
into the first part of next year it is going to be a long
forecasting season. 

Satellite imagery shows small circulation just off the North
Oregon coast early this morning. Doppler radar has showers
spinning around the low over the Central and Southern Coast
spreading into the Southwest Interior. With the cloud cover
temperatures in a narrow range at 3 AM/10Z, mid 40s to lower 
50s. 

Small upper level low opening up into a trough and moving mostly
into Northwest Oregon this morning with the highest pops over
the southern half of the area. Upper level low over Northern 
British Columbia this morning moving south during the day. Vort 
lobe spinning out of this feature over mainly the northern 
portion of the area this afternoon producing more showers. Snow 
levels in the 4500 to 5000 foot range confining snow showers to
the higher elevations. Highs in the lower to mid 50s.

Upper level low to the north moving over Vancouver Island by
late tonight. Showers rotating around the low moving through 
the area overnight. Air mass cooling with snow levels lowering 
to 3500 to 4000 feet. Up to 4 inches of new snow in the
Cascades overnight. Higher amounts possible from around Mount 
Rainier southward. Lower snow totals in the Olympics, less than
2 inches. Lows in the upper 30s to mid 40s.

Upper level low not moving much Wednesday with showers rotating
around the low continuing to move through Western Washington.
Snow levels lowering slightly to 3000 to 3500 feet. Up to
another 4 inches of new snow in the Cascades with slightly
higher amounts possible again Mount Rainier southward. Highs in
the lower to mid 50s. Winter weather advisory for the Cascades
tonight and Wednesday looks good and will be continued with 
this forecast package.

Little change in the forecast Wednesday night into Thursday as 
the upper level low hangs out off the Washington coast. Flow 
aloft becoming southwesterly ( versus westerly tonight and 
Wednesday ) as the low drifts slowly south. This will reduce the
orographics over the mountains lowering the precipitation 
amounts. The air mass will still cool with snow levels 2500 to 
3500 feet so most of the passes will continue to see light snow 
with another couple of inches of new snow. Lows mid 30s to lower
40s. Highs in the lower to mid 50s.

Upper level low hanging out off the Washington coast through
Friday night will finally weaken and shift inland Saturday.
Pops slowly decreasing Friday and Friday night with just chance

Upper level ridge building offshore Sunday moving inland Monday.
Atmospheric river on the backside of the ridge aimed at the

Highs in the lower to mid 50s warming into the mid and upper 50s
Monday. Lows in the mid 30s to lower 40s dropping to the upper

.AVIATION... 
Northwesterly flow aloft as upper level troughiness remains in place 
over the Pac NW. Surface winds mostly southwesterly with speeds less 
than 5 kts with some areas of light and variable. More pronounced 
southwesterlies expected by late morning/early afternoon for all 
terminals with speeds generally increasing to 5-10 kts with some 
terminals possibly seeing wind gusts up to 20 kts.

Wide selection of cigs in place this morning. Majority of obs show 
VFR conditions, however some terminals in the south Sound area, 
including Sea-Tac, are reporting MVFR to IFR conditions. Cigs will 
continue to lower throughout the early morning with widespread MVFR 
to IFR conditions expected by 12-14Z. Cigs begin to recover by late 
morning/early afternoon with generally VFR conditions returning, 
save for those terminals that favor lower cigs, where clouds may 
only lift to high-end MVFR. Showers will remain a contributing 
factor to lower cigs throughout the day, with scattered activity 
expected in the morning becoming more widespread this evening. Will 
begin to reflect increased chances of precip during this time frame 
with 12Z issuance. As rainfall becomes more prominent, widespread 
MVFR conditions expected to return by tonight.

KSEA...Conditions continue to deteriorate this morning with latest 
special ob reporting high-end IFR conditions as opposed to model-
advertised low-end MVFR. Will likely favor the latter as the 
dominant condition for 12Z update with TEMPO group reflecting the 
former. As southwesterly winds increase to 4-8 kts by 12-14Z, this 
should help cigs to lift a little bit more, nudging upward into low 
to mid MVFR. As speeds reach their peak this afternoon and early 
evening, generally ranging 8-12 kts with gusts up to 20 possible, 
should see cigs return to low-end VFR. As rain becomes more 
widespread tonight, cigs will droop again back to MVFR as wind 
speeds lower slightly to 5-10 kts.

.MARINE...
Onshore flow returns to the area waters today. Northwesterly winds 
along the outer coastal waters will become breezy this morning and 
linger into Wednesday, with wind gusts approaching 25 kt at times. 
That said, inherited SCA looks good and will remain in place. 
Another frontal system will move into the waters tonight into 
Wednesday, increasing winds and seas across the area. Increasing 
onshore flow will bring a brief push of small craft westerly winds 
to the central and eastern Strait late this afternoon into this 
evening. New SCA will cover this. Southerly winds will also pick up 
along the Puget Sound waters tonight, with latest guidance 
highlighting a 60-80% chance of gusts exceeding small craft 
criteria. Even though the time frame for these gusts seems to be 
pretty limited, will opt to cover with an additional headline here 
as well.

Winds will ease again across the area waters on Wednesday as the 
accompanying surface low drops into the area and tracks southward 
along the coastal waters. This low will allow for flow to turn back 
offshore, with offshore flow persisting the second part of the week. 

Seas 4-6 ft this morning will increase to 6-8 ft by this afternoon 
and will persist at this height into Wednesday. Conditions slowly 
subsiding back to 4-6 ft on Thursday and remain that way into the 
weekend.
