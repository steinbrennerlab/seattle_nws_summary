.SYNOPSIS...
Warm, sunny conditions this afternoon. A frontal system will 
traverse the area tonight into Saturday, bringing chances for 
rain early and gusty winds. Cooler temperatures are in store for
the weekend with localized frost potential. The next system 
looks to arrive Tuesday for the next round of rain chances and 
breezy winds.

.SHORT TERM /TONIGHT THROUGH SATURDAY NIGHT/...
A few patchy low clouds are lingering across portions of the
waters across the area, as well as the immediate coastline and
portions of the south Puget Sound, but these clouds will
continue to diminish this afternoon for mostly sunny skies.
Temperatures will vary depending on the time of clearing, with
temperatures varying from the low 60s along the coast to low 70s
in places with the most clearing. Seattle will likely reach the
upper 60s.

Clouds will increase this evening as a frontal system traverses
the area overnight into early Monday. A fairly-quick hitting
band of rain will traverse the area in associated with the
front. Behind the front, most of the remaining chances for rain
in the mountains and in a convergence zone over the central 
Puget Sound for much of the day on Friday. Rainfall amounts will
be light with most areas seeing less than a quarter to a half 
inch of rain. Some places in the south Sound could remain mostly
dry and shadowed.

In addition to rain, winds will increase early tomorrow morning,
peaking in the late morning/early afternoon timeframe. Winds
will begin as southwesterly before becoming more westerly behind
the frontal passage. Convergent flow is expected through the 
afternoon as winds turn north from the east entrance to the 
Strait south towards Seattle. Peak gusts for the majority of the
area look to remain in the 20-30 mph range, though areas along 
the Strait and near Whidbey Island/Port Townsend, as well as 
Point Roberts have around a 50-70% chance to see gusts greater 
than 45 mph. Winds will slowly ease Friday evening and 
overnight.

Lingering chances for light showers continue into Saturday as a
weak wave moves through the area. Shower chances diminish
Saturday night.

Highs will be much cooler on Friday, in the upper 50s on the
coast with low to mid 60s in the interior. Lows remain in the 
low 50s. Even cooler air will filter in on Saturday, with highs
in the mid 50s to near 60s, with lows in the mid 30s to mid 40s.
Will have to monitor for the amount of clearing and fog
potential to see how low temperatures can get, as the more
sheltered locations are beginning to near frost thresholds,
through the more likely evening will be Sunday night/Monday
morning.

.LONG TERM /SUNDAY THROUGH THURSDAY/...
Ensembles continue to highlight drier but cooler conditions
through the first half of the week. With the cooler
temperatures, the lows on Sunday morning will generally range
anywhere from the upper 30s into the lower 40s. Colder air in 
the Cascade valleys may drop the lows down to around freezing,
with latest probabilities showing a 30% chance of temperatures
32 degrees or less on Sunday morning in those aforementioned
areas. This will be the first in the season for any freeze or 
frost concerns, especially for those who have sensitive 
vegetation. Heading into the first half of the week, the lows 
will get warmer, generally in the 40s, while the afternoon highs
will stay in the upper 50s to low 60s.

Troughing looks to continue over the Pacific Northwest through
the work week, with a chance of a system entering the area 
Tuesday into Wednesday for more unsettled conditions.

.AVIATION...
Northwesterly flow aloft becoming westerly this afternoon as an
upper level trough continues to drop down into the area. Fog has
cleared way this afternoon, with most terminals rebounding to
VFR, the exception being HQM where stratus continues to hug the
coastline and the terminal, seeing LIFR conditions. A frontal
system will move through the area tonight into Friday morning,
lowering ceilings across much of the area to MVFR/IFR. Light
rain will start along the coast 10z-12z, with showers generally
affecting terminals 12z-17z. Will likely see lowered conditions
through the early part of the morning through the interior before
ceilings lift to VFR late afternoon. 

N/NW winds this evening 4 to 8 knots, turning southerly
overnight. Winds will increase Friday morning, 8 to 12 knots,
with periodic gusts up to 20 kts. 

KSEA...VFR conditions this afternoon continuing into the
evening. Light rail will enter the area early Friday morning,
lowering ceilings down to MVFR generally around 09z-15z. NW
winds 4 to 8 knots this evening will turn southerly overnight,
increasing to 8 to 12 knots through the morning with gusts
briefly up to 20 to 25 knots at times. Breezy winds will likely

.MARINE...
A frontal system will traverse the area waters tonight into
early Friday. Ahead of the system, a brief increase in onshore
flow through the central/eastern Strait of Juan de Fuca will
allow for winds to reach Small Craft thresholds. As the system
moves through, winds begin to increase area-wide early Friday
morning. Gales will be most likely through the northern portions
of the coastal waters, as well as through the central/eastern
Strait of Juan de Fuca where gusts could reach up to 40 kt.
Elsewhere through the Strait of Juan de Fuca and coastal waters,
as well as through the Strait of Georgia and Admiralty Inlet, 
Small Craft level winds are more likely. Winds peak Friday
afternoon before slowly easing Friday night. Headlines for the
interior waters should be able to drop by late Friday night, 
but winds look to persist into Saturday morning through the 
coastal waters.

North/northwesterly winds persist into Saturday as a weak low
moves through the area. High pressure will attempt to nose into
the area Monday before another low pressure system looks to
traverse the area on Tuesday for another round of stronger winds
and increased seas.

Seas 6 to 7 ft will increase to around 12-14 ft, potentially 
locally up to 15-16 ft over the northernmost coastal waters 
during the day Friday. Seas remain around 12-14 through early
Saturday morning before easing through the rest of the day. Seas
then become around 5-7 ft Sunday before rising once again as the
next system arrives on Tuesday.
