Models in good agreement with elongated weakening upper level
trough moving through Western Washington Saturday night. Not

.AVIATION...
An upper level trough will remain offshore of Western Washington 
today into Friday, resulting in southwesterly flow aloft today 
turning more southerly tonight. Surface winds largely light and 
variable with speeds 5 kts or less, although some terminals are 
reporting southerlies with slight variations to the southeast or 
southwest with speeds slightly increased, ranging 4 to 8 kts. This 
latter direction and speed will become more widespread by this 
afternoon, although a return to some variability in direction is 
possible by this evening.

The air mass is somewhat unstable which will maintain the chance for 
showers throughout the TAF period. Some isolated MVFR ceilings 
present this morning, but the majority of terminals are 
currently reporting VFR conditions. As the aforementioned 
showers remain possible, occasional dips down into MVFR cannot 
be ruled out, but overall the expectation is for widespread VFR 
conditions to persist throughout the TAF period.

.MARINE...
Low pressure out over the coastal waters will allow for offshore 
flow to persist into this weekend. A weak disturbance passing around 
this low may allow for increased winds for the coastal waters at 
times Friday and Saturday. Models have been shaky on speeds however, 
with some speeds approaching SCA criteria depending on the model 
run. At this time, will pass on any headline issuance but will 
advise future shifts to continue to monitor this time frame.

Seas will continue to ease, currently running 5 to 7 ft, subsiding 
slightly further to 4 to 6 ft by this evening before bottoming out 
at 3 to 5 ft this weekend. Looking ahead, seas will likely build 
early next week back to around 10 ft with the arrival of a long 
period swell into the coastal waters.
