.LONG TERM /FRIDAY NIGHT THROUGH TUESDAY/...
Friday night and Saturday will be cooler and wetter as a cold 
front and trailing trough push into western WA. Showers with a 
convergence zone are likely Saturday afternoon/evening near the 
King/Snohomish line. High will be cooler and in the 50s and 60s 
Saturday afternoon. Expect cooler lows Sunday morning with 30s 
around the south sound. The system slated for Sunday and Monday 
is now shifting south over the offshore waters, missing WA, with
lower probabilities of seeing gusty winds. The air mass will 
still be cool and showery with a trough overhead, though. Expect
a chance of showers moving into Tuesday before the trough 
exits. 33

.MARINE...
A weak front will continue to traverse the area waters this morning. 
High pressure will quickly build in across the offshore waters 
tonight, with thermal troughing developing along the coast. This 
will allow for winds to turn offshore tonight into Thursday. While 
winds are favored to remain below any thresholds for headlines, 
there will be some enhancement to the winds through the gaps, with a 
20-40% chance for gusts to exceed 20 kt near the west entrance to 
the Strait of Juan de Fuca and around Grays Harbor. Winds will ease 
on Friday as the thermal trough gets pushed inland by an incoming 
frontal system. Onshore flow will start to increase as a frontal 
system traverses the area Friday night into Saturday. Another low 
may follow suit Sunday into Monday but there are significant 
differences in the exact low track.

Seas remain around 4 to 7 ft through Friday, increasing through the 
weekend. The most likely forecast is around 8 to 10 ft, though seas 
could reach as high as 13-16 ft Sunday into Monday, depending on the 
track of the low pressure systems as they move across the northeast 
Pacific. GEFS is showing 40-50% chances of seeing greater than 12 ft 
during that time frame.
