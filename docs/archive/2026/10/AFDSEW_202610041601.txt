.SYNOPSIS...
High pressure overhead and a thermal trough shifting inland
today will provide one more day of mild temperatures. A system to 
the north will flatten the ridge Monday, with near-zonal flow aloft 
and onshore flow at the surface maintaining seasonable conditions, 
morning clouds and afternoon sun breaks throughout the week. Chances 
for rain increase by next Friday, though confidence remains low.

&&

.SHORT TERM /TODAY THROUGH TUESDAY/...
Current satellite imagery not too dissimilar from 24 hours ago with 
stratus and fog largely dominating the interior lowlands while 
higher elevations are party to clear skies. There are exceptions to 
this, such as while the river valleys in the SW interior are socked 
in, not so much for the remainder of the area. Also worth noting is 
that while the coast is also partaking of the foggy conditions, the 
northern and southern portions /from UIL north and from HQM south/ 
are seeing generally clear conditions. As such, inherited Dense Fog 
Advisories will remain in place as locations within those headlines 
still seeing visibilities of less than 1/2 mile. The gap between the 
two advisories along the I-5 corridor, including the Seattle and 
Tacoma metro areas, will remain outside of headline consideration at 
this time. While some spots in those areas are meeting criteria, it 
is not widespread enough nor consistent enough to warrant inclusion 
as visibilities in that area fluctuate. For example, at times here 
at the NWS office one could not see the other side of Lake 
Washington, yet 15 minutes later it re-emerged from the fog. Will 
continue to monitor in case a short-fused headline is needed. 
However, with the moisture layer proving to be a little bit thinner 
than yesterday at this time, there is some optimism for fog/stratus 
to burn off by late this morning. That said, the past few days have 
had trouble shrugging off low clouds until the early afternoon 
hours...so cautiously taking an around noon-ish approach to when one 
can expect to see clouds begin to break up. This might have a degree 
or two cooler impact to inherited forecast high temperatures for 
today, but not enough change to warrant a morning forecast update.

Remainder of forecast is on track. Please refer to Previous 
Discussion section for details.

From Previous Discussion...Despite high pressure overhead forcing 
light offshore flow, lower levels have not quite dried out enough to 
prevent another round of low stratus expanding across the lowlands 
this morning. Although slightly drier, low clouds may potentially 
linger into the early afternoon once again today. While temperatures 
today will once again depend on the timing of the eroding stratus, a 
thermal trough shifting inland will aid in warmer temperatures. 
Highs across the lowlands are favored to reach the low to mid 70s, 
with some areas along the Cascade foothills and valleys reaching the 
upper 70s to even lower 80s. 

This brief warm-up will be short-lived, however, as the upper 
level ridge flattens tonight as a weather system moves inland 
over British Columbia. This will bring a return to onshore flow 
as near-zonal flow develops aloft, bringing more seasonable 
temperatures alongside morning low clouds and afternoon sun 
breaks. There will be little change day to day between Monday 
and Tuesday, with dry conditions, highs generally in the mid 
60s to lower 70s, and lows in the upper 40s to lower 50s.

&&

.LONG TERM /TUESDAY NIGHT THROUGH SATURDAY/...
Much of next week will bring more of the same as zonal flow 
aloft and onshore flow at the surface maintain near- normal 
temperatures and morning clouds. There will be little change day
to day through Thursday, with highs once again in the mid 60s 
to lower 70s and lows in the upper 40s to lower 50s.

Forecast models have come into slightly better agreement over
cooler and wetter conditions on Friday as a trough passes over
the Pacific Northwest. However, models continue to hint that the
incoming frontal system and associated precipitation may break
apart as the system moves into western Washington, which would
by no means bring a wash out. Chances for rain increase Friday
afternoon into Saturday, though the intensity and coverage of
the potential incoming rainfall remain uncertain at this time.

.MARINE...
One final round of offshore flow today as a thermal
trough forms along the coast. Winds will turn onshore again Sunday 
night into Monday as low pressure shifts inland and surface high 
pressure builds offshore. Onshore winds will be strongest through 
the Strait of Juan de Fuca as a SCA remains in effect for the 
central/eastern zones. The NBM has a 50-80% chance of SCA gusts 
being met while the REFS is a bit more bullish with 70-90% chance, 
increasing forecaster confidence. Westerly pushes are favored 
throughout the upcoming week of varying strength. A more organized 
system appears favorable towards the week's end.
