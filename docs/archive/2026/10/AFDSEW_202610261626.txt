.SYNOPSIS...A weather system will move onshore today across 
northwest Oregon, resulting in lingering showers for Western 
Washington. Unsettled conditions will continue on Tuesday for 
light precipitation. A frontal system will move into the area 
late Tuesday into Wednesday, bringing lowland rain, mountain 
snow, and breezy conditions. Cooler conditions favored midweek 
into the weekend.

.SHORT TERM /TODAY THROUGH WEDNESDAY/...An upper-level low 
pressure system continues to spin offshore towards northwest 
Oregon early this morning. As the system pushes east today, 
expect lingering shower activity. A weak shortwave will drop 
down early Tuesday keeping conditions unsettled. A frontal 
system associated with a colder air mass will will move south 
from British Columbia into Washington late Tuesday into 
Wednesday. This system will bring lowland rain, mountain snow, 
cooler temperatures, and breezy winds. Snow levels are expected 
to drop Tuesday evening into Wednesday morning to 2500-3500 ft. 
These lower snow levels will introduce snow to the passes. 
Latest NBM guidance has trended slightly higher for snowfall 
amounts. At the moment, guidance suggests the passes may see 
between 4-6 inches and higher mountain peaks may receive 8-13 
inches by Wednesday evening. Additionally, there is a slight 
chance of thunderstorms on Wednesday for the lowlands, with 
probabilities currently between 10-15%. As for winds, latest 
guidance has wind gusts peaking between 20-25 mph. In the wake 
of the cold front passing on Wednesday, high temperatures are 
expected to only peak into the upper 40s to low 50s.

.LONG TERM /WEDNESDAY NIGHT THROUGH SUNDAY/...There is 
generally good agreement between ensembles that the upper level 
low will deepen offshore Thursday through late in the week, 
lowering the chances for precipitation over Western Washington. 
Cooler temperatures are favored to continue into the weekend. 
High temperatures in the upper 40s to low 50s. Low temperatures 
will be in the 30s, with upper 20s possible in the usual colder 
spots. Ensemble members are evenly split on the upper-level 
pattern on Sunday. Some members favor a trough over the Pacific 
Northwest, while others favor a transient upper level ridge.
