Terms in this discussion
Cold front
The leading edge of a colder air mass. Its passage often brings a burst of rain, a shift in wind direction, and gusty wind.
Instability
A condition in which air rises easily and can build towering clouds. It is what allows thunderstorms to form.
REFS
The Rapid Refresh Forecast System, a set of computer model runs used to estimate how likely different outcomes, such as strong wind gusts, are.
High Wind Warning
An alert for sustained or gusting wind strong enough to cause damage, such as downed trees and power outages.
Wind Advisory
An alert for wind strong enough to be a nuisance and possibly cause minor damage, a step below a High Wind Warning.
High Surf Warning
An alert for large, dangerous waves along the coast that can sweep people off beaches and jetties.
Snow level
The elevation above which precipitation falls as snow rather than rain.
QPF
Quantitative precipitation forecast, meaning the predicted amount of rain or melted snow.
Ensemble guidance
A group of computer forecasts run with slightly different starting conditions. When they disagree, confidence in the details is lower.
Base flows
The normal, steady level of water in rivers. Wet soils push these higher, so later rain can lead to flooding more easily.
