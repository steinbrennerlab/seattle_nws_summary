How confident forecasters are

Forecasters are fairly sure about the wind and rain from today through Sunday, since the systems are already arriving. They are less certain about the midweek storm, though models are converging on a track south of the region. The dry, cool outlook into next weekend rests on ensemble forecasts that favor it, and forecasters note the potential for more active weather later.

Terms in this discussion
Upper-level trough
A dip in the high-altitude winds that helps organize storms and showers at the surface. It often brings unsettled weather as it passes.
Storm track
The usual path that Pacific storms follow as they approach the coast. A shift south means most storms pass well away from the region.
Snow level
The lowest elevation where precipitation falls as snow. A lower snow level means snow reaches farther down the mountains.
Ensemble forecasts
A set of model runs with slightly different starting conditions. When most runs agree, forecasters have more confidence in the outcome.

The past month

Autumn's quiet, dry ridge gave way to a stormy Pacific pattern, with strong storms now expected through next weekend.

Covers Sun Oct 11 to Mon Nov 9.

Mid-October began with a ridge of high pressure aloft (a zone of warm, dry air) that forecasters expected to bring a warm, dry stretch, peaking around Friday the 16th. They kept that call steady even as they trimmed the first cooler, wetter system arriving Saturday to mostly light rain. Late October turned cool and mostly dry, with fog and frost and several weak systems that forecasters kept downgrading. They stayed unsure about weekend details and kept the weather quiet. The first fall-like storm arrived on the 24th, and a front on the 27th and 28th brought the season's first widespread Cascade snow and a Winter Weather Advisory. A cold, clear stretch followed, with a freeze and frost around Halloween.

The big shift came in early November. Forecasters first saw only hints of a more active pattern, then grew steadily more confident in a run of Pacific storms. Friday the 6th and Saturday night the 7th were expected to be the strongest so far. Wind Advisories were issued and the forecast grew more worried about damaging gusts on the coast and north of Whidbey Island. Trees still hold many leaves, so forecasters flagged possible power outages. Later forecasts pointed to a persistent stream of moisture (an atmospheric river) and a low near British Columbia keeping the pattern wet into the following week.

Now forecasters expect gusty south winds, rain and high-mountain snow through Wednesday, with a wind advisory for the coast, the northern lowlands and the San Juans. Three stronger storms are then likely from Friday through Sunday, with coastal gusts possibly over 50 mph and very high seas. Heavy rain could push the Skokomish River near minor flood stage. They are fairly confident about this week's wind and rain, but the timing and strength of the late-week storms remain uncertain.
